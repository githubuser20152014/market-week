## The Brief

Jackson Hole positioning is the gravitational pull on every asset class this week, and Friday's Warsh keynote is already bending the yield curve. The standout cross-asset signal: **gold at $4,700** is surging alongside a rising 10-year yield and a firming dollar, a combination that normally kills bullion. Which tells you the bid is structural and geopolitical, not a rates play. Asian equities sold off hard overnight, Nasdaq futures are the weakest of the three major contracts pre-market, and the open shapes up soft with tech leading the drag, right on schedule for a Nvidia print nobody feels ready for.

Yesterday's US session delivered a split verdict, and nobody won convincingly. The Dow added 0.98% to close at **53,277** and the Russell 2000 gained 0.85% to **3,017**, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq limped to a matching 0.43%, closing at **7,674** and **26,180**. Small caps beating mega-cap tech is not a rotation, it is a warning shot. **Gold surged 1.65% to $4,700.60**, the 10-year Treasury yield rose 4.2 basis points to **4.738%**, and the USD Index firmed to 98.98, the exact combination that should have killed the gold rally. It didn't. **WTI crude dropped 2.34% to $85.02**, reversing hard out of last week's $86-plus range, because sanctions on Iran's shadow fleet are apparently bearish for oil now.

The divergence tells the story. The US Treasury unveiled a sweeping "economic D-Day" sanctions package targeting Iranian maritime crude conduits and shadow-fleet intermediaries, which Secretary Bessent called the greatest financial offensive ever deployed against an adversary, modest as ever. That headline pulled crude lower, because tighter enforcement threatens demand channels while physical supply constraints are already priced in via the Cape of Good Hope rerouting now baked into structural freight rates. Meanwhile **Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote** on Friday is squeezing risk appetite out of high-duration tech, pushing capital toward value, small caps, and real assets. Gold is the clearest tell: buyers are piling in despite rising yields and a firming dollar, which means the bid is coming from geopolitical hedging around **Strait of Hormuz** risk and Iran escalation, not a rates rotation.
