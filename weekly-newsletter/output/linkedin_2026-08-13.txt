Yesterday's S&P rally on cooler CPI looked like consensus. It wasn't. Four different asset classes reacted to the same data point in four different ways, and only one of those reactions is telling the truth. Here's why you shouldn't trust the equity read alone.

THE DISSENTING MARKETS

Bonds: the 10-year yield barely moved on a print that was supposed to be dovish. If the market genuinely believed rate cuts were coming, yields would have dropped. They didn't. Three hawkish dissents at the last FOMC meeting are still hanging over this data, and bond traders aren't ready to price in a clean path lower.

Gold: it quietly pushed higher on a softening dollar, and not because anyone's celebrating a soft landing. A Hormuz standoff still has shipping traffic near three-month lows, and gold is the asset class that gets bid up when investors want insurance, not upside.

Crude: oil fell hard, but not because geopolitical risk eased. A massive weekly inventory build simply overwhelmed the Strait of Hormuz risk premium. Supply data beat headline risk this time. That's a different story than "markets stopped worrying about the Middle East."

THE CORPORATE PROOF POINT

The clearest signal came from earnings, not macro data. Two AI infrastructure names posted 19% single-day gains on strong results. A separate AI name that went public just weeks ago fell 14% in the same 24 hours. The AI trade isn't one trade anymore. Capital is getting selective about which AI story it believes, and that discipline is new.

THE SECOND-ORDER STORY

The angle nobody's covering: attacks on Russian grain export terminals in the Black Sea are back in the headlines, alongside shipping companies warning that port and trucking bottlenecks could push prices higher. Layer a food supply shock on top of an energy corridor that's still disrupted, and the inflation story the Fed just called "cooling" has two fresh wildcards nobody's pricing in yet.

THE VERDICT

So which market got it right yesterday? Equities, who took the CPI print as a green light? Or bonds and gold, who treated it as noise? My read: when three different asset classes disagree with the fourth, bet on the three. The bond market and the gold market are rarely both wrong at the same time.
