The Nasdaq lost 2.1% on Friday. Futures are up 1.2% this morning.

Nothing changed over the weekend. Nobody paid down a dollar of AI infrastructure debt. Before you trust the bounce, ask who is not buying it.

Three markets are not.

Bonds. The 10-year Treasury sits at 5.10%. That is the risk-free rate. Every company borrowing against future AI revenue now has to beat it, and the ones carrying junk ratings are finding out how hard that is. A rebound in equities does not lower a yield. The bond market did not get the memo that stocks are fine.

Gold. $4,189, flat through the selloff and flat through the bounce. When stocks rip and gold gives nothing back, someone large is treating every dip as a hedge, not a trade. Gold is not celebrating. It is not leaving either.

Crude. WTI fell 3.4% to $98.01 after the White House issued a 60-day waiver letting Russian diesel back into the market. Sanctions with a snooze button. Oil took the hint. Oil is still $98.

The corporate proof point: CoreWeave's credit default swap spreads blew out last week. A company burning debt at 5%+ risk-free rates has a math problem, and strong mega-cap earnings do not solve it. Netflix, Tesla and Meta report this week into a very high bar.

The second-order story nobody is covering: the diesel waiver. Diesel is freight. Freight is groceries, construction, and everything else that moves on a truck. A 60-day waiver is a pressure valve ahead of winter, and the valve closes right when heating demand peaks. The question is what happens on day 61.

Europe is not helping. French civil unrest has the CAC 40 sliding and the DAX down 1.1% in early trade. America is selling off on credit. Europe is selling off on politics.

So which market got it right? Equities say the worst is over. Bonds, gold and crude say the worst was priced somewhere else. Three against one is not a coin flip. I know which side I would rather explain to a client.
