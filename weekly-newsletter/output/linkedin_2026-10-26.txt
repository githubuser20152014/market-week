Trump said "there will not be a strike today" on Iran. Oil fell 2.13% to $101.07. Futures are green.

Here is why you shouldn't trust the relief rally.

Friday's close was red. The S&P 500 lost 0.52%. The Dow lost 1.23%, which is a lot for the index that is supposed to be boring. Nobody sent a warning.

Three markets are not celebrating.

Bonds: the 10-year yield is 4.99%, up 3.5 bps on Friday. That is one tick from the 5% line that already broke equities once. Stocks are pricing a good mood. Treasuries are pricing a bill.

Gold: it closed at $4,032 and held $4,000 again after Friday's margin-call flush. Sovereign and physical buyers are treating that level as a floor. This is not a bond proxy. It is a hedge for geopolitics and fiscal chaos, and the market keeps supplying both.

Crude: still above $100 even after the drop. A "no strike today" headline does not erase last week's Houthi premium, when Brent hit $116.72 after drone strikes near Saudi Arabia's Shaybah oil field and Dammam airport.

The corporate proof point: Lockheed Martin jumped 5% on a record $292 billion backlog. Meta fell nearly 7% and Lam Research fell 9% on the AI capex backlash. Same day, same tape. Money walked out of high-multiple tech and into companies with actual backlog. The Dow stayed flat while the Nasdaq dropped 2.6% Thursday. That is where institutional money is going.

The second-order story nobody is covering: Europe is leading the open. DAX up 0.89%, CAC up 0.79%, FTSE up 0.44%, all on the Moody's France reprieve pulling OAT yields below 5%. Asia split on tech anxiety. Nikkei fell 0.80% on semiconductor selling. KOSPI dropped 1.46%. Hang Seng gained 0.51%. The world is not buying one story, and the week still has Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon earnings to go.

So which market got it right? Equities say calm. Bonds, gold, and crude say the Fed meeting is Wednesday and nobody has seen the dots. One of them is wrong, and bonds have a long memory.
