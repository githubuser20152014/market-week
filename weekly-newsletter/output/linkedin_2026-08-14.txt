The S&P 500 closed above 7,800 for the first time in history yesterday.

Here's why you shouldn't trust it: gold gained more.

Cooling inflation was supposed to be a clean win for risk assets. July CPI came in soft, PPI landed flat, and the Fed is now all but locked into holding rates at 3.50-3.75% through September. Every high-multiple growth name from Nvidia to CoreWeave re-rated on cue. The equity market threw itself a party. Three other markets sent regrets.

Bonds didn't buy it. The 10-year dipped a few basis points on the disinflation print, sure, but the 30-year Treasury touched its highest yield since 2001 on weak auction demand, at the exact same moment the front end was rallying. Somebody on the long end is worried about who's financing all this, and it isn't the crowd chasing Nasdaq breakouts.

Gold didn't buy it either. It gained 1.13% into a dollar selloff, outrunning every major US equity index. That's not a disinflation trade anymore. An indefinite US naval blockade of Iranian ports is putting a geopolitical floor under safe-haven demand, and gold is pricing that risk independently of whatever equities decide to do next.

Crude didn't buy it. Oil held firm despite a 17.4-million-barrel inventory build that, on paper, should have driven prices sharply lower. It didn't, because a naval blockade diverting dozens of vessels around the Cape of Good Hope doesn't care what the storage tanks say.

The corporate proof point that this isn't just multiples expansion: Uber just partnered with China's Pony.ai to deploy 2,000 robotaxis across Europe. That's real capital committing to real infrastructure, not another AI ticker re-rating on sentiment.

And the story nobody's covering: a niche copper trade has quietly become one of the market's most reliable real-time gauges for the next tariff move out of Washington, arguably faster and cleaner than anything equities are pricing right now. While everyone stares at the S&P's new high, the commodities desk is watching copper spreads for the actual policy signal.

So which market has it right: equities celebrating a soft landing, or bonds, gold, and oil all pricing risk that never actually left the building?

My money's on the dissenters. A breakout built entirely on rate-hold optimism, with the long end of the curve flashing fiscal stress and a geopolitical chokepoint still shut, is a fragile breakout. Not a new bull leg.
