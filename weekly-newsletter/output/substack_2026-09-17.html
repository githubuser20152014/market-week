First Hike in Three Years. Nasdaq Futures Up 2.06% Regardless.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh hiked rates for the first time in three years, and equities responded by... rallying. Futures are ripping 1.5-2% higher this morning while bonds sit this one out entirely: the 10-year is glued near 5% and T-note futures barely budged.

Gold and oil both sold off into the close and are flat pre-market, which means the inflation-hedge trade got benched the same week it was supposed to be right. Somebody in this market is wrong, and it is probably not the bond desk.

The S&P 500 closed at 7,551, down 0.45%, the Dow shed 1.21%, and the Russell 2000 dropped 0.40%, all curtsying to Warsh's 25-basis-point hike to 3.75%-4.00%, about as welcome as it sounds. The market threw a party the day the bill came due.

Here's my read:

The 10-year Treasury settled at 5.006%, a level the market hasn't seen since 2007, back when "subprime" was still just a word and not a warning label. Gold fell nearly 1% to $4,345 and WTI dropped 1.08% to $101.32, because Saudi Aramco quietly rerouted crude around the Strait of Hormuz mess via Oman's Sohar port, and the market's geopolitical risk premium evaporated on the spot. Warsh's press conference was the real story: he told markets, in so many words, that services inflation and $40 trillion in sovereign debt supply mean rates stay restrictive until the data forces his hand, like a landlord raising rent the same week the tenant thought the lease was up. Pre-market futures are pricing a gap-up open anyway, S&P futures at 7,682, up 1.67%, Nasdaq futures up 2.06%, which is either short-covering relief or the market front-running a summit that hasn't happened yet. Europe is dead flat, offering exactly zero confirmation either way. Watch the 10-year: if yields pop back above 5.05% while futures stay bid, that is not bullish divergence, that is the market lying to itself.

The One Trade: Short $QQQ

Nasdaq futures are up 2% into a confirmed 5% rate environment with Warsh signaling no pivot, making this gap a short-squeeze to sell, not a breakout to chase.

Confirms: QQQ fails to hold above Wednesday's close of 25,978 within the first 30 minutes of trading, or the 10-year yield ticks back above 5.02% as equities stay bid.

Kill switch: BOJ hike triggers yen strength and broad carry unwind that paradoxically sends capital into U.S. tech as a perceived quality safe haven, pushing QQQ above 27,000 on volume. Get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Fade the gap-open in high-multiple tech ($QQQ) unless the 10-year drops below 4.95% and holds. A 2% futures surge into a 5% rate environment is a short-squeeze, not a trend change.
  • Add to short-duration Treasuries ($BIL, $SHV) on any intraday dip. Warsh left the door open for another hike before year-end, and a 4%+ risk-free yield is the benchmark every equity now has to clear.
  • Hold energy ($XLE) with a tight leash. The Saudi bypass is working for now, but don't you dare treat that geopolitical premium as gone for good.
  • Position in semis and electronics ($SOXX, $SMH) ahead of the September 24 Trump-Xi summit. $30 billion in tariff relief is a real margin tailwind, size small until there's an actual communique.
  • The trade nobody is talking about: dividend aristocrats ($NOBL) and utilities ($XLU) underperformed the whole growth-euphoria phase and are now the closest equity substitute for a 5% bond yield. Stay away from anything that still trades like it's 2023.
