The S&P dropped 0.58% Tuesday. That headline undersells what actually happened. Oil broke $100. Gold hit a record. The 10-year yield rose. Equities were the least interesting asset in the room.

Here's why you shouldn't trust the -0.58% number as the story:

Bonds: Treasury yields ticked up to 4.806% even as stocks sold off. In a normal risk-off day, bonds catch a bid as investors flee to safety. They didn't. The market is pricing an inflation shock, not a growth scare, and the Treasury is now running a debt buyback operation just to keep the 10-year from breaking higher.

Gold: Gold surged to $4,446, up nearly 1.2%, on the same day the dollar softened and yields rose. That combination almost never happens together, unless the market has decided this is a pure geopolitical fear trade. Gold isn't hedging inflation this week. It's hedging the Strait of Hormuz.

Crude: WTI jumped 2% to $94.89 and Brent cleared $100 after Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure and a declared Iranian exclusion zone across the Strait of Hormuz, the corridor that carries roughly a fifth of the world's daily oil supply. This isn't a headline risk premium. It's a physical supply disruption with a price tag attached.

Away from the majors, demand for riskier mortgages is rising right alongside interest rates, a sign that credit markets are already adjusting to a higher-for-longer regime before the Fed has said a word.

The angle nobody's pricing yet: defense. A new US missile factory just opened, backed by high-profile tech investors, exiting stealth the same week NATO convened an emergency hybrid warfare task force. Procurement cycles don't turn on a single headline, but this one keeps adding data points.

So which market got it right Tuesday: equities down half a percent, or bonds, gold, and oil all screaming inflation shock at the same time? The answer isn't close. Stocks are still catching up to what the rest of the tape already knows.
