Mines Hit a Tanker. Oil Hit $86.52. The Fed Hit “No Cuts.”

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The Strait of Hormuz reopened for business Monday, and business was mines. U.S. strikes on Larak Island sent WTI up 3.7% to $86.52, and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh used his Jackson Hole debut to confirm that rate cuts are not coming, pinning the 10-year at 4.72%.

Small-caps got the memo first. The Russell 2000 dropped 1.39% while the S&P shrugged off just 0.25%, because credit stress always shows up in the small names before anyone admits it's happening broadly.

Futures are flat this morning. That's not calm, that's the market waiting to see if the Gulf stays quiet.

Here's my read:

The catalyst was a one-two punch: the Strait of Hormuz and Jackson Hole, in that order of drama. U.S. Central Command struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island after Iran seeded naval mines in the transit corridor, and a commercial tanker took two mine hits for its trouble. Roughly 20% of global oil transit runs through that stretch of water, so crude's biggest single-day move in months is not a coincidence, it's geography. Meanwhile Warsh's keynote confirmed rate cuts are dead for now, with swap markets pricing a 57% chance of a September hike. Oil up plus a hawkish Fed is a compression trade with a bow on it: oil feeds inflation, inflation delays cuts, and rate hikes shrink the value of every future earnings dollar. Small-caps broke hardest because they run on floating-rate debt and thin margins, exactly the combination the market punishes first.

The One Trade: LONG $XLE

A U.S. military strike on Larak Island and an Iranian mine that damaged a commercial tanker have put 20% of global oil transit at active risk, and UN corridor negotiations are 48 to 72 hours from conclusion at best.

Confirms: WTI crude holds above $86 through 10 AM ET and XLE opens higher and holds those gains into mid-morning, confirming energy buyers are not fading the geopolitical premium.

Kill switch: A formal UN Security Council ceasefire announcement or confirmed Hormuz convoy corridor agreement before 11 AM ET collapses the geopolitical oil premium and invalidates the trade, so get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Long $GLD: gold is holding above $4,500 with the dollar soft and equities weak at the same time, a configuration that historically signals durable safe-haven buying, not a bounce. Don't you dare sell it here.
  • Short or underweight $TLT: with 57% odds on a September hike and Warsh refusing to anchor forward guidance, long-duration Treasuries face selling pressure from both rising short rates and fiscal supply. The 10-year at 4.72% is not the ceiling.
  • Underweight $IWM: small-cap credit stress, not just broad risk-off. Stay away from adding until the hike picture clarifies post-NFP Friday.
  • The trade nobody is talking about: watch $IAU or $GLD relative to $UUP. The dollar is softening even as the Fed leans hawkish, partly because the SCO summit in Bishkek is accelerating non-dollar trade settlement frameworks. If that persists, it amplifies gold and commodity ETF returns beyond the geopolitical bid alone.
