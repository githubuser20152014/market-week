The FTSE Rallied 1.23% on Peace. The Russell Fell 0.45% on Everything Else.

Framework Foundry Weekly · Global Investor Edition

Trump suspended the bombing campaign against Iran on July 27. Brent crashed more than 7% to $85/bbl in a single session. Cairo piled on days later with a Hamas disarmament framework. Traders treated two ceasefires like two rate cuts.

The economy did not get the memo. Q2 GDP printed 1.5% against a 1.8% consensus, core PCE held at 3.7%, and the Fed's own vote split 9-3 with three members demanding a hike anyway. Chair Warsh abandoned forward guidance entirely, which is Fed-speak for "we don't know either."

The FTSE rallied 1.23% on the ceasefire. The Russell 2000 fell 0.45% on the actual macro data. Only one of those two markets is telling you the truth about next quarter.

Here's my read:

This is a stagflation setup wearing a ceasefire bow. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 9 basis points to 4.74% and the 30-year hit a multi-year high of 5.28%, because bond traders are pricing a policy mistake, not a soft landing. Slowing growth, sticky inflation, a hawkish Fed minority, and Section 301 tariffs now blanketing 99.4% of U.S. merchandise imports don't add up to a bull case. The sugar rush has a shelf life measured in days, not quarters.

Equities split hard on geography. The FTSE 100 gained 1.23% and the Hang Seng surged 3.56%, both cashing in on relief, while the Nikkei fell 1.23% after an 8% top-to-bottom swing and the KOSPI dropped a full 11% mid-week as traders decided Samsung and SK Hynix's AI capex story had gotten ahead of itself. Back home, the Dow and Nasdaq managed polite gains near 0.6%, but the Russell 2000 fell 0.45%, small caps still footing the bill for tariffs and borrowing costs the mega-caps can shrug off.

The dollar broke the usual playbook doing it: the dollar index fell 1.5% to 99.80 while yields rose, a combination that isn't supposed to happen at the same time. Gold slipped 1.0% to $4,049, but it only gave back $69 off its highs despite a full-blown ceasefire rally, which says the central-bank bid underneath it hasn't gone anywhere.

The One Trade: Long $EWU

The FTSE 100 led every developed market index this week with a 1.23% gain, and the setup argues that move has legs into next month. UK large caps have three concurrent tailwinds: the ceasefire directly benefits the FTSE's heavy weighting in energy, mining, and industrial names that had been suppressed by Hormuz risk for two straight weeks; the dollar's 1.5% weekly decline is a direct mechanical boost for unhedged sterling returns with GBP/USD already at 1.346; and UK large caps have almost no direct exposure to the USTR's Section 301 tariff structure. The FTSE closed near 10,868, well above its intraweek low of 10,736.

Kill switch: U.S.-Iran back-channel talks collapse or Hormuz maritime disruptions resume, reversing the energy risk premium and hammering FTSE energy constituents fast. Until that headline exists, the rally is the base case.

What else I'm watching

  • FEZ caught the same ceasefire bid as the FTSE and remains supported by ECB policy stability. The Kazimir hike signal is a real risk, but energy relief outweighs it near-term. Don't you dare sell European exposure into this rotation.
  • TLT is the trade nobody wants yet. A 30-year at 5.28% and a 10-year at 4.74% are entering territory where long-duration bonds offer asymmetric upside if July payrolls disappoint and September cut odds revive. Size small, the 9-3 Fed vote means the path is volatile.
  • GLD at $4,049 on a full ceasefire rally is a dip worth watching, not a trend break. Stagflation, dollar weakness, and ongoing central bank demand all argue for a resumption of the uptrend.
  • IWM stays away from. Small caps fell 0.45% in a week large caps rallied, and sticky inflation plus 5.28% long-end yields plus broad tariffs is a structural headwind through Q3.
  • Samsung and SK Hynix's 11% KOSPI collapse is the trade nobody's talking about. If the AI capex skepticism that hit Korean chipmakers mid-week spreads to U.S. semiconductor names, that's the next leg of this story, not the ceasefire.
