Diesel Hit a Record $6. Gold Hit $4,380. Neither Number Is a Coincidence.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Two records got set yesterday, and the bond market noticed both. Diesel crossed $6 a gallon for the first time ever. Gold sat at $4,380 like it already knew something the rest of the tape didn't.

The 10-year Treasury yield surged 10.7 basis points to 4.944%, its highest print of the year and six basis points from a number nobody wants to say out loud. The S&P 500 fell 0.58% to 7,591, the Nasdaq dropped 0.65% to 26,081, and the Russell 2000 got hit hardest at -1.04%, because small-caps have no oil hedge and zero patience for basis points. WTI actually fell 3.16% to $99.24 and diesel still set a record, which tells you this stopped being a crude story a while ago. It's a choke-point story.

The bond market already knows what CPI is going to say. Everyone else finds out at 8:30.

Here's my read:

The driver is the Strait of Hormuz, still. Iranian forces are enforcing maritime exclusion zones, Houthi forces seized a key Yemeni port, and Treasury Secretary Bessent is lining up sanctions on a major bank Monday as part of the Iran strategy. Thursday's PPI print came in at 5.4% annual, wholesale inflation running hot enough that markets treated it as a preview, not an outlier. This is a supply shock wearing a geopolitical fuse, and the Fed doesn't have a rate that fixes a shipping lane. Powell can hike all he wants. He can't reopen the Strait.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

Gold held $4,380 while the 10-year yield surged 10.7 basis points and the dollar sat flat at 99.17, a divergence that only holds up if buyers believe the safe-haven trade beats the rate trade today.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,350 through the 8:30 AM CPI release and doesn't sell off with equities if the print runs hot. A hold or uptick above $4,380 by 10:00 AM says institutional buyers are in control.

Kill switch: core CPI prints below 0.2% monthly, equities rip on the relief, and gold breaks below $4,320. If that happens, the safe-haven divergence was noise, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Stay away from TLT and anything long-duration. The 10-year at 4.944% has no ceiling in sight until CPI proves otherwise, and duration is the last place you want exposure into a print like this.
  • Hold XLE, don't you dare trim it. $99 WTI plus a record diesel print means upstream producers are still cash machines, Hormuz risk or not.
  • Trim IWM. The Russell led yesterday's selloff at -1.04%, and a 30-year mortgage rate above 7.45% is not a friend to leveraged small-cap balance sheets.
  • The trade nobody is talking about: ITA. NATO 3.0 acceleration plus the Iran bank sanctions is a structural defense-spending story that a single CPI print won't derail.
