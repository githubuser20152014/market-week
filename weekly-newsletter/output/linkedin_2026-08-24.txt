The Dow rallied 0.98% yesterday. Here's why you shouldn't trust it.

Three other markets spent the same session disagreeing, loudly.

BONDS: The 10-year yield rose 4.2 basis points to 4.738%, but that move has less to do with yesterday's data than with what's coming Friday. Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote is the real event, and the bond market is holding its breath, not confirming the equity rally.

GOLD: Gold surged 1.65% to $4,700.60 into rising yields and a firming dollar, the exact combination that's supposed to kill a gold rally. When gold ignores that setup, it's telling you the bid isn't about rates at all. It's geopolitical, priced around Strait of Hormuz risk and Iran escalation.

CRUDE: WTI dropped 2.34% to $85.02 the same day the US Treasury unveiled a sweeping new sanctions package targeting Iran's maritime shadow fleet. Normally, sanctions on a major producer's export network tighten supply and lift prices. Instead, oil sold off, because tighter enforcement threatens demand channels and the physical supply risk is already priced in through the Cape of Good Hope reroute. The oil market did its worrying weeks ago.

CORPORATE PROOF POINT: Reports that Nvidia is warning customers about AI-related price hikes ahead of Wednesday's earnings aren't a company flexing pricing power. That's expectation management before a print the market is nervous about, and the KOSPI's 3.12% overnight drop, the sharpest loss in Asia, suggests semiconductor investors got the memo first.

THE SECOND-ORDER STORY: The angle nobody's covering is shipping. The Cape of Good Hope reroute tied to Red Sea and Hormuz risk is no longer a temporary detour, it's getting baked into structural freight rates. That's a cost that shows up months from now in everything from electronics to industrial inputs, long after this week's headlines have scrolled past.

THE VERDICT: So which market got yesterday right? Equities, celebrating a soft rally? Or gold and crude, pricing in a geopolitical shock that hasn't finished playing out? One of them is wrong, and it's probably not the one with a Treasury sanctions briefing landing this afternoon.
