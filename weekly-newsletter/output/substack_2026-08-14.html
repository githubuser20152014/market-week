The S&P Broke 7,800 for the First Time Ever. Gold Broke Its Own Record Anyway.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The S&P 500 closed above 7,800 for the first time in history yesterday. You'd think that's the headline. It's not. Gold gained more, up 1.13% to $4,413, while the dollar slid to 99.66 and the 10-year yield dropped 4.1 basis points. That is not what a soft landing looks like. That is what a market hedges its own party looks like.

Cooling inflation was supposed to be the whole story. July CPI came in soft at 3.4% annual, PPI landed flat at 0.0%, and the Fed is now locked into holding at the September FOMC meeting. Every high-multiple growth name from Nvidia to CoreWeave re-rated on cue like clockwork. The Nasdaq ran 0.81%. The Dow limped to +0.13%, because apparently industrials didn't get the invite.

Oil held $81.70 despite a 17.4-million-barrel inventory build that should have sunk it. The Strait of Hormuz blockade doesn't do glut discounts.

Nobody trusts the party they're at, and the data is starting to agree with them.

Here's my read:

Twin inflation prints did the heavy lifting this week: July CPI at 3.4% annual, 0.1% monthly, PPI flat at 0.0%, both below consensus. That combination locked in a Fed hold at 3.50-3.75% and sent every rate-sensitive growth trade higher. Nothing surprising there. What's surprising is that the 30-year Treasury touched its highest yield since 2001 on soft auction demand, at the exact same moment the front end rallied on disinflation. Somebody on the long end is nervous about who's footing the bill, and it isn't the same crowd buying Nasdaq calls. Meanwhile gold ripping 1.13% into a dollar selloff isn't a disinflation trade anymore, it's a geopolitical floor. The Strait of Hormuz blockade, now diverting 59 vessels around the Cape of Good Hope, is doing the work that used to belong to rate-cut hopes. The bond market and the gold market are telling two different stories, and the equity market is pretending it only heard one of them.

The One Trade: Long $GLD

Gold outran every US equity index by 50+ basis points yesterday while the dollar fell and bonds rallied, a three-way divergence that, combined with an indefinite US naval blockade of Iranian ports, gives buyers two independent reasons to own it and no obvious catalyst to sell.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,390 spot equivalent through the 10 AM New Home Sales print. Any equity softness that doesn't drag gold lower confirms the geopolitical bid is running independent of the risk-on/risk-off toggle.

Kill switch: A de-escalation headline out of the Strait of Hormuz, specifically a US-Iran agreement to resume commercial transit, collapses the geopolitical floor and invalidates the trade fast.

What else I'm watching

  • QQQ / SMH: Hold tech and semiconductor exposure into the open, the trade stays alive as long as 10-year yields stay under 4.70%. Don't you dare sell the AI trade over one soft print, but watch that yield level like it owes you money.
  • XLE: The asymmetric call nobody is talking about. Oil at $81.70 should be lower given the inventory glut, but the Hormuz blockade is holding a floor under crude anyway. If it holds above $80 on weakness, the geopolitical premium is real and there's room to run.
  • TLT: Stay away from long-duration Treasuries. Short-end yields fell, but the 30-year auction weakness and fiscal deficit fears mean TLT buyers are fighting the current. Wrong side of the trade until refunding demand improves.
  • IYR / XLRE: New Home Sales at 10 AM ET is the real-time test. Housing is the most rate-sensitive sector in the economy, so trade the data, not the pre-market drift.
