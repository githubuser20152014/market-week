Payrolls Shrank 23,000 in July. Gold Popped 2% Like It Knew Something.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Three Fed presidents mutinied on interest rates last week. Wall Street's reaction was to buy stocks anyway.

Gold ripped 2.05% to $4,473. The 10-year yield barely moved off 4.684%. Neither of those is a coincidence.

Today's CPI print decides who was right, and somebody is about to be very wrong.

Here's my read:

Equities closed lower Monday because holding risk into a Fed civil war felt like a bad trade. The S&P 500 dropped 0.32% to 7,728, the Nasdaq fell 0.60% to 26,445, and the Dow shed 0.34% to 53,792, a tantrum, not a rout. Payrolls contracted by 23,000 in July, torching the soft-landing story, while the FOMC minutes revealed the first three-way dissent among regional Fed presidents in over a decade, all of them wanting a hike. Add the Strait of Hormuz still choking off a fifth of the world's oil supply, with the IEA warning on demand destruction and the EIA hiking its 2026 price baseline, and you get a market hedging with both hands. Gold's 2% pop is the tell: nobody trusts either side of the Fed's mandate right now.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

Gold at $4,473 is bid into a CPI print with a three-way FOMC dissent in the background and Strait of Hormuz risk still unresolved. Buyers win on a hot print and only partially give it back on a cool one.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,440 through the 9:30 AM open regardless of the CPI outcome.
Kill switch: CPI prints 3.2% or below headline with core at 0.1%, and this stagflation bid unwinds fast, GLD below $4,400 intraday.
