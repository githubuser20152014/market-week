Brent Hit $100. Gold Hit $4,216. The G7 Hit Snooze.

Global Investor Edition · Week ending October 9, 2026

September payrolls printed +29,000 against a consensus of 85,000-90,000. Stocks cheered. Obviously. Bad jobs news means the Fed pauses, and a paused Fed is the only thing this market has loved all year.

Then the FOMC minutes crashed the party. All 19 policymakers backed the last 25-basis-point hike. A vocal minority wants a long pause. Rate futures now price 38% odds of an October hike.

A relief rally with a 38% asterisk. Enjoy it while the asterisk is small.

Here's my read:

The macro driver is a Fed that bought itself a pause and nobody else. Brent punched through $100 a barrel on Middle East infrastructure strikes and Gulf hurricane threats. The G7 released 100 million barrels from strategic reserves. Oil shrugged. The ISM Services Prices Index hit 74.0, the hottest since July 2022, and Eurozone inflation printed 3.8% on an 18.8% energy surge. That is a stagflation trap with a European accent. The Fed got a pause. The ECB got a headache.

The bond market delivered the plot twist. A $39 billion 10-year auction cleared at 2.52x bid-to-cover, and the $22 billion 30-year drew 2.54x at a 5.618% high yield. Buyers showed up right at 5.5%, coupons in hand. The selloff has a ceiling. Cash earning real returns is now a genuine rival to your equity allocation, and it is not leaving.

International equities. Europe was the week's casualty. The Euro Stoxx 50 fell 1.29% on a Sentix Eurozone reading of -13.8 and a French budget fight that pushed 10-year yields toward 4.95%. The DAX lost 0.65% even after a Friday bounce, and Germany's +2.0% August industrial production barely registered. The FTSE 100 gained 0.86%, because London finally got to be the safe one. Energy and commodities have their uses. In Asia-Pacific, the Hang Seng rose 1.03% and the Nikkei 225 slipped 0.12% to 69,031 after an AI wobble in the morning. China's CSI 300 reopened down 1.09% after Golden Week, with tourist spending 9% below 2019. Broad emerging markets lagged. Stimulus hopes, meet household budgets.

Currencies, commodities and metals. The USD Index rose 0.28% to 102.21. EUR/USD fell 0.43% to 1.1206, GBP/USD barely moved at 1.3233, and the franc held near 1.2048. The yen slid another 0.33% even with a Bank of Japan hike pencilled in for December. Carry trades unwind slowly. In commodities, WTI finished at $91.85, flat for the week, after a range of $86.86 to $93.20. Natural gas jumped 6.62% to $3.22 on hurricane evacuations. Gold closed at $4,216.30, up 1.12%, within 0.4% of its record. Silver lagged, down 1.38% to $60.67. Gold is no longer just a rate trade. It is a hedge against everyone's fiscal homework.

Meanwhile US large caps kept the party going. The S&P 500 rose 1.04% to 7,811.54 and the Nasdaq set a record close of 27,599.79 on Tuesday. Then it lost 1.3% Thursday on OpenAI revenue doubts and clawed back Friday. The Russell 2000 fell 0.96%. Small caps are canaries of domestic credit, and right now they are canaries with a cough.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

The signal: gold closed at $4,216.30, within 0.4% of its record, after a 10-year yield spike to 5.365%. In past rate scares, gold sold off as the dollar rose. This week it didn't. That is the whole trade.

The causal stack: French yields near 4.95% with an 80-basis-point Bund spread. An IMF outlook warning about uncoordinated debt-financed fiscal policy. A Fed whose own minutes show a fractured committee. Hormuz tanker harassment keeping the geopolitical premium on. The dollar is only modestly firmer at 102.21, and strong auction demand is keeping real yields contained. The headwinds are manageable. The tailwinds are structural.

Confirms: gold spot holds above $4,150 on a closing basis as the French budget crisis escalates through committee votes early next week.

Kill switch: a clean October FOMC hike with hawkish guidance pushes the 10-year above 5.50% and the dollar above DXY 104. That is where gold's nominal price gets squeezed even when the real risks stay. If it happens, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Gold: don't you dare sell it into this. It just absorbed a 5.365% yield spike without blinking.
  • SPY over IWM: this is a mega-cap rally, not a broad one. Stay away from small caps until rates stop flinching.
  • FEZ and EWQ: stay away. French yields, 3.8% Eurozone inflation and a -13.8 Sentix reading make this a value trap with a nice logo.
  • EWH over broad EM: the Hang Seng gained 1.03% while the mainland stumbled. Hong Kong carries less direct exposure to CSI 300 policy risk.
  • UNG: the trade nobody is talking about. Natural gas jumped 6.62% on hurricane evacuations, and a small position hedges the energy leg of the stagflation story.
