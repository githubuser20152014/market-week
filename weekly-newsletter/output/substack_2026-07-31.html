Amazon Popped 13%. Apple Dropped 7%. Bonds Believed Neither.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Amazon jumped 13% yesterday on an AWS beat, and the market got busy believing the AI monetization story again. Apple fell 7.2% on the same tape, killed by China Services weakness, and nobody blinked.

Here's the accusation: the Nasdaq only remembers the winner. Bonds did not. The Fed held rates, Chair Warsh killed forward guidance, and the 10-year still climbed to 4.663% while stocks partied like it was risk-on.

Futures point to a gap-up open this morning. But KOSPI ripped 17.9% overnight and the yen just got stronger, not weaker, and neither of those numbers screams "buy the gap." Read the whole tape before you chase anything.

Here's my read:

The catalyst split was clean, and the market chose to ignore half of it. Amazon's AWS cloud numbers were a genuine beat, up 13%, confirming that AI capex stories without free cash flow are out and AI monetization proof is in. Apple fell 7.2% on China Services weakness, a structural problem wearing a one-quarter miss as a costume. Meanwhile the Aschenbrenner-linked AI fund Situational Awareness got forced into liquidating its concentrated AI infrastructure book to Citadel, which is what froth clearing actually looks like.

The bigger tell is the bond market. The Fed held at 3.50%-3.75% with three hawk dissenters, and Warsh dropped formal forward guidance entirely. The 10-year yield rose to 4.663% anyway, extending its multi-year high streak. Bonds never bought the equity rally. Yields climbing alongside stocks is a compression of the risk-free rate, and it always ends up biting multiples.

The One Trade: LONG $XLE

Iran's strikes on U.S. assets in Kuwait and Bahrain are keeping a geopolitical premium baked into crude at $84.72, and Exxon and Chevron earnings just confirmed the energy sector is printing cash at these prices. The Cairo disarmament deal is a ceiling risk, not a floor collapse.

Confirms: WTI holds above $83.50 through the 10 AM ET New Home Sales print and XLE trades above yesterday's close on above-average volume in the first 30 minutes.

Kill switch: WTI crude breaks below $82 on any concrete progress signal from the Cairo disarmament working group meeting over the weekend. If that happens, the geopolitical premium that is this trade's entire thesis is gone, and you get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • QQQ, selectively: Amazon's beat and the pivot to FCF-generative AI names is tradeable for at least a session, but trim fast if Apple's drag widens and the equal-weight index starts showing breadth failure underneath the mega-cap move.
  • TLT and long-duration bonds: stay away. Warsh killed forward guidance, three FOMC members wanted a hike, and the 10-year has room to run toward 4.70%-4.80%. If you need fixed income at all, rotate into FLOT or USFR instead.
  • XLE and XOP: don't you dare sell it yet, but keep a tight ceiling. The Cairo framework is a real release valve on the geopolitical premium the whole trade depends on.

  • KOSPI-linked exposure (EWY) is the trade nobody should touch this morning. A 17.9% single-session move is either a data anomaly or a halt resolving. Confirm the print is real before going anywhere near Korean equities.
