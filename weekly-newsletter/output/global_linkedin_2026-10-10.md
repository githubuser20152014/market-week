Payrolls missed by 56,000. Stocks rallied. Here's why you shouldn't trust it.

The logic is "bad jobs news means the Fed pauses." Then the FOMC minutes landed: all 19 policymakers backed the last hike, a vocal minority wants a long pause, and rate futures now price 38% odds of another hike in October. A relief rally with a 38% asterisk.

Three markets refused to join the party.

Bonds. A $22 billion 30-year auction cleared at a 5.618% yield. The 10-year spiked to 5.365% mid-week. Buyers did show up at 5.5%, which tells you the selloff has a ceiling. It does not tell you the worry is over.

Gold. It closed at $4,216, within 0.4% of its record, after absorbing that yield spike. In past rate scares gold got sold as the dollar rose. This time it held. Gold is trading less like a rate bet and more like a hedge against everyone's fiscal homework.

Crude. Brent punched through $100 on Middle East infrastructure strikes and Gulf hurricane threats. The G7 released 100 million barrels from strategic reserves. Oil shrugged.

The proof point: the ISM Services Prices Index hit 74.0, the hottest since July 2022. Eurozone inflation printed 3.8%. That is the stagflation setup in two numbers.

The second-order story is Europe. French yields are near 4.95%, an 80-basis-point spread over Germany, while the ECB cannot cut into 3.8% inflation. The Euro Stoxx 50 fell 1.29% for the week. Meanwhile the Russell 2000 dropped 0.96% while the S&P 500 gained 1.04%. This is a mega-cap rally, not a broad one. Small caps are the canaries of domestic credit, and right now they are canaries with a cough.

So which market got it right? Equities say the Fed is done. Bonds say prove it. Gold and crude say the real risks are fiscal and geopolitical, and neither is on a pause schedule. My money is on the three dissenters.
