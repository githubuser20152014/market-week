## The Brief

One pipeline reopened in Saudi Arabia and half the risk premium in global markets evaporated overnight. Tech and small caps ripped higher, the Dow bled, and gold got dumped, because apparently the market's entire fear trade was priced on one stretch of steel staying broken. WTI is now at $89.64, down over 5% on the session, as the Saudi East-West pipeline reopening and [UNGA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UNGA) diplomacy drained the Strait of Hormuz premium everyone swore was permanent. Futures are barely green this morning, which means the real event is the Trump-Xi Oval Office summit at 11 AM [EST](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/EST): go well, the rotation runs. Stall, and tech gives it all back by lunch.

Yesterday's close was a tale of two markets pretending not to notice each other. The Nasdaq added 0.45% to **27,244**, a record nobody will remember by Friday, the Russell 2000 gained 0.52% to 2,875, and the S&P 500 sat dead flat at **7,764.64**, the market equivalent of a shrug emoji. The Dow dropped 0.36% to 51,863, still sulking over financials. **Gold fell 0.54% to $4,352.90**, its safe-haven premium leaking out the second diplomats started talking. **WTI crude cratered 5.23% to $89.64**, a two-week low, because nothing kills a war premium faster than the war logistics working again. The 10-year yield slipped 3.5 basis points to **4.963%**, and the USD Index firmed 0.44% to 100.87, dragging EUR/USD to 1.1412 and GBP/USD to 1.3279 down with it.

The proximate cause is crude's breakdown, and it's almost insultingly simple. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline came back online, restoring the bypass around the Strait of Hormuz that traders had been pricing as gone for good. Confirmed during [UNGA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UNGA) 81 sideline talks, it popped the geopolitical risk premium that had WTI flirting with $100 days ago. Call it the market getting a refund on a war-insurance policy it should never have bought in the first place: risk recedes, energy sells, inflation expectations cool, yields drift. Meanwhile **Xi Jinping's arrival at Andrews** for a three-day state visit is doing the opposite for tech, injecting supply chain certainty into semiconductors just as the Nasdaq needed a reason to keep ripping. Institutional money is rotating into chips on the thesis that the tariff truce holds through Q4. Two catalysts, one session, zero patience for nuance.

For portfolio positioning, the crude collapse is the most actionable signal, and it's not subtle. Energy names face continued pressure ([XLE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLE), [XOP](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XOP)), but the disinflationary knock-on effect is a tailwind for rate-sensitive and growth-oriented exposures. **Bonds finally caught a bid** as yields pulled back from the 5.01% peak, making [TLT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TLT) worth watching for a tactical bounce if the 10-year holds below 5.0%. The Nasdaq's record momentum, powered by semiconductor and [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) infrastructure names, supports [QQQ](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/QQQ) and [SOXX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SOXX). Gold's drop signals the flight-to-safety trade is unwinding: not a reason to chase [GLD](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GLD) lower, but a reason to stop pretending it's a must-hold. Small caps ([IWM](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/IWM)) outperforming confirms lower energy costs are being read as a growth positive, not a demand-destruction warning.

Futures this morning are nearly flat, which is the market politely waiting to see if two men in a room can agree on anything: S&P futures at 7,838, Dow futures at 52,310, Nasdaq futures at 31,029. The Nikkei surged 1.38% and KOSPI gained 1.05%, both already voting yes. **The Oval Office summit at 11 AM [EST](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/EST) is today's swing factor.** A joint statement confirming tariff truce extension sends [QQQ](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/QQQ) and [SOXX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SOXX) higher and squeezes any remaining defensive positions. No communique, and the semiconductor bid reverses fast. Iran's posture on the **Strait of Hormuz** is the wildcard that could make this whole rally look premature.
