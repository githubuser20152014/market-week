### Gridlock Was Supposed to Be Bullish. The S&P 500 Lost 3.1% by Friday.

Democrats swept Congress. The Dow celebrated on Wednesday, because apparently gridlock is a bullish catalyst. Then Thursday showed up. A Democratic House and Senate means oversight of AI data-center power use, tighter export controls, and War Powers limits on Iran policy. The Nasdaq fell **5.5% in a single day**. The VIX hit **28.18**. The S&P 500 finished the week down **3.1%**. The rally lasted roughly one news cycle.

Oil ran its own horror show. WTI started the week above $100 and **fell 11.3% to $90.83** as Qatari mediators floated a Hormuz transit deal and Trump admitted that energy inflation cost Republicans Congress. Nothing lowers oil prices like losing an election. **Gold took the opposite side of the room.** Spot cleared $4,000 mid-week and closed near **$4,066**, up 4.5% on the week, as fiscal gridlock, Lagarde's early ECB exit and a Europe stuck in stagflation made bullion the only adult in the building.

The regime signal is unusually clear: the U.S. political cycle has just structurally increased uncertainty around AI capex, tech regulation, and Middle East military posture. Europe is locked in stagflation, with **Eurozone inflation jumping to 4.0%** and the Bank of England hiking to **4.0%** with a forecast of **5.0% UK CPI by mid-2027**. Gold and defensive real assets are absorbing the inflows that were previously funding momentum tech. This is not a temporary rotation. The VIX closing at 25.6 after a week that included both an election-day Dow rally and a 1,833-point collapse tells you the regime is unstable.

### What it means for you

The 2026 U.S. midterm elections delivered a Democratic congressional sweep that initially rallied markets on gridlock optimism, then crashed them as investors priced the regulatory risk to AI infrastructure and hyperscaler capex. **The S&P 500 fell 3.1% and the Nasdaq dropped 4.0%** for the week, with semiconductors taking the worst of the institutional liquidation and VIX closing at 25.6. **Gold broke $4,000/oz and ended the week up 4.5% near $4,066**, the most important market signal of 2026 so far, driven by ECB leadership uncertainty, U.S. fiscal gridlock, and European stagflation. WTI crude collapsed 11.3% to $90.83 as Trump publicly acknowledged that energy inflation cost Republicans Congress, cementing the political incentive for Middle East de-escalation. For a patient globally diversified investor, the week's moves argue for holding or adding GLD, trimming overweight U.S. tech exposure via QQQ, and monitoring whether Beijing's capital-market support translates into durable upside for EWH after its 5.1% weekly gain.

### Equity Markets - US Crashes as Europe Holds, Asia Splits

U.S. equities had one of the most volatile weeks of 2026. The S&P 500 fell **3.1%** and the Nasdaq shed **4.0%**, with the worst damage concentrated in semiconductors: Nvidia dropped roughly 9% and AMD and Micron fell over 17% on Thursday as the newly elected Democratic Congress signaled scrutiny of AI infrastructure power subsidies and local ballot measures rejected data-center zoning expansions. The Russell 2000 fell **2.7%**, confirming this was not a narrow tech selloff. The Dow's **1.4% decline** was cushioned by its value tilt, but even that came after the index had briefly touched 53,356 on Wednesday before Thursday's 1,833-point reversal erased the gains. VIX at **25.6**, up 34.6% on the week, reflects the speed of the institutional repositioning.

Europe and Asia-Pacific split cleanly. The CAC 40 gained **2.3%** and the Euro Stoxx 50 added **0.6%** despite Fitch revising France's credit outlook to negative and the ECB projecting **3.0% Eurozone inflation against 1.0% GDP growth**. European indices benefited from the dollar's mild weakness and from investors rotating out of U.S. tech, though the Fitch downgrade and ECB leadership vacuum will cap the rally. The Hang Seng was the week's standout, surging **5.1%** as Beijing convened major banks and brokerages to roll out capital-market support mechanisms. Japan's Nikkei was the week's worst performer by far, collapsing **10.7%**: the index reopened from Culture Day on Wednesday into the full force of the Thursday U.S. tech unwind, with semiconductor heavyweights Advantest and Kioxia bearing the brunt.

### Currency Markets - Swiss Franc Leads, Aussie Dollar Bleeds

The dollar index closed up a marginal **0.3% at 102.3**, masking significant intra-week volatility. The greenback weakened sharply after the midterm results as markets priced in legislative gridlock reducing deficit-expansion risk, pulling the **10-year Treasury yield 4 basis points lower to 4.86%**. That dollar softness was the mechanical catalyst for gold's $4,000/oz breakout. The Swiss franc was the week's strongest major currency, gaining **1.2% against the dollar** as risk-off positioning accelerated following Thursday's equity rout. The franc and gold moving in tandem confirms safe-haven demand was the dominant FX theme.

The Australian dollar fell **1.4%**, the worst performer among the majors tracked, reflecting falling oil prices, Nikkei-linked risk-off sentiment in Asia-Pacific, and commodity demand concerns. The pound added **0.5%** as the Bank of England's hawkish **8-1 vote to hike to 4.0%**, with Catherine Mann dissenting for 50 basis points, reinforced sterling's yield advantage. The euro was essentially flat, down **0.1%**, caught between a hawkish ECB December hike signal and the leadership vacuum left by Lagarde's announced February 2027 departure. **FXF (Swiss franc ETF) and FXB (sterling ETF)** benefited from the week's flows; **FXA (Australian dollar ETF)** underperformed.

### Commodities & Metals - Oil Crashes, Gold Breaks $4,000

**WTI crude fell 11.3% to $90.83**, the week's most consequential commodity move. The collapse started on Monday when Qatari mediators circulated a Strait of Hormuz reopening proposal and Trump ruled out pre-election strikes on Iran. Each diplomatic step eroded the $15-20/bbl geopolitical risk premium that had been embedded in crude. By Wednesday, Qatar had circulated a draft MoU and WTI had fallen to $96. Then Trump publicly acknowledged the energy spike cost Republicans Congress, removing any remaining White House appetite for escalation. Oil rebounded modestly on Friday as Middle East nuclear friction flared again, but the weekly loss was severe. Midstream operators and integrated majors with low breakevens are better positioned than leveraged upstream names for the new $88-100 trading range.

Gold's move in the opposite direction was historic. **Spot gold gained 4.5% to $4,066**, breaking cleanly above the $4,000/oz psychological barrier mid-week. The catalyst sequence: post-midterm dollar softness, retreating Treasury yields, Lagarde's ECB departure announcement (triggering European monetary uncertainty), and the structural U.S. fiscal gridlock that guarantees debt-ceiling brinkmanship in 2027. Silver also gained **3.4%**, confirming precious metals demand was broad. Natural gas added **3.8%**, a quiet but notable move as COP31 warnings and Indonesia's bioethanol mandate underscore longer-term energy security capex. Gold above $4,000/oz is not a trade at this point. It is a regime signal.

### This Week's Economic Events

Economic data releases were secondary to political events this week but materially reinforced the macro narrative. The October ISM Manufacturing PMI printed **55.5**, beating the 54.6 consensus, while the Prices Paid sub-index cooled from 77.9 to 75.2. That combination, manufacturing expanding while input cost pressures ease, pulled the 10-year yield toward 4.88%-4.90% mid-week. The ADP private payrolls report showed **86,000 October jobs added**, beating forecasts and pushing the CME FedWatch probability of a December Fed hike to **82%**. The ISM Services PMI printed 54.5, below expectations but still comfortably in expansion. The economic data picture is one of solid-but-moderating U.S. growth with sticky-but-easing domestic inflation. That is a reasonable backdrop for equities. The problem is not the economy: it is the political and regulatory uncertainty layered on top of it.

The Bank of England's decision was the most significant central bank event of the week. The **8-1 vote to hike to 4.0%**, with Catherine Mann pushing for 50 basis points, was more hawkish than markets anticipated. The BoE's November Monetary Policy Report projected UK CPI reaching **5.0% by mid-2027**, driven by Middle East energy pass-through and wage pressures from the Autumn Budget minimum wage changes. That forecast eliminates any near-term BoE pivot narrative. Simultaneously, the ECB's Survey of Professional Forecasters revised Eurozone 2026 inflation to **3.0%** against **1.0% GDP growth**, providing the clearest quantitative definition yet of Europe's stagflationary trap.
