## The Brief

Tech is celebrating a Nasdaq record while the macro floor shifts beneath it: the Trump-Xi state visit beginning today injects a genuine supply chain catalyst, and oil's 4.8% drop on [UNGA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UNGA) diplomacy is doing the Fed's work on inflation. Futures are essentially flat, which is deceptive, because the Swiss franc is rising, gold held near $4,352 after its own pullback, and the 10Y yield at 4.96% is still not confirming that equity multiples are safe. Today's session is a news-driven tape, not a momentum tape.

Tuesday was a standout session: the **Nasdaq surged 2.26% to a record 27,122**, the **S&P 500 cleared 7,764**, and the Dow added 0.71% to 52,048, because nothing says "healthy market" like every index ripping on the same day for three different reasons. The 10-year Treasury yield pulled back 3.5 basis points to **4.963%**, easing pressure that had briefly touched 5.01% earlier in the week, which counts as a rally in bond world. **WTI crude collapsed 4.83% to $91.15**, the sharpest single-day drop in weeks, courtesy of diplomats doing OPEC's job for free. Gold slipped 0.73% to **$4,352**, and the USD Index barely moved at 100.44, the financial equivalent of a shrug.

Three catalysts drove the move. First, [UNGA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UNGA) diplomatic sideline talks between Western and Middle Eastern delegations cooled the geopolitical risk premium baked into crude, and WTI's drop from above $100 to $91 delivered an immediate disinflationary signal, giving the bond market permission to exhale. Second, the Trump-Xi state visit confirmation, with Xi arriving at Andrews today, injected supply chain certainty into semiconductors and consumer electronics, the exact names that led the Nasdaq's 2.3% rip. Third, Industrial Production printed flat at 0.0%, confirming the economy is not reaccelerating, which keeps the Fed on pause rather than leaning toward another hike. Think of it as three compression springs releasing simultaneously: lower energy, lower yields, and trade-truce optimism all hitting at once.
