Drones Hit Riyadh. WTI Jumped 3.6% to $91.49. Futures Are Up Anyway.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The Nasdaq lost 1.25% to 27,193. Gold gained. The 10-year dropped 4.6 basis points. Bonds and bullion are whispering caution while equity futures bounce like nothing happened.

The Dow gained 0.10%. The S&P 500 slid 0.47% to 7,765. The Russell 2000 did absolutely nothing. Congratulations to the index that stayed home.

Then the drones showed up. Houthi strikes on Riyadh airport sent WTI up 3.6% to $91.49, and tankers near Hormuz are still getting harassed. S&P futures are up 0.40% anyway. That is not confidence. That is a borrowed coat.

Here's my read:

A 5.23% 10-year is a gravitational anchor on long-duration growth, a slow leak in the tire of every future earnings stream. That is why the Nasdaq took the beating. The 30-year auction cleared at 2.54x bid-to-cover, which kept long yields off their worst and stopped the unwind from getting uglier.

The Fed is still holding the leash. September FOMC minutes put a 38% implied probability on another October hike. Meanwhile Europe is rallying 1.0 to 1.3% on German industrial production, which makes this open a rally built on somebody else's good news.

KOSPI fell 2.6% and nobody seems to care. That is exactly when you should.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

Gold futures are up 1.2% to $4,207 while equity futures also rally. That divergence exposes real institutional hedging demand. Riyadh, a French fiscal wobble, and a Fed that may still hike on October 27 are all live at once, and gold is not giving back a single dollar of its geopolitical bid.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,175 cash equivalent through 11am ET while SPY is also green. Strength in both means institutional safe-haven buying, not just a risk-off rotation.

Kill switch: WTI drops below $88 on confirmed Hormuz de-escalation and GLD retreats below $4,140. If that happens, the geopolitical premium is unwinding, so get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Energy: long XLE if WTI holds $89.50 at the open. Free cash flow machines at $90+ crude. Break below $88 and the catalyst is gone. Delta's fuel-cost warning this morning is the canary, and the canary is coughing.
  • Duration: stay away from TLT. A 5.23% 10-year and a possible hike means real mark-to-market pain. Take SGOV or SHY near 4.95% instead. Boring is currently winning.
  • Gold: don't you dare sell it if GLD opens strong and holds past 10am ET while SPY rallies. That is hedging demand, not momentum.
  • Tech: underweight QQQ on any fade. Nasdaq futures are up 0.83%, but if the 10-year drifts back toward 5.30%, multiples compress again and the gap fill becomes a short.
  • Europe: the trade nobody is talking about is EWG. The DAX is up 1.28% early on German industrial production. If the French budget debate doesn't trigger a no-confidence motion, follow-through is on the table.
