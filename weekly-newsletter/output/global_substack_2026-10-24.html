France Crossed 5%. The Hang Seng Lost 5.05%. Europe Brought Riots, Asia Brought Hope.

The Global Investor Edition · Framework Foundry · October 24, 2026

Houthi drones hit Saudi Arabia's Shaybah oil field and the Dammam airport. Brent blew past $114. WTI cleared $100. Gold, the thing you buy when the world is on fire, fell 4.23% to $4,007.90.

Sure. That tracks.

Meanwhile the market spent the week firing megacap tech. Meta fell nearly 7% for asking investors to fund data centers with new share classes. Lam Research dropped 9%+. Tesla lost 5%. Lockheed Martin jumped 5% on a $292 billion backlog, and Procter & Gamble jumped 5% for selling toothpaste at higher prices. The AI spending party now has a cover charge, and nobody brought a wallet.

Here's my read:

The macro driver is a rotation, and the rotation has a price tag. At a 4.99% 10-year, cash-generative boring beats speculative compute. The Nasdaq fell 1.67% and the S&P 500 dropped 1.03%. The Russell 2000 gained 1.72% and the Dow added 0.46%. Same week, same country, opposite verdicts. Boring is the new alpha. At these yields, it always was.

International equities: Europe lost everywhere, evenly. The Euro Stoxx 50 fell 1.97%, the FTSE 100 dropped 1.76%, the DAX lost 1.41%, and the CAC 40 slid 1.30%. France's 10-year crossed 5% for the first time since 2002, riots ran from Paris to Tirana, and the IMF hinted the ECB may hike again before year-end. Hiking into riots. Bold. Asia had it worse. The Hang Seng fell 5.05% after the PBOC held loan prime rates for the 17th straight month and stimulus hopefuls went home empty. The Nikkei dropped 3.28% as AI hardware valuations deflated. MSCI EM fell 2.84%. The ASX 200 slipped just 0.13%. Commodities and geography, doing their job.

Currencies, commodities & metals: The USD Index slipped 0.20% to 101.81. The yen gained 0.62%, the franc 0.45%, and sterling 0.28% on a 3.5% UK CPI print. The euro lagged at -0.22%, which is what happens when your bond market and your streets both catch fire. WTI closed at $103.27, up 1.49%, and a 60-day Russian diesel waiver bought Washington roughly 24 hours of cheaper oil. Natural gas collapsed 14.56%. Gold fell 4.23% and silver dropped 5.79% to $53.71, as higher-for-longer rate fears and equity margin calls hit the bullion. The $4,000 level is now the test.

The One Trade: LONG $IWM

US small caps gained 1.72% while the S&P lost 1.03% and the Nasdaq fell 1.67%. That is the first clean week of Russell outperformance during a broad large-cap selloff since the AI concentration trade started unwinding. The Fed is on hold at 93% probability, the 10-year slid roughly 10 basis points on the week to 4.99%, and small caps carry no exposure to AI data-center capex or European fiscal contagion. The Russell held a weekly high of 2,791 and closed at 2,775, while large-cap indices closed near their lows.

Confirms: IWM holds above 2,750 and the Russell keeps beating QQQ on any further tech earnings miss.

Kill switch: the 10-year back above 5.15% on hot inflation, or a Middle East escalation that sells every equity size factor at once. If it hits, get out fast.
