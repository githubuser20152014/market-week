## The Brief

The Strait of Hormuz reopened for business Monday, and business was mines. U.S. strikes on Larak Island sent WTI up 3.7% to $86.52, while Fed Chair Kevin Warsh used his Jackson Hole debut to remind everyone that rate cuts are not happening, pinning the 10-year at 4.72%. Small-caps got the memo first, the Russell 2000 dropped 1.4% while the S&P shrugged off just 0.25%, and futures are sitting flat this morning because the real catalyst isn't on the calendar, it's floating in the Persian Gulf.

Two macro forces landed on the same Monday, which reads more like bad luck than bad planning. The Dow barely blinked, down 0.02% to **53,559**, the S&P 500 slipped 0.25% to **7,711**, and the Nasdaq dropped 0.52% to 26,402, all polite compared to what came next. The Russell 2000 cratered 1.39% to 2,972, because small-caps are the first to flinch when credit tightens, and right now they're flinching hard. Cross-assets weren't buying the calm: **WTI crude exploded 3.7% to $86.52** on the Hormuz strike, the 10-year Treasury yield jumped 4.8 basis points to **4.72%** on hawkish Fed talk, gold added 0.5% to **$4,500.80** because paranoia never goes out of style, and the USD index slipped 0.18% to 99.52, sliding even as the Fed talks tough, which tells you nobody actually believes the talk.

The catalyst was a one-two punch, the **Strait of Hormuz** and Jackson Hole, in that order of drama. U.S. Central Command struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island after Iran seeded naval mines in the transit corridor, and a commercial tanker took two mine hits for its trouble. Roughly 20% of global oil transit runs through that stretch of water, so crude's biggest single-day move in months is not a coincidence, it's geography. Meanwhile Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote** confirmed rate cuts are dead for now, with swap markets pricing a 57% chance of a September hike. Oil up plus a hawkish Fed is a compression trade with a bow on it: oil feeds inflation, inflation delays cuts, and rate hikes shrink the value of every future earnings dollar. **Small-caps broke hardest** because they run on floating-rate debt and thin margins, exactly the combination the market punishes first.

The Hormuz escalation is not resolved, the UN Security Council called emergency sessions and a commercial tanker is already limping home with mine damage. **Energy trade is back on the table** via [XLE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLE) and [OIH](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/OIH), with crude likely to stay elevated as long as the corridor stays contested. On rates, **bonds never bought the soft-landing story**, and with Warsh signaling hikes instead of cuts, [TLT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TLT) remains a short candidate while [SGOV](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SGOV) and short-duration cash equivalents look like the only sane parking lot left. Gold at **$4,500** is holding through a softer dollar and weaker equities at the same time, the classic double signal that nobody trusts the calm. [GLD](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GLD) deserves a core allocation. Avoid high-multiple growth and rate-sensitive sectors: [XLU](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLU) and [XLRE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLRE) both face margin compression if September delivers the hike.

S&P futures sit at -0.18% to 7,708 this morning, technically flat, practically holding its breath. APAC closed mixed overnight, with KOSPI the lone bright spot at +0.46%, though a Coupang-related tariff spat may cap Korean upside before lunch. Industrial Production drops at 9:15 AM ET, but nobody actually cares about that print. The real catalyst is whatever comes next from the **Strait of Hormuz**: convoy negotiations, another tanker incident, or an IRGC maritime advisory escalation could all push WTI toward $90 and drag the rotation out of growth and into energy and defensives. Watch $87.50 on WTI as the trigger level for the next leg.
