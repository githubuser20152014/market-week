S&P +0.70%. Nasdaq +1.00%. Dow +0.53%. A clean risk-on close on paper.

Here's why you shouldn't trust yesterday's tape as clean risk-on.

Bonds didn't buy it. The 10-year yield jumped 8.2 basis points to 4.745% on the same day equities rallied. Yields climbing into a rally isn't confirmation, it's the bond market pricing risk the index level is choosing to ignore.

Gold didn't buy it either. It surged 1.60% to $4,113, moving in the same direction as yields. Gold and yields rising together almost never happens in a market that actually believes its own optimism. It's the safe-haven crowd and the bond market raising an eyebrow at the exact same time.

Crude is the one move that makes sense on the surface: WTI cratered 5.24% after diplomatic talks in Muscat pulled the Iran war premium out of the market overnight. But that also means one of the props holding up the "everything's fine" story just got kicked out.

Amazon's AWS beat is still doing the heavy lifting for the Nasdaq's outperformance this stretch. Strip that one result out and the tech rally looks a lot thinner than the headline suggests.

Here's the angle nobody's covering: the US and Japan just ran their first coordinated currency intervention in 15 years, and the yen jumped nearly 2% against the dollar overnight, forcing a rapid unwind of yen-funded carry trades. KOSPI took the worst of it, down 5.12%, on heavy Japanese institutional cross-ownership. That's a bigger structural story than a single green close in New York.

So which market got it right yesterday, equities or bonds? Yields don't jump 8bps into a rally for fun, and gold doesn't climb alongside them for fun either. Both are pricing something the S&P hasn't gotten the memo on yet.

I know which one I'm listening to.
