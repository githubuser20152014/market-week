## The Brief

Tech partied, gold partied, and the Dow got left holding everyone's coats. Cooler July CPI gave equities an excuse to celebrate, but a 17.4-million-barrel oil inventory build crashed the after-party before crude could show up. Gold ripped to $4,440 on a softening dollar, and the 10-year yield just shrugged, because the bond market has heard this rate-cut story before and isn't buying a ticket yet. Futures are green this morning, but only the polite kind: the Hormuz standoff, shipping-chaos headlines, and an 8:30 Durable Goods print are all standing by to ruin it.

The S&P 500 closed at **7,748.50, up 0.26%**, the Nasdaq added 0.54% to **26,588.49**, and the Russell 2000 gained 0.61% to 3,045.48, small-caps finally showing a pulse. The Dow dipped 21 points to 53,770, the industrials sitting this one out like they weren't invited. Gold climbed 0.71% to **$4,440**, because nothing says confidence in the soft landing like everyone quietly buying the hedge anyway. WTI crude fell 1.39% to **$82.11** on that inventory build, the 10-year yield barely budged at **4.682%**, and the USD Index slipped to 99.90, three assets collectively declining to RSVP to the rally.

Two catalysts did the heavy lifting. First, July CPI printed at 0.1% month-over-month and 3.4% year-over-year, right on consensus, confirming inflation is still drifting lower despite three FOMC members grumbling about it. Think of the Fed as a car with one foot still on the brake: the CPI print didn't lift that foot, it just confirmed nobody's pressing harder. Second, [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) earnings actually delivered for once: Super Micro Computer and CoreWeave each surged 19%, Nvidia gained 3%, and that trio dragged the Nasdaq and S&P higher while the industrials-heavy Dow watched from the parking lot. Oil's drop was its own story: **a 17.4-million-barrel [EIA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/EIA) inventory build** steamrolled the **Strait of Hormuz** risk premium, because apparently a glut beats a geopolitical crisis every time.

Going into today, S&P futures are up 0.17% to 7,783.75, Dow futures up 0.20%, Nasdaq futures up 0.08%, cautiously optimistic and not much more. KOSPI's 3.56% surge overnight is the loudest move in the region, Korean chipmakers riding the same [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) wave carrying US tech. The real test is **Durable Goods Orders** at 8:30 ET: a hot print checks whether rate-sensitive longs actually hold, a miss just hands the soft-landing crowd more ammunition and sends [TLT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TLT) and [GLD](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GLD) higher. Watch the 10-year at 4.68%, that's the line in the sand today.
