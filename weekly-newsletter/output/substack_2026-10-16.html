The Nasdaq Gained 0.08%. The Russell Gained 1.38%. Growth Stocks Are Having a Quiet Breakdown.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Producer prices ran at 6.3% year-over-year. The 10-year yield fell 10 basis points to 5.089%. Pick the one that makes sense, because bonds apparently didn't read the data.

Thursday's tape was a rotation with a pulse. The Russell 2000 gained 1.38% to 2,767. The Dow added 1.08% to 52,177. The S&P 500 managed 0.33% to 7,744. The Nasdaq did +0.08% to 26,713, which is a rounding error with a ticker.

Growth isn't dead. It just stopped getting invited.

Here's my read:

Wednesday's sticky 3.7% CPI torched growth names as the 10-year touched 5.25%. Thursday's PPI and a strong 0.8% retail sales gain should have finished the job. Instead, BNY Mellon, Charles Schwab, U.S. Bancorp and Morgan Stanley's $9 billion wealth quarter handed allocators a reason to buy banks and industrials.

The regime is nominal growth and higher for longer. Cash-flow cyclicals beat long-duration tech, and the tape agrees. $TLT buyers show up every time yields poke 5.25%. That's a ceiling forming, even if the Fed can't cut.

Gold held $4,196 in a risk-on tape. That's dollar weakness (the dollar index slipped 0.32% to 101.67) and real-rate skepticism talking, not fear. WTI crude dumped 2.03% to $95.98: profit-taking after Brent's run near $106, plus Trump ruling out nuclear weapons or ground troops in any Iran conflict. Nothing says de-escalation like a list of things you promise not to do.

Pre-market, S&P futures are up 0.16% near 7,803 and Nasdaq futures are down 0.07%. Same rotation, one more day. APAC closed green, led by KOSPI +2.05% on chip exports after TSMC's $46.72 billion Q3 revenue. The swing factor today: does the 10-year hold below 5.10%? A drift toward 5.20% pressures the Nasdaq and reopens the growth-versus-cyclicals fight.

The One Trade: LONG $XLF

Three bulge-bracket earnings beats in two sessions, a 10-year yield retreating from its 5.25% ceiling, and institutional money visibly leaving Nasdaq for financials. The sector has fundamental wind at its back and no offsetting catalyst in sight.

Confirms: XLF holds above yesterday's close and adds 0.3% or more by 11 AM ET, with the 10-year staying below 5.12%.

Kill switch: the 10-year breaks back above 5.18% before noon. That means bond buyers are retreating and deposit-cost anxiety returns for the regional banks inside XLF. Get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • $IWM: The surge to 2,767 is real rotation, not a squeeze. A resilient New Home Sales print (10 AM ET) gives it a path toward 2,800. A miss with yields backing up hits small caps first. They carry the variable-rate debt.
  • $GLD: Don't you dare sell it. Gold isn't dropping into a risk-on open, and that's the signal. It breaks only if the 10-year resets above 5.20% and the dollar index reclaims 102.50.
  • $XLE: Stay away. The escalation premium just got drained. The trade nobody is talking about is LNG exporters like $LNG, with EU gas storage at 74% against a historical 85-90% buffer.
  • $TLT: A bounce trade, not a thesis. If yields hold below 5.10% today, rent it. Don't marry it: 6.3% PPI and a Fed boxed into holding rates high keep the structural trend lower.
