The economy added 29,000 jobs. Wall Street wanted 85,000 to 90,000. Stocks rallied anyway.

Here is why you shouldn't trust the celebration.

The S&P 500 gained 0.73% to 7,722.72. The Nasdaq jumped 1.19%. The logic: weak jobs means the Fed holds at the October 27-28 meeting, and futures now price 75-80% odds of exactly that. Equities heard "pause" and threw a party. A Fed that holds is not a Fed that cuts.

The dissenting markets:

Bonds. The 10-year yield rose 4 basis points to 5.277% on the same day stocks rallied. If the jobs number really cooled the rate story, yields would have fallen. They didn't. The bond market still wants compensation for a $40.1 trillion debt load and $739 billion in quarterly issuance. One soft print does not change that math.

Gold. It fell 0.90% to $4,162.30 during the US session, then bounced to roughly $4,196 in overnight futures. Buyers showed up the moment equities went home. That is what a hedge looks like when it doesn't believe the celebration.

Crude. WTI is stuck near $90 with the IEA's 100-million-barrel SPR release acting as a ceiling and OPEC+ quota discipline acting as a floor. Stuck is its own kind of message.

The institutional proof point: Goldman's Gutman is on the tape this morning telling governments to cut spending to curb runaway borrowing costs. When a Wall Street heavyweight is lecturing sovereigns on the bond market, the bond market has your attention.

The second-order story nobody is covering: Europe. The CAC 40 is off 0.97% while the Nikkei jumped 2.40%, and Eurozone headline inflation is running at 3.8%. Stagflation pressure on one side of the Atlantic, a relief rally on the other. Those two stories do not stay separate forever.

So which market got it right? Stocks are betting the debt problem solves itself. Bonds and gold are betting it doesn't. One of them is wrong, and I know which one I'd rather be explaining to my clients in six months.
