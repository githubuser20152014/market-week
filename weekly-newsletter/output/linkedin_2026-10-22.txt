The S&P 500 closed up 0.58% on Wednesday. Here's why you shouldn't trust it.

The Nasdaq gained 1.05%. Then the earnings dropped, and Nasdaq futures are now down 1.76% before the coffee is done. The rally lasted about as long as a Tesla capex target.

The markets that didn't join the party:

Bonds. The 10-year yield rose 2.9 bps to 5.146%. That is not a market relaxing. That is a market telling every rate-sensitive sector the ceiling is still there, and it's low.

Gold. Down 1.09% to $4,105. Before you call that a verdict on safe havens, look at who is selling. Leveraged tech and crypto longs are dumping their winners to cover Nasdaq losses. The thesis didn't break. The margin accounts did.

Crude. WTI jumped 2.38% to $99.46, with Brent near $112. The State Department ordered non-essential diplomats out of Saudi Arabia and the UK sent RAF jets to guard Saudi airspace. Oil is pricing in a Gulf that is not calm.

The proof point: Lam Research fell 9% on soft wafer-fab tool guidance. Tesla slid 5% after trimming full-year capex. Meta edged lower because Wall Street wanted margins, not another AI spending narrative. Three misses at record valuations. The market has stopped paying for stories.

The angle nobody is covering: housing. A 5.146% 10-year, homebuilder confidence at 14-year lows and housing starts down 5.2% month over month. While everyone argues about AI capex, the rate story is quietly squeezing the most rate-sensitive corner of the economy.

Asia already read the room. KOSPI lost 3.52%, Hang Seng lost 3.03%, Nikkei lost 2.65%. The yen strengthened sharply, with USD/JPY falling to 156.93.

So which market got it right? Equities celebrated for one session. Oil, yields and Asia all said something else. The dissenters have the better track record this week. Equities are going to have to explain themselves, and the earnings calendar is not on their side.
