Brent Is at $105. The 10-Year Is at 5.24%. Bank Earnings Are the Good News.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Houthi missiles hit two Saudi airports over the weekend. Brent is at $105. And gold, the thing you buy when missiles fly, dropped 0.44% to $4,198.

The Dow rose 0.83% to 51,654. The S&P gained 0.59% to 7,811. Nobody believed it, least of all the bond market, which sits at a 5.244% 10-year and refuses to clap.

A war broke out and the safe haven sold off. The dollar took the bid. Gold got the bill.

Here's my read:

Oil spikes on a Middle East escalation and gold falls. That is not a typo. The dollar is eating the safe-haven trade: DXY at 102.44, USD/JPY at 158.63. WTI jumped 1.09% to $92.85 after strikes on King Khalid International in Riyadh and King Fahd International in Dammam. It was already parked near $90 on Strait of Hormuz warnings, so this was less a shock than a second helping. Trump hinting he might join the fight did not help the de-escalation crowd. North Korea fired missiles too: KOSPI fell 3.65%, and everyone else shrugged. Meanwhile futures are flat. S&P at 7,862, up 0.03%. Nasdaq futures are down 0.23%, so money is quietly leaving tech for value and energy. The market is not calm. It is waiting for JPMorgan.

The One Trade: LONG $XLE

Houthi strikes on Riyadh's main airport and live Hormuz warnings have repriced the geopolitical premium into crude. Brent is above $105. WTI is at $92.85. Trump's signal about military involvement adds an escalation layer that energy equities have not fully priced.

Confirms: XLE holds above its prior close and WTI stays above $91 by 11:00 AM ET, with no de-escalation headlines out of Riyadh or Washington.

Kill switch: Trump or Saudi Arabia formally rules out direct US involvement and WTI drops below $90. That premium is the whole trade, so if it goes, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Gold: stay away from GLD near-term. It fell during a war. Re-engage only if the dollar index breaks back below 102.
  • Banks: JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, and Citigroup report before the open. With the 10-year at 5.24% and jobless claims under 200k, net interest income should be fat. Watch KBE. If credit loss provisions surprise higher, the move reverses quickly.
  • Tech: go light on QQQ. Intel and Broadcom CEOs say capital could be AI's biggest bottleneck. That is not a tailwind for high multiples.
  • Duration: TLT stays in the penalty box. CPI is Wednesday and PPI is Thursday. A hot print pushes yields toward 5.36%.
  • The level: 7,850 on the S&P is support. If it holds through an earnings miss, that is constructive. If it doesn't, you will know by 10 AM.
