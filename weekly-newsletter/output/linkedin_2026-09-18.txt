Nasdaq jumped 1.69% Friday on a good Nvidia forecast. Small caps moved 0.55%. That gap should worry you more than the headline number comforts you.

Here's why: when one earnings story does all the lifting for an index, that's not conviction, it's concentration risk wearing a rally costume.

The bond market wasn't fooled. The 10-year yield eased six basis points off its 5.01% high, but it's still sitting near 4.95%, a level that keeps the "higher for longer" thesis fully intact. Bonds aren't celebrating anything. They're taking a breath before the next test.

Gold refused to play along too. It held near $4,418 even as yields fell and the dollar firmed, the exact combination that's supposed to send gold lower. When gold ignores its own playbook, it's usually pricing something the equity market hasn't priced yet, most likely the slow campaign against dollar clearing coming out of the BRICS New Delhi Declaration, with a Trump-Xi summit landing September 24 to complicate things further.

And crude told its own story entirely. Oil fell nearly 6% in a single session after Saudi Aramco quietly started routing tankers around a chokepoint near Oman's Sohar terminal instead of through it, and a surprise EIA distillate inventory build confirmed supply is looser than the market believed. Three straight red sessions have unwound most of the geopolitical risk premium that had crude flirting with $109 just days ago.

Nvidia's datacenter guidance is the corporate proof point behind Friday's move, and it's real. But one company's hardware outlook is doing the work that used to require broad-based earnings strength across dozens of names. That's not how durable rallies get built.

The angle nobody's covering: tanker routing decisions out of the Gulf are becoming a bigger swing factor for oil supply than the geopolitical headlines dominating the news cycle. Watch shipping and logistics data, not just Houthi headlines, if you want to see the next oil move before the wires report it.

So which market got it right on Friday? Equities, betting the rate-hike shock is behind us? Or bonds and gold, both still pricing a Fed that isn't done? I'll take the side of the market that doesn't get a hardware upgrade every time Nvidia posts a good forecast. Bonds and gold are telling the more honest story right now.
