The S&P fell 0.52% yesterday. If the headline you read said "markets dipped amid geopolitical uncertainty," don't trust it. The real story broke in the bond market, and it's uglier than a half-point equity move.

The 30-year Treasury just hit 5.31%, its highest level in nineteen years. That's not equities catching a cold. That's the yield curve pricing in a Fed that just lost its rate-cut runway.

Bonds: The 30-year at a 19-year high while stocks fall is the market telling you this isn't a growth scare, it's a supply shock. Duration isn't a safe haven right now, it's the thing getting sold.

Gold: Gold pushed past $4,452 on the same day equities dropped and bonds sold off too. That combination only shows up when the market is buying inflation insurance, not chasing a trade. Watch what gold is doing, not what the headlines say.

Crude: Brent spiked above $91 and WTI cleared $87 intraday after a tanker was struck in the Strait of Hormuz, the corridor carrying roughly one in five barrels of crude traded globally. The 60-day US-Iran ceasefire expired hours before the strike. Oil didn't wait for confirmation, and neither did the Fed's math.

Japanese automakers are the clearest read-through right now. They're catching a crude-and-yen double punch at the same time, the exact mechanism this macro story predicts: higher input costs, a strengthening yen against a weakening dollar, and margin compression nobody priced in two weeks ago.

Here's the angle nobody is covering: a prolonged Hormuz disruption doesn't stop at gas prices. Tanker insurance premiums and freight rates spike first, and that cost moves through global shipping before it ever shows up in a CPI print. Watch freight and shipping data over the next two weeks, not just oil.

So which market got it right yesterday, equities or gold? Equities treated this as a half-point news day. Gold treated it as a regime change. I'm siding with gold. The bond market already agrees.
