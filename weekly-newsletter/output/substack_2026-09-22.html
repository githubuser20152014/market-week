Oil Dropped 4.8% on Diplomacy. The Fed Didn't Have to Lift a Finger.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The Nasdaq closed at a record 27,122 yesterday and nobody in that rally actually did anything. Diplomats did. Xi is landing at Andrews today, UN sideline talks knocked oil down 4.8% in a single session, and the Fed got a disinflation gift it didn't have to earn.

Three separate stories, three separate rooms, and somehow they all rhymed: lower energy, lower yields, tech at a record high. That's not a coincidence you should trust blindly.

When geopolitics does the central bank's job for free, ask what happens the day the diplomacy stops working.

Here's my read:

Tuesday: Nasdaq +2.26% to 27,122, S&P 500 cleared 7,764, Dow +0.71% to 52,048. Meanwhile WTI crude collapsed 4.83% to $91.15, its sharpest single-day drop in weeks, on UN General Assembly diplomacy cooling the Middle East risk premium. The 10-year Treasury yield pulled back to 4.963% from a 5.01% high earlier in the week, which is the bond market finally exhaling. Gold slipped 0.73% to $4,352 even as yields fell, which is the tell: the fear premium is unwinding faster than the rate case can justify. Add in a flat 0.0% Industrial Production print keeping the Fed on pause, and you get three compression springs releasing at once. Convenient, if you don't ask too many questions about the timing.

The One Trade: Long $SOXX

Xi's bilateral agenda today reportedly includes critical mineral supply agreements and AI hardware export terms, the exact two inputs that have kept a ceiling on semiconductor valuations all year. That's the ceiling coming off, not just a good news day.

Confirms: SOXX holds above yesterday's close in the first 30 minutes, NQ futures sustain 30,800+ by 10:30 AM ET, and any joint statement carries tariff-exclusion language for electronics.

Kill switch: the joint statement slips past Thursday's summit, or leaks say AI chip export rules are tightening instead of easing. If SOXX breaks below yesterday's open on that news, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • QQQ - long it conditionally on real tariff-exclusion language; if the statement is vague, trim into any morning gap-up rather than chase it.
  • XLE - stay away from it here. WTI broke its $96 floor and is targeting $88. Great for your gas bill, bad for integrated oil names.
  • TLT - don't you dare chase it at 4.96% on the 10Y. Wait for a close below 4.85% before adding real duration.
  • GLD - the trade nobody is talking about is that gold selling off while yields fall and diplomacy advances is not bullish. Hold, don't add, until it reclaims $4,400.
  • Watch the Swiss franc, up 0.41% against the dollar. A quiet hedge that hasn't been unwound yet tells you someone doesn't fully believe the peace dividend.
