The 30-Year Hit 5.31%, a 19-Year High. The Fed's Homework Just Got Harder.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The bond market just told the Fed to sit down. The 30-year Treasury broke 5.31%, a 19-year high, on the same day equities dropped and gold ripped past $4,450. That is not a coincidence. That is a market pricing in a ceasefire that expired and a tanker strike nobody had on the calendar.

The 60-day US-Iran ceasefire ran out overnight. Within hours a commercial vessel took a hit in the Strait of Hormuz, the corridor that ships roughly one in every five barrels of crude on earth. Traders did the math before breakfast: higher oil, higher inflation, fewer rate cuts. The Fed's homework just got a lot harder.

Here's my read:

This is stagflation math wearing a trenchcoat. The S&P 500 dropped 0.52% to 7,745 and the Dow shed half a percent to 53,460, while the 10-year yield climbed to 4.696% and the 30-year printed its highest level in nineteen years. Normally bonds catch a bid when stocks fall. Not today. Brent spiked above $91 and WTI cleared $87 intraday before settling near $84, and that is the entire story: crude went vertical, so the market re-priced the Fed's rate-cut runway shorter. Gold surged to $4,452 and held it, which is the market's way of saying it does not trust duration, cash, or the Fed to fix this before Hormuz resolves. When gold outperforms everything without breaking a sweat, that's not a rally, that's a warning.

The One Trade: Long $GLD

Gold is holding above $4,450 as equities drop and real yields rise at the same time, a combination that only shows up when the market is buying inflation insurance, not chasing momentum. With Hormuz unresolved and zero Fed easing catalyst on the calendar, the floor holds.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,420 intraday through the first hour of trading (10:30 AM ET) with no confirmed diplomatic re-engagement on the Iran ceasefire.

Kill switch: Washington announces a renewed ceasefire framework with Tehran, crude reverses below $82, and gold futures break below $4,400 intraday. If that happens, get out fast.
