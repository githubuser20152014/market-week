## The Brief

The market is pricing a stagflation shock, not a growth scare. Oil at $97 and the 10-year at 4.837% closed together in the same session, a combination that historically compresses equity multiples fast. The Hormuz interdiction is the dominant macro variable, keeping a floor under crude, a ceiling on bonds, and a question mark over every Fed cut priced for Q4.

Yesterday's tape had one theme: sell stocks, buy anything that survives a war. The **S&P 500 fell 0.48% to 7,636**, the Nasdaq dropped 0.64%, the Dow shed 0.77%, and the **Russell 2000 led the carnage at -1.32%**, because small caps have no oil hedge and no Fed put, just vibes and a maturity wall. **WTI crude closed at $97.22, up 1.22%**, stacking gains on Brent's break above $101, the kind of move that makes airline CFOs update their resumes. The **10-year Treasury yield settled at 4.837%, up 3 basis points**, closing in on the highest levels since late 2023, because nothing says "credible inflation fight" like a bond market that refuses to be bought. Gold held firm at **$4,435, up 0.43%**, quietly doing its one job. The dollar index barely moved at 98.76, the only asset yesterday with the decency to be boring.

The driver is the **Strait of Hormuz**, again. U.S. CENTCOM strikes disabled three Iranian tankers earlier this week, Iran answered with naval exclusion zone threats and its own tanker interdictions, maritime war-risk insurers pulled Gulf coverage like it was a bad Yelp review, and OPEC+ confirmed it will hold production quotas through October just to make sure nobody feels better. Net effect: 20% of global oil shipments now trade on geopolitical mood, [PPI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PPI) and CPI expectations are re-accelerating, and central banks are back in hiking posture whether they like it or not. Treasury Secretary Bessent expanded the debt buyback facility to $6 billion hoping to cap the 10-year, and the bond market said thanks for the liquidity, still selling. **Bonds never bought the rally**, and now they are pricing another Fed hike at the September 15-16 FOMC, with interest-rate futures showing an 82% probability of 25 basis points.

## What it means for you

For investors, the setup is simple: own things you can touch, skip things that owe you money in thirty years. Energy producers are cashing an enormous windfall: WTI near $97 with Brent above $100 is a margin event, not a headline, for domestic E&P names ([XLE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLE), [XOP](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XOP)). Gold at $4,435 is not just a war hedge, it is a currency hedge, because the dollar can't collect a rate-differential bid when oil is blowing out the trade deficit at the same time ([GLD](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GLD), [IAU](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/IAU)). Short-duration Treasuries and T-bills still yield 4.5%-plus and are the only fixed-income position that doesn't bleed when yields rise ([SGOV](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SGOV), [BIL](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BIL)). **Long-duration bonds are a trap here**: every [TLT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TLT) holder is fighting the Fed and the oil market at the same time, and losing both fights. Small caps ([IWM](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/IWM)) remain the most exposed: high refinancing costs, energy input sensitivity, and a safe-haven bid of exactly zero.

Going into today, S&P futures are up a token 0.11% and Dow futures +0.19%, while Nasdaq futures sag -0.11%, because tech multiple compression doesn't take days off. The Hang Seng dropped **1.27% overnight** and the [ASX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ASX) fell 1.03%, both pricing the same Gulf escalation and rate-hike math everyone else is. Europe is flat, wisely waiting to see what New York does before committing to an opinion. The **8:30 AM ET Durable Goods Orders** print is today's swing factor: a weak number confirms the stagflation thesis and drags equities lower, a beat on capital spending buys industrials one good news cycle. Watch whether the 10-year yield holds below 4.85% into the release. A breach there means the bond market has stopped waiting for the Fed and started making its own decisions.
