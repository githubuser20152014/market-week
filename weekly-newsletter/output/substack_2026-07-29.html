The Fed Held Rates at 3.50%. Iran Fired First. Guess Which One Moved Markets.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Iran fired ballistic missiles at U.S. forces in the Middle East overnight. The Fed also met yesterday and held rates at 3.50%-3.75% for the fifth straight time. One of these events moved every asset class. It was not the Fed.

WTI crude ripped 5% to $83.23 because oil traders price fear before facts. Gold added 1.23% to $4,085 because paranoia is a permanent bull market. The 10-year yield slid 3.7 basis points to 4.604% as bond buyers quietly bought the "this could get worse" trade. Equities, meanwhile, could not decide what century they were in: the Dow gained 1.03% to 52,747 on energy and defense weight, the Nasdaq slipped 0.22% because tech earnings scrutiny does not care whose missiles are flying.

Chair Warsh held the line on rates. Tehran held the headline.

Here's my read:

One event explains the entire cross-asset picture: crude spikes on supply-disruption fear, gold bids as the geopolitical panic button, Treasuries rally on safe-haven demand, tech sells off because nothing kills a growth multiple like actual missiles. Textbook geopolitical shock playbook, executed on schedule. The Senate's 86-12 vote advancing Iran sanctions legislation had already told you this fight was coming. The missiles just RSVP'd. Overseas, the KOSPI cratered 6% as Samsung and SK Hynix got hit on AI-capex-ROI skepticism that predates the strike entirely, which means Asia was already nervous before Iran gave it a reason to be. Pre-market S&P futures are up a token 0.17% to 7,478. That is not confidence. That is a market that has not finished pricing this yet.

The One Trade: Long $XLE

Iran's strike put a geopolitical risk premium back into crude that the market had not fully priced, and WTI's 5% single-day move is the loudest signal on the board today.

Confirms: XLE holds above its prior session close in the first 30 minutes with WTI sustaining above $82.50. Green tape on energy while broader futures stay flat or soften is your tell.

Kill switch: Iran issues a formal de-escalation statement or a ceasefire lands before 10 AM ET, sending WTI back below $80 and taking the entire risk premium out behind the woodshed. If that happens, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Long GLD: gold at $4,085 refused to give back gains even as equities found a bid. Add on any dip below $4,060. The trade breaks if gold reverses below $4,020, but until then, don't you dare sell it.
  • Long ITA: Iran's strike is a direct defense-spending catalyst, and the White House's Patriot missile deal with Ukraine only adds fuel. ITA holds up even if equities soften on tech.
  • Underweight QQQ: the Nasdaq's underperformance plus KOSPI's near-6% AI-capex scare is a direct read-through to U.S. semis and hyperscalers. Stay away until Microsoft and Meta prove the cloud math still works.
  • Hold TLT: the yield dip is a genuine safe-haven move, but Chair Warsh's hawkish-leaning posture caps the upside. This is the trade nobody is talking about because it is boring, and boring is the point right now.
