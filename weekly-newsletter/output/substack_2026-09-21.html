Trump and Xi Made Nice for a Week. Oil Lost $7 a Barrel Overnight.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Trump and Xi agreed to be civil for a week, and oil believed them instantly. WTI cratered nearly 7% to $93.51 overnight, its fourth straight losing session, because nothing kills a war premium like a handshake in Washington.

Equity futures are throwing a party. S&P futures up 1.4%, Nasdaq futures up 2.1%, APAC already dancing: KOSPI +1.6%, Nikkei +1.4%, Hang Seng +1.2%. Gold, on the other hand, isn't buying any of it.

Gold is flat while yields ease, which is the market's polite way of admitting this rally is about reduced fear, not a growth upgrade.

Here's my read:

Blame diplomacy for the crude massacre. The Trump-Xi summit, locked in for September 23-25 in Washington, drained the war premium that had oil trading above $105 last week. Bessent called his sit-down with Vice Premier He Lifeng "successful," the two sides agreed to a $30 billion tariff mitigation framework, and suddenly nobody needs to hedge against a trade war that isn't happening. Saudi Aramco's expanded Red Sea bypass capacity and a 2.1 million barrel distillate inventory build finished the job: there is no supply-shock story left to tell, just diplomacy making the traders who bet on chaos look foolish. Meanwhile the Bank of Japan's 1.25% policy rate keeps unwinding the yen carry trade, USD/JPY up to 157.32 overnight, because someone always has to ruin a good mood.

The One Trade: Long $QQQ

Nasdaq futures up 2.1% on confirmed Trump-Xi summit progress and the removal of the tariff overhang on semiconductor supply chains. The same AI capex narrative that drove Friday's relief rally now has a geopolitical tailwind stacked on top.

Confirms: QQQ holds above 453 through 10:30 AM ET with volume tracking above the 30-day average, confirming institutional buying into the gap rather than retail chasing.

Kill switch: QQQ fades below 448 in the first hour. Get out fast if that happens, it means smart money is selling the open into summit uncertainty.

What else I'm watching

  • Stay away from rebuilding energy exposure yet. Trim XLE and XOP into any bounce: WTI hasn't found a floor after a 4-session, nearly 12% drop from $105, and this is a structural repricing, not a one-day catalyst. Wait for Tuesday's API inventory data.
  • Don't you dare sell QQQ or QQQM on a summit-headline dip if it holds above 26,600 in the first 30 minutes. This is the same AI infrastructure trade that carried the market last Friday.
  • The trade nobody is talking about: JETS. Airlines are direct beneficiaries of $93 crude versus $105, and transatlantic demand hasn't cracked. Slow build, not a day trade.
  • Hold GLD, don't add. Gold's failure to rally as the 10-year eases below 5% is the safe-haven trade quietly admitting it has nothing left to hedge against.
  • Watch KWEB and SMH for the China summit trade: a $30 billion tariff exclusion framework is a direct tailwind for Chinese internet names and semiconductor packaging. Buy the dip if White House briefings confirm the agenda holds.
