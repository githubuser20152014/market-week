## The Brief

Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole speech as Fed Chair today, and the bond market already voted on what it expects to hear: ten-year yields climbed to 4.67% yesterday, pricing in a chair comfortable with elevated term premiums before he's said a single word. Equities didn't get the memo, closing broadly higher on Nvidia's earnings afterglow. **Gold at $4,656** is the tell nobody's talking about: up on a day yields rose and the dollar shrugged, which is not how gold is supposed to behave unless something structural just changed. Nasdaq futures are off 0.31% this morning, tech quietly sobering up from the Nvidia high and staring down Marvell's hangover, with Warsh's actual remarks still ahead of us.

Yesterday's scoreboard: **S&P 500 at 7,730.99** (+0.72%), Nasdaq +1.57%, Dow +0.20%, Russell 2000 +0.28%. **Gold at $4,656** (+1.00%), the 10-year yield up to **4.672%**, WTI slipping to $83.47, dollar index dead flat at 99.18. Two catalysts are doing the work. Nvidia's $96.2B Q2 print, Data Center revenue up 117% year-over-year, Q3 guidance of $108B, acted like jet fuel under the Nasdaq and dragged the whole [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) trade higher on fumes of relief. Then there's Warsh: preview coverage of his Jackson Hole debut frames him as comfortable with elevated term premiums and allergic to rigid forward guidance, and the bond market leaned that way before he's even at the podium. Ten-year yields at 4.67% are a bet on what he says, not a reaction to it. Add **Strait of Hormuz** corridor talks easing crude's war premium, and WTI just snapped a two-week win streak on the mere suggestion of diplomacy.

Set your entry condition off the data and the speech, not the pre-market noise.
