Oil crashed 6.1% yesterday the moment Trump paused the airstrikes on Iran. Nasdaq futures popped 1.3% on the relief.

Before you chase that open, look at who didn't celebrate.

---

Gold is the tell. It edged up 0.51% to $4,088.20 in the exact session it was supposed to sell off into a genuine risk-on catalyst. An asset that's supposed to bleed on good news instead held its ground, which means somebody big still isn't convinced the Hormuz story is closed.

Bonds barely moved either. The 10-year slipped just 3.2 basis points to 4.647% and the dollar drifted a rounding error to 101.40. If the market genuinely believed the geopolitical risk premium was gone, that's a lot more yield to give back than a few basis points. Instead, the bond market shrugged.

Crude is the one asset that fully believed the news, dropping 6.1% to $83.89 in a single session. That's the entire "relief rally" story in one number, and it's the only one of the three markets acting like the war-risk premium is fully gone.

---

The corporate proof point: options traders are pricing unusually large swings into Apple's upcoming earnings, well above the normal pre-print range. That's not what a market convinced the coast is clear looks like. That's a market hedging for a surprise, in the same week the Fed meets and Microsoft and Amazon report.

---

The angle nobody's covering: wildfires in Spain and France have forced more than 300,000 people to flee and pulled Macron into a crisis meeting. That's not a headline that stays contained to travel and insurance. Mediterranean crop losses and heat-driven agricultural stress compound quietly for weeks after the news cycle moves on, and food and insurance costs don't reprice on the same day the fire does.

---

So which market got yesterday right? Oil says the crisis is over. Gold and bonds say not so fast. They can't both be correct, and the FOMC meeting starting today, plus a wall of big tech earnings this week, is what actually settles it.
