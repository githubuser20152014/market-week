Amazon jumped 13% on an AWS beat. Apple fell 7% on a China miss. Same tape, same day, and the Nasdaq only wanted to talk about the winner.

Here's why you shouldn't trust yesterday's close as clean risk-on.

Bonds didn't buy it. The Fed held rates at 3.50%-3.75%, three members wanted a hike, and Chair Warsh dropped formal forward guidance entirely. The 10-year yield still climbed to 4.663% on the same day equities rallied. Yields rising alongside stocks isn't confirmation, it's the risk-free rate quietly getting more expensive underneath every valuation model on the desk.

Gold added another 0.4% to a fresh multi-year high near $4,116. Gold doesn't go up on conviction that everything is fine. It goes up when someone, somewhere, is hedging a scenario the equity market isn't pricing.

Crude climbed 1.35%, and it has nothing to do with global growth optimism. Iran struck U.S. assets in Kuwait and Bahrain following the Egypt drone attack, and that geopolitical premium is now sitting inside every barrel.

Exxon and Chevron just posted quarters that confirm it directly: the energy sector is printing cash off a war premium, not a demand story. That's the plainest read you'll get all week.

Here's the angle nobody's covering: the yen just strengthened sharply overnight, and that kind of move looks a lot like the early stage of a carry trade unwind. Pair that with KOSPI ripping close to 18% in a single session, an outlier that still needs an explanation, and you've got the ingredients for exactly the kind of cross-asset air pocket that turns an orderly Friday into a disorderly one.

So which market got it right yesterday, equities or bonds? Equities are pricing an AI monetization story and a Fed that's done hiking. Bonds are pricing a Fed that just removed its own guardrails and a yield curve with room to keep steepening.

I know which one I'm listening to. Bonds are rarely the market throwing a party for no reason.
