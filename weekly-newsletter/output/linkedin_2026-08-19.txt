The S&P fell 0.7% yesterday and the headlines will call it a valuation reset. Don't buy that story.

Gold just told you what's actually happening, and it's not about growth.

Here's what the dissenting markets were saying while equities sold off:

Bonds: The 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.335%, a 19-year high. That's not a rate-cut market pricing a soft landing, it's a bond market demanding more compensation to hold long-duration paper, full stop. When the discount rate moves like that, every dollar of distant tech earnings gets repriced lower.

Gold: Gold surged 1.1% to $4,415 on the same day equities fell. If this were a simple growth scare, gold would be lagging behind a flight to cash. Instead it's acting like the primary asset in the room, with buyers defending every dip.

Crude: WTI held above $85 while the US-Iran truce expired without extension, the UAE severed financial ties with Tehran, and a commercial tanker took a direct hit in the Strait of Hormuz, a corridor carrying roughly 20% of global oil supply. Crude didn't need to spike to tell you the risk premium is back. It just needed to hold.

The corporate proof point: the semiconductor complex is the tell. Nvidia, Broadcom, and Micron all compressed on the same day the 30-year punched through a 19-year high. That's the discount-rate math working exactly as advertised, the longer the duration of the earnings story, the harder the multiple gets hit when the risk-free rate moves.

The story nobody's covering: global food and ag supply chains are quietly repricing the same input-cost pressure showing up in bond markets. Deoleo, the world's largest olive oil producer, popped over 15% on takeover interest as rivals circle a commodity business suddenly worth fighting over. Inflation-adjacent assets are getting bid across categories most portfolios ignore.

So which market got it right yesterday, equities or gold? Gold. Every time growth fear and inflation fear show up together, gold prices the outcome equities are still in denial about. The bond market backs it up, 5.335% on the 30-year is not a soft-landing signal.
