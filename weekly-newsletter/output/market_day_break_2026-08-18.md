## The Brief

The dominant signal is a sovereign bond market in revolt: the 30-year Treasury just hit a 19-year high and the 10-year is pressing 4.70%, driven by the twin shock of the US-Iran ceasefire expiring and a tanker strike in the Strait of Hormuz reigniting crude inflation fears that box the Fed in. Gold is holding above $4,450 while equities sell off, a classic stagflation tell where the safe-haven bid is in hard assets, not duration. Pre-market futures confirm the pressure, with Nasdaq futures down over 1% and S&P futures pointing to another down open near 7,731.

The **S&P 500 dropped 0.52% to 7,745** and the Dow shed half a percent to 53,460, proof that a ceasefire clock beats an earnings beat every time. The Nasdaq slipped 0.32% to 26,645 while the Russell 2000 limped to a 0.51% gain, because somebody has to play designated optimist. The **10-year Treasury yield climbed 5.5 basis points to 4.696%**, and the 30-year broke 5.31%, its highest print in 19 years, bond traders' polite way of telling the Fed its rate-cut homework just got harder. **Gold surged to $4,452** while oil settled at $84.05 and the dollar index sat frozen at 99.66, proof even FX traders know a coin flip when they see one.

The causal chain is direct: the **60-day US-Iran ceasefire expired without renewal**, and within hours a commercial vessel was struck by a projectile in the **Strait of Hormuz**, the chokepoint carrying roughly 20% of global crude. Energy markets that had priced in a diplomatic extension were caught offsides, sending Brent above $91 and WTI above $87 earlier in the session before settling. Higher crude means higher headline inflation inputs, which means the Fed's path to rate cuts narrows further. Bond traders sold duration immediately, a reflex that reads higher energy as a ceiling on easing. Think of it as the market recalculating the Fed's optionality in real time: every dollar on crude is another month the Fed sits on its hands.
