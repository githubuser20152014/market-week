The Nasdaq just closed at a record high. Don't trust the rally.

Yesterday, the S&P 500 cleared 7,764, the Nasdaq ripped 2.26% to 27,122, and the Dow added 0.71%. On the surface: a broad, confident risk-on tape. Underneath: three unrelated diplomatic and economic events did the heavy lifting, not earnings, not fundamentals. That distinction matters more than the headline number.

Start with crude. WTI collapsed 4.83% to $91.15, its sharpest single-day drop in weeks, on UN General Assembly sideline diplomacy easing Middle East tensions. That's not a demand story. It's a geopolitical premium unwinding, and geopolitical premiums have a habit of reinflating the moment the diplomacy stalls.

Then bonds. The 10-year Treasury yield pulled back to 4.963% from a 5.01% high earlier in the week. The move reads as relief, but it's really the bond market pricing oil's drop as disinflationary, not pricing in any actual change in growth or Fed policy. Borrowed conviction, not earned conviction.

Then gold, which is the market that should worry you most. Gold slipped 0.73% to $4,352 at the exact moment yields fell and diplomatic optimism rose, the two conditions that usually support it. When the safe-haven asset stops behaving like a safe haven, it's telling you the "risk-off unwind" narrative is more fragile than the headline suggests.

The proof point tying it together: Industrial Production printed flat at 0.0% for the month. Not a contraction, not an expansion, just enough stagnation to keep the Fed comfortably on pause rather than reaching for another hike. That's the quiet variable letting equities run without a policy fight.

The angle nobody's covering: today's Trump-Xi summit reportedly includes critical mineral supply agreements, the input rare earth and chip supply chains have been rationing around for two years. If that language firms up, the read-through isn't just semiconductors, it's the entire downstream manufacturing base that's been holding excess inventory as a hedge against exactly this kind of shortage. Watch that space, not just the index levels.

So which market got it right yesterday: equities celebrating a clean sweep, or gold quietly signaling the celebration is premature? I'll take the market that's been right more often lately, and it isn't the one making new highs on borrowed diplomatic goodwill.
