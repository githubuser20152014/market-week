## The Brief

Amazon's AI cloud beat and Apple's China miss traded on the same tape yesterday, and the Nasdaq only remembers the winner. Bonds weren't buying it: the Fed held, Chair Warsh killed forward guidance, and the 10-year still climbed to 4.663% while stocks partied like it was risk-on. Futures point to a gap-up open, but a 17.9% overnight KOSPI moonshot and the yen ripping stronger to 160 say check your assumptions before you chase it.

U.S. equities closed sharply higher Wednesday, led by the Nasdaq's **+2.78% surge to 25,122**, with the S&P 500 gaining 1.66% to 7,437 and the Dow adding 1.19% to 52,208. The Russell 2000 rose 1.37%, WTI crude climbed 1.35% to **$84.72** on Iran-related supply risk, gold added 0.4% to $4,116, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose 4 basis points to **4.663%**, extending its multi-year high streak. The USD Index ticked up a modest 0.24% to 100.25.

The catalyst split was clean. Amazon reported blowout [AWS](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AWS) cloud numbers and jumped 13%, pulling Nasdaq higher and confirming the market's new thesis: [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) capex stories without free cash flow are out, **[AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) monetization proof is in**. Apple fell 7.2% on China Services weakness, and the Aschenbrenner-linked [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) fund Situational Awareness was forced to liquidate its concentrated [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) infrastructure book to Citadel, a textbook sign that the speculative froth in that corner of the trade is clearing. Separately, the FOMC held rates at 3.50%-3.75% with three hawk dissenters, and Chair Warsh's decision to drop formal forward guidance is now actively steepening the long end. **Bonds never bought the equity rally**: yields rising alongside stocks is a compression of the risk-free rate that will eventually bite multiples.

The **KOSPI's 17.9% single-session surge** is an outlier that demands explanation before being read as a global risk-on signal. USD/JPY breaking to 160.13 from 163.3 overnight is a significant yen strengthening move that cuts against a simple risk-on read and may reflect repatriation flows or BOJ signaling. Europe is flat at the open. Today's only scheduled data point is New Home Sales at 10 AM ET, a tier-one importance and likely a non-event unless the print is deeply surprising. The real swing factor is whether the **10-year yield holds below 4.70%**: a breach there would test how much of yesterday's equity rally was multiple expansion versus earnings-driven.
