Yesterday's selloff got filed under "risk-off day, nothing to see here." Stocks down, oil up, gold bid. Standard playbook.

Except the bond market just told you that playbook is wrong.

Here's why you shouldn't trust the equity tape on this one:

BONDS: The Treasury expanded its debt buyback facility to $6 billion specifically to hold down the 10-year yield. Primary dealers took the liquidity and kept selling anyway. The 10-year settled at 4.837%, closing in on the highest levels since late 2023. Rate futures now price an 82% probability of a hike, not a cut, at next week's FOMC. When a government intervention fails this visibly, the bond market isn't pricing a growth scare. It's pricing a policy error.

GOLD: Gold held firm even as the dollar stayed firm and real yields climbed, which is the anomaly that matters. Gold isn't supposed to rally into higher real rates. When it does anyway, that's institutional money treating gold as a currency hedge, not a fear trade, because the trade deficit math around $97 oil doesn't work for the dollar either.

CRUDE: WTI closed at $97.22, up 1.22%, after maritime war-risk insurers pulled standard coverage for Gulf transit this week following the Strait of Hormuz escalation. That's not a speculative bid. That's the physical insurance market repricing a fifth of global oil shipments as uninsurable at normal rates, which shows up in freight costs and refining margins long before it shows up in a CPI print.

The corporate data backs the supply-shock read over the demand-destruction read: TSMC posted August revenue up more than 53% year over year to a record high. If this were a genuine growth scare, the world's largest chipmaker wouldn't be printing records. Capex demand is intact. The problem is input costs and energy, not appetite.

The angle nobody's covering: those same insurance pullbacks and freight cost spikes flow straight into fertilizer and ag input pricing, since ammonia production is natural-gas intensive and shipping lanes for both energy and grain overlap more than most portfolios account for. Watch food and ag input costs over the next two quarters, not just headline crude.

So which market got it right yesterday, stocks or bonds?

Bonds. Equities are still bidding a growth scare. The bond market, gold, and the physical insurance market are all pricing the same thing: a supply-side, geopolitical inflation shock that doesn't get fixed by a rate cut. Until equities catch up to that repricing, expect more sessions like yesterday.
