## The Brief

Relief, but the selective kind. Nvidia flashed a good datacenter forecast, yields backed off six basis points from their 5.01% scare, and Nasdaq sprinted 1.7% while small caps shuffled 0.55% like they weren't invited to the party. That is not risk-on. That is mega-cap cosplay. Gold is still sitting near $4,418 despite falling yields and a firmer dollar, which means somebody in the hedging community did not get the all-clear memo. Futures want a gap higher at the open, but oil's third straight losing session and a yen that just blew through 157 are the signals actually worth listening to.

Friday's scoreboard: S&P 500 up 1.14% to **7,637**, Nasdaq up 1.69% to 26,418, because nothing says broad-based confidence like everyone piling into the same handful of stocks. The Dow limped to +0.61% and the Russell 2000 to +0.55%, the equivalent of getting invited to the rally and standing by the snack table. **Gold finished at $4,418** with a quiet 0.43% gain, refusing to budge even as yields fell, which is its way of saying it does not believe any of this. Oil cratered 5.79% to **$96.01**, the 10-year yield fell to **4.947%**, and the USD Index nudged up 0.14% to 100.36. Four assets, four different opinions about what just happened.

The catalyst chain, for anyone keeping score. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh hiked 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00% on Thursday and rattled everyone's nerves, then Nvidia showed up Friday with **an upbeat datacenter hardware outlook** and talked the [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) trade off the ledge. Yields easing six basis points off their 5.01% high gave equity multiples room to breathe again. Oil's story is uglier: Saudi Aramco quietly started running tankers around **the Sohar terminal** instead of through the usual chokepoints, the [EIA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/EIA) found a surprise 2.1-million-barrel distillate build, and just like that the Houthi risk premium that had crude flirting with $109 evaporated. Three straight red sessions for crude. Geopolitics giveth, tanker logistics taketh away.

## What it means for you

For your book: nuance, not a victory lap. [QQQ](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/QQQ) rode the Nvidia halo, but with **a 10-year yield still near 4.95%**, Warsh's higher-for-longer regime is not handing out favors to money-losing growth names. The cash-flow generators win. The speculative tier gets left holding the bag. On oil, the [XLE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLE) and [USO](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/USO) thesis just flipped: **the Saudi Aramco bypass route** is a structural supply fix, not a one-headline wonder, so anything still priced for a Houthi risk premium needs to reprice, fast. **Gold near $4,418 is the one refusing to play along.** It is not selling off despite a firmer dollar and falling yields, which usually means someone bigger than you is hedging something you have not heard about yet, likely the [BRICS](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BRICS) New Delhi Declaration's slow-motion attack on dollar clearing and the September 24 Trump-Xi summit looming over everything.

Today's setup: S&P futures up 1.04% to 7,719, Nasdaq futures up 1.45% to 29,873, a clean gap higher and a market that has apparently already forgiven Thursday. The yen just blew through **157.88**, sailing past the 156.00 line FX desks were watching for BOJ intervention, and Governor Ueda's cautious tone did nothing to stop it. South Korea's KOSPI jumped 2.66% and Japan's Nikkei added 1.38%, both cheering a weaker yen the way exporters always do, on someone else's currency pain. Europe is flat, which is Europe's version of an opinion. The number that actually matters today is the **10:00 AM ET New Home Sales** print: at a 3.75%-4.00% fed funds rate and a 10-year near 5%, this is the test of whether the hike is already biting housing. Miss it and rate-sensitive sectors reprice fast. Oh, and Buffett announced he is stepping down as Berkshire's chairman, because apparently even Father Time reads the macro calendar.
