$40.1 Trillion in Debt. 75% Odds of a Hold. The S&P Closed at 7,722.72 Anyway.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The economy added 29,000 jobs. Wall Street expected 85,000 to 90,000. Stocks rallied anyway.

The S&P 500 gained 0.73% to 7,722.72. The Nasdaq jumped 1.19%. The Russell 2000 added 0.94%. Everything went up. Nobody asked why.

The logic is bad news means no rate cut means... a party? Futures now price 75-80% odds of a hold at the October 27-28 FOMC. Equities heard "Fed pause" and celebrated like it was a rate cut. It is not a rate cut. It is a Fed that has run out of excuses.

Here's my read:

The bond market read the same jobs number and shrugged. The 10-year yield climbed 4 basis points to 5.277% while stocks popped. That is the bond market politely declining to join the party. It still wants paying for a $40.1 trillion debt load and $739 billion in quarterly issuance. Goldman's Gutman is on the tape this morning begging governments to cut spending. Good luck with that.

Gold fell 0.90% to $4,162.30, then bounced to $4,196 in futures the second the US session closed. That is not a coincidence. That is a bid. It's what a hedge looks like when nobody believes the celebration.

Today's swing factor: Industrial Production at 9:15 AM ET. Watch the 10-year. Above 5.30%, equity bulls get a headwind and gold accelerates. Below 5.25%, growth names get a second wind and everyone pretends yields were never a problem. S&P futures are down 0.09% to 7,770.25. The easy money already left the building.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

Gold dropped 0.90% during the US session and immediately recovered to $4,196 in overnight futures. That is buyers defending the floor against surging sovereign debt, active geopolitical escalation, and a Fed on hold instead of cutting. The asymmetry is the whole point.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,170 through the 10:00 AM ET hour and the 10-year stays below 5.30%. The overnight futures premium holding into the cash open is the first sign.

Kill switch: The 10-year drops below 5.20% on a surprise soft Industrial Production print, triggering a risk-on rotation that pushes gold back under $4,150. If that happens, get out fast.

  • XLE: Hold it, don't add. WTI is stuck near $90.67 with the IEA's SPR release acting as a ceiling and OPEC+ acting as a floor. Stuck is not a trade. Above $92 on fresh Bab al-Mandeb headlines, it becomes a buy.
