## The Brief

WTI just blew through $84 and gold is sitting near record highs while the S&P closed red: that's not a market shrugging off risk, that's a market hedging two different disasters at once. The Hormuz talks collapsed, the SPR is at a 1983 low, and July CPI lands this morning to decide which fear wins. Futures are barely green, which means Wall Street isn't betting on a clean print, it's just too scared to bet against one.

Equities barely moved, which is the whole point. The S&P 500 slipped to **7,753.11 (down 0.06%)**, the Dow dropped 0.11% to 53,975.98, the Nasdaq fell 0.32% to 26,605.36, and the Russell 2000 led the retreat at **down 0.56%** to 3,017.40, small-caps flinching first because they always do when rates so much as twitch. The real story was everywhere else: **WTI crude surged 2.56% to $84.23**, ripping clean through the $78-$82 range that had held for weeks, **gold climbed 1.58% to $4,430.80** for no reason a calm market would need, and the 10-year Treasury yield ticked up to **4.699%**, up 3.9 basis points, while the dollar index sat frozen at 99.87 like it forgot how to participate.

Blame Hormuz first. The Strait of Hormuz talks between Iran and Oman collapsed into impasse, US resistance killed the transit fee framework, and traders priced in prolonged rerouting risk and fatter war-risk insurance premiums on every barrel moving through the Gulf. Then blame the data. Nonfarm payrolls contracted 23,000 in July, yet the 10-year yield is climbing anyway, a split that means the bond market doesn't believe inflation is beaten. Think of it like a patient whose fever is coming down but whose blood pressure is spiking: something is still very wrong. The July FOMC vote split 9-3, with three hawks pushing for a hike, so don't expect the Fed to calm anyone down before CPI prints.

Japan's Nikkei ripped +2.08% on Nvidia's $500 billion financing announcement and the [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) capex bid, while Hang Seng dropped 1.10% because China's [WTO](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/WTO) tariff challenge and real estate strain don't care about anyone else's rally. **Today's entire swing factor is the July CPI print**: economists forecast 3.4% headline YoY. Above 3.5% and the board resets; at or below 3.3% and the bulls get their green light. No Fed speakers today, so the data gets to talk without anyone talking over it.
