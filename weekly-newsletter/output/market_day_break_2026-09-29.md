## The Brief

Late-cycle compression, live and in color: the 10-year at 5.24% and a Shiller [CAPE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CAPE) parked north of 40 are squeezing equity multiples from both ends, and desks are de-risking into quarter-end like it's a fire drill. The White House told Iran's Hormuz ceasefire offer to get lost, tanker traffic is down 85%, and WTI is holding above $92 anyway, because nothing says risk-off like crude that refuses to cooperate. Futures are comatose ahead of the open, so today's PCE print from the BEA is doing all the heavy lifting.

Equities slid across the board Tuesday as the **10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.24%**, up 5.6 basis points and now trading at its highest sustained level since 2007, because apparently bonds decided 2007 wants its yields back. The S&P 500 fell 0.77% to **7,683.69**, the Nasdaq dropped 0.92% to 26,820, the Dow shed 245 points to 51,481, and the Russell 2000 lost 0.69% to 2,817, a broad retreat with no hero sector to hide in. Gold edged up 0.27% to **$4,179.60**, WTI closed at $92.31 after flirting with $94.60 intraday, and the dollar firmed to 101.42, so everyone found somewhere to hide except the people who own bonds.

Two things did the damage. Regional Fed presidents reaffirmed higher-for-longer, and swap markets now price a **68% probability of a 25-basis-point hike before year-end**, which is the bond market politely informing a 40-[CAPE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CAPE) equity portfolio that its rent is due. Then the **White House rejection of Iran's 7-day Strait of Hormuz ceasefire offer** hit the tape, tanker traffic collapsed to under 15% of normal, and crude's energy-inflation premium came roaring back, because nothing keeps oil bid like a blockade nobody actually wants to end.

## What it means for you

Positioning-wise, 5.24% on the 10-year is the market screaming to stay short duration. [TLT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TLT) and [EDV](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/EDV) remain stuck in a structural headwind until the Fed blinks or the data breaks first, and neither looks close. The Hormuz standoff, meanwhile, is a gift with a bow on it for North American upstream and pipeline names, **the energy trade is back on the table** with [XLE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLE) and [AMLP](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMLP) as the direct expressions. Gold rising while the dollar also firms is the tell: institutions are treating [GLD](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GLD) as a geopolitical hedge, not a rate play. High-multiple growth and rate-sensitive sectors like software and utilities ([XLRE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLRE), [XLU](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLU)) are the ones paying the toll as risk-free yields keep undercutting their multiples.

Futures are doing their best impression of a flatline into the open: S&P futures at 7,743 are down a rounding error of 0.04%, Nasdaq futures are barely green at 30,595. APAC went uniformly red overnight, Nikkei and Hang Seng both down roughly half a percent, dutifully echoing Wall Street's mood, while Europe shrugged and opened flat. The entire session hinges on the **[BEA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BEA)'s revised PCE price index release**, which recalibrates October FOMC odds in real time. A hot read above 2.8% core year-over-year sends yields higher and squeezes equities further. A soft read below 2.5% could spark a relief rally in rate-sensitive names, though the geopolitical oil premium caps how far that rally can run.
