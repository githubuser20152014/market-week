The Strait of Hormuz Is Down to 2 Ships a Window. Oil Only Found 3%.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The Strait of Hormuz is functionally shut. Maritime insurers won't touch it, commercial transit has collapsed to as few as two vessels a window, and CENTCOM hit three Iranian tankers over the weekend. Tehran responded by declaring the end of proportionate responses, which is a diplomatic way of promising the opposite.

And oil went up 3%. Not 30%. A polite $94.25, like the market read the headline and decided to wait for confirmation before it panics.

That's not calm. That's a market that hasn't finished pricing the worst case yet.

Here's my read:

Friday's close was ugly everywhere that matters (markets were shut Monday for Labor Day). The S&P 500 dropped 0.38% to 7,718, the Dow shed 0.51%, the Nasdaq slipped 0.29%, and small caps barely held ground. Meanwhile gold added 0.26% to $4,441 and refused to give it back, the 10-year yield climbed to 4.784%, and the yen ripped through 154 as carry trades unwound overnight. Friday's payrolls print of 162,000 jobs was already talking the Fed out of a cut. Now crude at $94 is doing the same thing from the inflation side. The Fed just got handed a stagflation two-by-four, and it's holding it with both hands.

The One Trade: Long $XLE

The Strait of Hormuz isn't closed for a news cycle, it's closed structurally, and the energy sector hasn't finished repricing that. WTI held $94 overnight with zero diplomatic off-ramp in sight, and the geopolitical premium hasn't even reflected the possibility of a formal exclusion zone out of Tehran yet.

Confirms: WTI holding above $93 by 10:30 AM ET with XLE trading above Friday's close, and energy showing relative strength against the S&P through the first hour.

Kill switch: A confirmed US Navy escort announcement for commercial tankers through Hormuz, or any Tehran back-channel signaling de-escalation. Either one drops WTI $3-4 immediately, and the trade needs to be gone fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Gold ($GLD): hold it, don't you dare sell it here. $4,441 refusing to sell off on a risk-off equities day is the tell that this is a safe-haven bid stacked on top of the inflation hedge, not just one or the other.
  • Long bonds ($TLT): stay away from it. Yields rose while equities sold off, which means bonds never bought Friday's move in the first place. Park cash in $SGOV or $BIL instead.
  • Defense ($ITA): the trade nobody is talking about. The Leipzig drone attack and an emergency NATO hybrid-warfare task force convening in Brussels today is a live catalyst for European defense spending that flows straight into US contractors.
  • Discretionary ($XLY): avoid it. $94 crude is a consumption tax, and if WTI pushes toward $98-100 before Friday's CPI print, retail and leisure margins take the hit first.
