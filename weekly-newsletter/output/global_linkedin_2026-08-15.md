The U.S. Navy just launched an indefinite blockade of Iranian port traffic in the Strait of Hormuz, after drone strikes hit two UAE tankers. Oil jumped 5% on the news. The S&P 500 crossed 7,800 for the first time in the same week. Two markets, two very different reads on how dangerous this actually is.

**The dissenting markets:**

Bonds weren't buying the "clean data" narrative. The 30-year Treasury yield closed at 5.26%, its highest level since 2001, on soft demand at the weekly refunding auction. If July's cooperative CPI print had actually settled the inflation question, the long end would have rallied, not sold off to a 25-year high.

Gold was the quietest asset of the week, and that's the tell. It added just 1.02% to $4,380 despite a fiscal repricing story on the long end AND a live geopolitical blockade unfolding simultaneously. An asset built to hedge exactly those two risks barely moved.

Crude was the one market pricing the Hormuz risk honestly. WTI surged 5.1% to $82.40, absorbing a 17.4 million barrel inventory build along the way, proof that supply fear, not demand strength, is driving the move.

**The corporate proof point:**

AI infrastructure earnings did the heavy lifting behind the S&P's record close. Two names in the space each surged 19% on their results this week, while the broader Dow actually slipped 0.63%. This was a narrow rally wearing a broad-market headline.

**The second-order story nobody's covering:**

The Hang Seng fell 2.67% this week, and the headline excuse was Typhoon Dolphin disrupting Eastern Chinese manufacturing. The real story sitting underneath it is Beijing's WTO dispute filing against U.S. industrial tariffs, an unresolved trade fight getting a fraction of the attention a naval blockade is getting, despite arguably longer-lasting consequences for global supply chains.

**The verdict:**

Equities are pricing safety. Oil is pricing danger. Gold, the one asset supposed to hedge both a fiscal crisis and a geopolitical one at the same time, moved almost nothing. Which market has this right?
