Oil fell 3.12% on a phone call. Here's why you shouldn't trust it.

Brent was above $110 on Tuesday. Then Steve Witkoff flew to St. Petersburg, a 60-day Russian diesel sanctions waiver landed, and the Hormuz risk premium vanished in one session. Republicans are already threatening to block it. A rally built on diplomacy has the shelf life of milk.

The dissenting markets are the ones to watch.

Bonds: the 10-year closed at 5.117%. It barely moved while the Dow added 530 points. A yield above 5% does not care how your earnings season is going, and it is quietly strangling everything that needs a loan.

Gold: $4,155, holding its floor with real yields above 5%. That's not a trade. That's central banks buying like they know something you don't.

Crude: down to $98.61, but the market is pricing a ceasefire headline, not a fix. One angry committee hearing and the premium is back.

The corporate proof point: GE Aerospace, 3M and Northrop Grumman all beat, and the Dow and Russell 2000 (+1.65%) celebrated. The Nasdaq finished down 0.02% because Netflix cratered after hours on weak guidance. Industrial America is getting paid. Tech is waiting for permission.

The second-order story nobody is covering: housing. Homebuilder confidence just hit its lowest since 2012 and starts fell 5.2% in a month. A 10-year pinned above 5% works like a mortgage freezer. The French 10-year also touched 5% for the first time since 2002, so this isn't just an American problem.

Meanwhile KOSPI jumped 3.29% overnight on Samsung and SK Hynix. AI hardware demand is real. Apparently Seoul got the memo and Main Street didn't.

Watch the 10-year today. If it slides, everyone exhales. If it climbs, the equity crowd gets a reminder of who actually sets the price of money.

So which market got it right? Equities say the economy is fine. Bonds say money is expensive. Gold says nobody trusts either. I'm betting on the market that doesn't need a press release to move.
