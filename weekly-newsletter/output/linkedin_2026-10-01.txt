Micron's data center revenue grew 11-fold. The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.29% anyway.

Happy new quarter. Futures are green. Here is why you should not trust the color.

Bonds said no. The 10-year is at its highest level since 2002, and the 30-year sits at 5.67%. This is not a Fed story. It is a supply story: $40.1 trillion of US debt and $739 billion of Q3 issuance. When the bond market wants paying, one great earnings print does not change the invoice.

Gold said hold on. It finished at $4,205 against a stronger dollar and rising yields. That combination is supposed to hurt gold. It did not. Somebody is buying something other than the story equities are telling.

Crude said inflation is not done. WTI rose to $91.31 after Chinese refiners reportedly banned October fuel exports. Brent is flirting with $100. Add August PCE at +0.9% month over month and a GDP revision to 2.2% from 2.5%, and you get slow car, hot engine. The Fed's October hike is still on the table.

The proof point is Micron. Data center revenue up 11-fold, guidance beat across the board. The Nikkei ripped 3.3% overnight on chip demand. The AI buildout is real. But the Dow just snapped a five-month winning streak, the Russell 2000 lost another 0.39%, and the ASX 200 dropped 1.99%. One sector is celebrating. Everyone else is paying the rate bill.

The second-order story nobody is covering: mortgage rates above 7.85%. Long yields do not just hit bond funds. They hit anyone who needs to borrow for a house, a building, or a business. That is where a 5.29% 10-year stops being a market headline and starts being a kitchen-table headline.

So which market got it right? Equities say growth is fine. Bonds, gold, and crude say the price of money is going up and the price of everything else is not coming down. I'll take the market that is paid to be pessimistic. Futures are a vote. The 10-year is a bill.
