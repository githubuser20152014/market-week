Stocks fell Monday and gold ripped 2%. If you think that's a contradiction, you're reading it wrong. It's a warning.

Three regional Fed presidents just publicly broke with their own chair, wanting a rate hike while July payrolls contracted by 23,000. That's not a split, that's the Fed admitting it doesn't have a clean answer. The bond market noticed before equities did.

Here's why you shouldn't trust yesterday's tape:

Bonds: The 10-year yield sat at 4.684% while stocks sold off, essentially unchanged. That's bonds saying the bad news was already priced in weeks ago. No panic, no relief, just conviction.

Gold: Up 2.05% to $4,473 in a single session. That's not a diversification trade, that's institutional money hedging a scenario where the Fed tightens into a weakening labor market and loses on both mandates at once.

Crude: Slipped even with the Strait of Hormuz still disrupted and the IEA flagging demand destruction. Oil is pricing the growth scare harder than the inflation scare, which tells you where the smart money actually thinks the economy is headed.

The corporate proof point: CoreWeave's stock popped 14% Monday as revenue doubled on accelerating AI infrastructure demand. Capital spending on AI infrastructure is still the one corner of this economy that hasn't gotten the slowdown memo, and it's papering over a lot of cracks underneath.

The second-order story nobody's covering: Hormuz-driven reroutes around the Cape of Good Hope are quietly pushing up tanker insurance premiums and transit times right now. That's a cost that shows up in import-heavy company margins months from now, long after everyone stops talking about a chokepoint in the Middle East.

So which market got it right? Equities, betting the Fed can thread the needle between a cooling labor market and sticky inflation? Or bonds and gold, betting it can't do both?
