The Morning Brief - 2026-07-31

Daily Edition · Market intelligence at the open

The Brief

Amazon's AI cloud beat and Apple's China miss traded on the same tape yesterday, and the Nasdaq only remembers the winner. Bonds weren't buying it: the Fed held, Chair Warsh killed forward guidance, and the 10-year still climbed to 4.663% while stocks partied like it was risk-on. Futures point to a gap-up open, but a 17.9% overnight KOSPI moonshot and the yen ripping stronger to 160 say check your assumptions before you chase it.

U.S. equities closed sharply higher Wednesday, led by the Nasdaq's +2.78% surge to 25,122, with the S&P 500 gaining 1.66% to 7,437 and the Dow adding 1.19% to 52,208. The Russell 2000 rose 1.37%, WTI crude climbed 1.35% to $84.72 on Iran-related supply risk, gold added 0.4% to $4,116, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose 4 basis points to 4.663%, extending its multi-year high streak. The USD Index ticked up a modest 0.24% to 100.25.

The catalyst split was clean. Amazon reported blowout AWS cloud numbers and jumped 13%, pulling Nasdaq higher and confirming the market's new thesis: AI capex stories without free cash flow are out, AI monetization proof is in. Apple fell 7.2% on China Services weakness, and the Aschenbrenner-linked AI fund Situational Awareness was forced to liquidate its concentrated AI infrastructure book to Citadel, a textbook sign that the speculative froth in that corner of the trade is clearing. Separately, the FOMC held rates at 3.50%-3.75% with three hawk dissenters, and Chair Warsh's decision to drop formal forward guidance is now actively steepening the long end. Bonds never bought the equity rally: yields rising alongside stocks is a compression of the risk-free rate that will eventually bite multiples.

What it means for you

For you, the earnings divergence demands a clean surgical response. Amazon's print validates cloud infrastructure ETFs (SKYY, CLOU) and selective mega-cap tech (QQQ), but Apple's miss is a reminder that China exposure is still a live grenade inside any broad consumer tech position. The oil move to $84.72, driven by Iran targeting U.S. assets in Kuwait and Bahrain following the Egypt drone attack, keeps energy names relevant (XLE, XOP) even as the Cairo disarmament framework offers a possible ceiling on crude upside. Rising 10-year yields at 4.663% continue to pressure long-duration bonds (TLT is a fade, not a buy) and should support short-duration or floating-rate instruments (FLOT, USFR). The Aschenbrenner liquidation is a data point: forced selling in AI infrastructure names may not be finished.

Futures are constructive: S&P futures at 7,502 imply a +0.9% gap-up, Nasdaq futures at 28,565 are +1.16%, and Dow futures add 0.51%. The KOSPI's 17.9% single-session surge is an outlier that demands explanation before being read as a global risk-on signal. USD/JPY breaking to 160.13 from 163.3 overnight is a significant yen strengthening move that cuts against a simple risk-on read and may reflect repatriation flows or BOJ signaling. Europe is flat at the open. Today's only scheduled data point is New Home Sales at 10 AM ET, a tier-one importance and likely a non-event unless the print is deeply surprising. The real swing factor is whether the 10-year yield holds below 4.70%: a breach there would test how much of yesterday's equity rally was multiple expansion versus earnings-driven.

The One Trade

XLE - Long

Iran's strikes on U.S. assets in Kuwait and Bahrain are keeping a geopolitical premium in crude at $84.72, and Exxon/Chevron earnings just confirmed the energy sector is printing cash at these prices. The Cairo disarmament deal is a ceiling risk, not a floor collapse.

Confirms: WTI holds above $83.50 through the 10 AM ET New Home Sales print and XLE trades above yesterday's close on above-average volume in the first 30 minutes. Kill switch: WTI crude breaks below $82 on any concrete progress signal from the Cairo disarmament working group meeting over the weekend, compressing the geopolitical risk premium that is the entire thesis.

Positioning Notes

  • Long QQQ selectively into the open: Amazon's AWS beat and the pivot to FCF-generative AI names is a tradeable theme for at least a session, but trim quickly if Apple's drag widens and pulls the equal-weight index (RSP) lower, signaling breadth failure underneath the mega-cap move.
  • Avoid TLT and any long-duration bond exposure: Chair Warsh dropped forward guidance, three FOMC members wanted a hike, core PCE is at 3.7%, and the 10-year at 4.663% has room to push toward 4.70%-4.80%. Yield compression is the enemy of bond prices right now. If you need fixed income, rotate into FLOT or USFR instead.
  • Hold XLE and XOP but set a tight ceiling: WTI at $84.72 is fueled by Iran's strikes on U.S. assets in Kuwait and Bahrain, and Exxon/Chevron profits confirm the earnings story. But the U.S.-brokered Cairo disarmament framework is a real geopolitical risk-premium release valve. If crude breaks back below $83, the energy trade loses its catalyst.
  • Fade AAPL-heavy positions and trim any ETF with outsized Apple weight (XLK, VGT): the China Services miss is structural, not a one-quarter noise event. Apple's weight in these ETFs means the drag is real and potentially sustained if China revenue doesn't recover.
  • Watch KOSPI-linked exposure (EWY) with extreme caution: a 17.9% single-session move in a major index is either a data anomaly or a circuit-breaker/halt situation resolving. Do not chase. Confirm the print is valid and understand the catalyst before adding any Korean equity exposure.
