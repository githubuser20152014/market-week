Payrolls Missed by 56,000. The Nasdaq Hit a Record. Oil Never Left $100.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Friday's jobs report was 29,000. Wall Street wanted 85,000. The Nasdaq responded by closing at a record 27,477, up 1.05%.

That is not a rally. That is a market that read "the economy is stalling" and heard "the Fed will let us keep the party going." Fed-pause odds are now above 78% for October 27-28. Bad news is good news again. Congratulations to everyone involved.

Meanwhile the Dow added 0.18% because the rally is a mega-cap tech party and the rest of the index wasn't on the list. The jobs market is limping and the stock market is doing victory laps.

Here's my read:

The bond market did not get the memo. The 10-year yield rose 3.4 basis points to 5.31%, a level that makes 30x earnings multiples look like a dare. The dollar index rose 0.28% to 102.18. Gold slipped 0.13% to $4,156.80. Everyone with a tighter-policy view got a little richer. Everyone holding the record-high Nasdaq got a little more exposed.

Then there is the line item nobody on TV wants to read out loud. ISM Services Prices spiked to 74.0, the highest since July 2022. Labor is cooling. Services inflation is not. One foot on the gas, one on the brake, and the equity market is only watching the gas pedal.

And oil. The G7 released reserves. Middle East shipments are rising. Brent is still sitting at $100, with Houthi attacks keeping traders on edge. That is not a market pricing relief. It is a market that heard "supply is easing" and said "sure, but I'm staying right here."

The One Trade: LONG $XLE

Oil refuses to leave $100 despite a G7 reserve release and rising Middle East shipments, while ISM Services Prices at 74.0 says the inflation behind the Fed pause is still running. If the pause is wrong, energy is the hedge.

Confirms: Brent holds above $100 through the U.S. open and XLE outperforms the S&P 500 on the day.

Kill switch: Brent settles below $97. That means the reserve release and the export recovery finally won, and you should get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Hold QQQ, but tighten stops near 27,200 on the Nasdaq cash. The record close is constructive. The 74.0 on services prices is a problem that arrives later and invoices you in full.
  • Stay away from TLT and long duration (EDV, ZROZ) until the 10-year breaks below 5.20%. The bond market is pricing sticky inflation correctly. Don't argue with it.
  • Keep an eye on MLPA and AMLP. Midstream gets paid when crude sits at $100 and tankers take the long way around. Lower drama than pure upstream.
  • Trim XTN. Airlines and trucking pay for $100 Brent in jet fuel and diesel. The trade nobody is talking about is how many transports are running on hope.
