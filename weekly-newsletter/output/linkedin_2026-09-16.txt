Everyone's celebrating the futures rally this morning. Here's why you shouldn't trust it.

Stocks sold off yesterday going into today's Fed decision. Twelve hours later, futures are up more than a percent like the sell-off never happened. That's not conviction. That's positioning ahead of a coin flip, and the rest of the market isn't buying it.

Start with bonds. The 10-year yield is parked at 4.996%, a hair under a level this market hasn't touched since 2007. Swap markets are pricing a 92% chance of a rate hike this afternoon after August's CPI ran hot. Bonds have refused to rally with equities for a month straight, and yesterday they went further: yields held near 5% even as stocks fell. That is not a market betting the Fed is almost done.

Then there's gold. It closed up 1.25% at $4,387 yesterday, rising at the same time as a steady dollar and a 5% yield. Historically, gold does not do that. Gold rises when the dollar weakens or when real yields fall. Neither happened. What's left is a genuine safe-haven bid, and the timing lines up with escalating tension in the Persian Gulf, not with anything monetary.

And crude gave the market a one-day gift it probably shouldn't trust. Oil fell 2.11% on a surprise inventory build and news that Saudi Aramco's pipeline bypass testing is holding up. That eased the immediate fear of a supply shock. It did not resolve the underlying risk: a Houthi drone was reportedly aimed at Mecca this week, and the Strait of Hormuz remains the tripwire nobody has priced correctly. One clean data point on inventories bought the market a day, not a de-escalation.

The angle nobody is talking about here is insurance, not oil price. Every time a drone gets close to a holy site or a pipeline needs bypass testing, tanker war-risk premiums move before crude prices do. That is the leading indicator worth watching this week, and it does not show up in the futures curve.

So which market got it right yesterday: equities, betting the storm passes, or bonds and gold, betting it doesn't? Bonds and gold have been right for a month. I am not betting against that streak into a Fed decision with a 92% priced hike and CPI still running hot.
