The Nasdaq rallied 1.18% on Tuesday. The 10-year Treasury sat at 4.993%, one rounding error from 5%.

Stocks are celebrating at a yield that used to cause panic. Here is why I would not trust the party.

Start with bonds. A near-5% 10-year is not a rounding error for a market priced on long-duration growth. It is the market telling you the price of money is high and may stay there. Equities heard that and bought anyway.

Then gold. It slipped 0.25% to $4,064 and still refuses to leave $4,000. Nobody holds gold through a 5% yield out of boredom. That is a hedge being held on purpose.

Then crude. WTI closed at $101.68, up 1.78%, after a Houthi missile claim against Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery wiped out the diplomacy discount. Oil is not celebrating. Oil is pricing a problem.

The corporate proof point came from Seoul. SK Hynix posted record quarterly earnings and KOSPI exploded 6.63% to 6,012. That is the AI hardware trade doing exactly what it says on the label. Korea's exchange triggered a buy-side sidecar for the second straight session, which is the market equivalent of someone grabbing the steering wheel. The Dow, for comparison, gained 0.03%. It also explains the Nasdaq move: chips up, everything else politely follows.

The second-order story is oil feeding inflation feeding the Fed. Today's 2:00 PM decision is widely expected to be a hold. The tone is the event. If Powell talks about energy-driven inflation, the rate path for 2027 gets harder to defend, and a Nasdaq priced for cuts has to explain itself. Alphabet and Microsoft report after the close, and cloud margins will matter more than capex headlines.

So which market got it right? Stocks say the AI cycle outweighs a 5% yield. Bonds, gold and crude say inflation is not finished. One of them is wrong, and I know which one tends to be right more often when the Fed is in the room.
