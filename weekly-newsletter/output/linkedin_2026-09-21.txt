Oil crashed 6.77% overnight on a Trump-Xi summit that hasn't happened yet. Equity futures are up across the board. Here's why you shouldn't trust either move at face value.

Bonds: The 10-year eased 4 basis points to 4.957%, pulling back from this week's 5.01% scare. That's not conviction, that's relief. Bond markets aren't pricing a growth acceleration, they're pricing one less geopolitical tail risk.

Gold: Gold fell 0.78% to $4,390.30 and is barely moving even as yields ease, exactly the setup where a safe-haven asset should catch a bid. It isn't. Gold is quietly telling you the fear trade that's supported it all year just lost its biggest catalyst, at least for now.

Crude: WTI's 6.77% collapse to $93.51 is the loudest signal in the market, and the most fragile. It's the fourth straight down session on a summit that's confirmed for September 23-25, not concluded. Diplomacy can un-happen in a single tweet.

Corporate proof point: Volkswagen's latest profit warning, and its resulting exit from a blue-chip index, makes the macro thesis concrete. Tariffs, fuel costs, and higher rates are squeezing companies that don't have pricing power. The relief rally in equities is real for mega-cap tech. It is not universal.

The angle nobody's covering: Saudi Aramco just expanded its Red Sea bypass capacity, permanently trimming a chunk of the geopolitical risk premium that's been baked into shipping and energy logistics for years. That's a structural change to how oil moves, not a one-week diplomatic mood swing. It matters more than the summit headline does.

So which market got it right? Equities, pricing a clean relief rally? Or gold and bonds, pricing a cooling of fear rather than an actual growth story? The gap between a risk-off unwind and a real economic upgrade is the trade that matters this week, and right now, bonds and gold are the ones being honest about it.
