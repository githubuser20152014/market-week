Wall Street decided one reopened pipeline erases a war premium that took months to build. That's not conviction. That's relief with a short memory, and relief trades don't usually last past the second data point.

Here's why I'm not fully buying yesterday's tape:

Treasuries only moved 3.5 basis points. If oil's collapse were a genuine, structural growth-and-inflation story, bonds would have rallied harder than that. A 3.5bp move is a market hedging its bets, not endorsing a new regime.

Gold fell just half a percent. That's a token retreat, not a capitulation. Real conviction that the geopolitical risk is gone looks like gold giving back 3-4%, not 0.5%. The safe-haven bid didn't leave the building, it just stepped into the hallway.

And the dollar firmed on a day everyone was supposedly celebrating a diplomatic breakthrough. Risk-on rallies usually come with a softer dollar. A firmer greenback into a "peace dividend" narrative is the market quietly hedging the other side of the trade.

The corporate data point worth watching: an AI-focused defense drone maker just hit a $6.4 billion valuation this week, part of a wave of European defense-tech raises tied to the region's rearmament push. That's capital voting for a world where the geopolitical premium doesn't actually go to zero, even as equities price yesterday's pipeline news as the all-clear.

The angle nobody's covering: the Trump administration is examining a diesel export ban, floated by lawmakers because high diesel prices are hammering farmers and truckers ahead of the November midterms. That's the real economy still absorbing energy costs that Wall Street's screens say are old news. Crude at multi-week lows on the tape and diesel policy panic in Washington are describing two different economies, and only one of them votes in November.

So which market got it right yesterday? The Nasdaq chasing a record on Xi's arrival and a tariff truce thesis, or the bond and gold markets that barely moved? I'd bet on the ones that didn't throw a party. Rallies built on a single data point erasing months of risk pricing tend to need a second data point fast, and today's Trump-Xi summit is exactly that test.
