Super Micro and CoreWeave Surged 19%. Cerebras Fell 14%. The AI Trade Just Got Selective.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The AI trade stopped being one trade yesterday. Super Micro Computer and CoreWeave each ripped 19%, Nvidia tacked on another 3%, and somewhere in the same 24 hours Cerebras cratered 14% in its first weeks as a public company. Same sector, same earnings season, wildly different verdicts.

Meanwhile gold quietly climbed to $4,440 on a softening dollar, the S&P 500 closed at 7,748.50, and the Dow lost 21 points because nobody invited industrials to this party. July CPI landed exactly where consensus expected: 0.1% month-over-month, 3.4% year-over-year. Boring, on paper. The market's reaction to it was anything but uniform.

Four different assets, four different opinions about the same data point. That's not consensus, that's a coin flip with extra steps.

Here's my read:

Think of the Fed as a car with one foot still on the brake. The CPI print didn't lift that foot, it just confirmed nobody's pressing harder. Equities took that as permission to rally: the S&P added 0.26% to 7,748.50, the Nasdaq gained 0.54% to 26,588.49, and the Russell 2000 finally showed a pulse, up 0.61%. But the 10-year yield barely budged at 4.682%, and that's the tell. Bonds have heard the rate-cut story before. They're not buying a ticket this time.

Gold is the honest broker here. It climbed 0.71% to $4,440 on a softening dollar (USD Index down to 99.90) and a Hormuz standoff that's kept shipping traffic near three-month lows. Nothing says confidence in the soft landing like everyone quietly buying the hedge anyway. Oil didn't get the geopolitical memo either: WTI fell 1.39% to $82.11 after a 17.4-million-barrel inventory build steamrolled the Strait of Hormuz risk premium. A glut beat a geopolitical crisis. That's the market's real scoreboard today, not the headlines.

The One Trade: Long $GLD

Gold is bid at $4,440 with the dollar softening, Hormuz traffic at three-month lows, and the bond market refusing to fully price the Fed easing story. Three independent reasons buyers are defending this floor regardless of what equities do today.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,420 through the 8:30 ET Durable Goods release and keeps diverging from any dollar bounce attempt. A move toward $4,460 in the first hour confirms the bid is institutional, not retail noise.

Kill switch: USD Index reclaims 100.50 on a strong Durable Goods print and GLD breaks below $4,400 on volume. If that happens, the dollar-softening thesis is dead and you need to get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • QQQ / SOXX: the AI trade is still bid, but it just got a lot more selective after Cerebras. Hold what you've got, don't chase. KOSPI's 3.56% overnight surge says Korean chipmakers are still riding the wave, but not every AI ticker gets to.
  • TLT: don't you dare add aggressively here. A Durable Goods miss below consensus is the entry trigger, nothing else counts yet.
  • XLE: stay away from new longs. The inventory glut is beating the Hormuz premium, and crude needs to stabilize above $83 or a fresh escalation before that math changes.
  • Durable Goods at 8:30 ET: the trade nobody's talking about is how much a single print is about to decide across four asset classes at once.
