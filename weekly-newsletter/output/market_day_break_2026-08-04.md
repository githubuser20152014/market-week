*Daily Edition · Market intelligence at the open*

## The Brief

Wall Street decided a pause is the same thing as peace. Trump paused planned strikes on Iran, Omani diplomats started chatting about a Strait of Hormuz shipping corridor, and that was apparently enough: not a deal, not a treaty, a pause and some small talk in Muscat. The **Dow closed at a record 53,178**, the **S&P 500 jumped 1.5% to 7,600.50**, and the **Nasdaq ripped 2.1% to 25,913.90**. Hope, it turns out, trades at a premium.

Gold didn't get the memo. **Gold rose 1.4% to $4,106** on the same day equities partied and the dollar firmed, which is not how a risk-on day is supposed to script itself unless the market secretly doesn't trust its own rally. The 10-year yield agrees with gold, not stocks: it added 8.2 bps to **4.745%**, because bond traders are professionally immune to good vibes. **WTI crude crashed 3.4% to $81.75**, cashing out five months of war premium in a single session, and the **Russell 2000 slipped 0.5%**, the only major index that missed the party invite.

The whole rally is built on a pause and a diplomatic chat, which is a thin foundation to hang a record close on. Palantir jumped 16% on "otherworldly" commercial [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) revenue, strong enough that CEO Alex Karp used the earnings call to accuse rival AI labs of trying to "drug addict" the world - a hell of a line two hours after your own stock hits a new high. Snap beat too, up 8%. BP's profit doubled hours after Trump publicly complained Big Oil is "making too much money." Nothing says free markets like getting scolded for a good quarter. Meanwhile the Fed's plan to cut FOMC meetings from eight to six a year under Chair Kevin Warsh barely registered, which is its own kind of tell: nobody has the bandwidth to care about central bank plumbing when Iran and Oman are the headline act.
