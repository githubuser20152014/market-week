## The Brief

Tesla cut capex. Lam Research guided soft. Meta asked Wall Street to trust its AI spending, and Wall Street asked for a receipt. Nasdaq futures are down 1.7%, handing back Wednesday's 1.05% gain before the coffee finished brewing.

Wednesday's close was a victory lap taken one hour too early. The **S&P 500 gained 0.58% to 7,757**, the **Nasdaq added 1.05% to 26,703**, and the Dow rose 0.69% to 52,856. Then the earnings dropped. **WTI crude surged 2.38% to $99.46**, because the Gulf did not get the memo about calm. The 10-year yield ticked up 2.9 bps to **5.146%**, a number that makes every homebuilder flinch. Gold fell 1.09% to **$4,105**: the safe haven sold itself to pay for the tech margin calls. The dollar index slipped 0.16% to 101.62, the Swiss franc jumped 0.58%, and **USD/JPY fell to 156.93**. The yen woke up and chose violence.

The gains were built on sand. Tesla slid 5% after trimming full-year capex, so the Cybercab and the humanoid robots just got pushed back a few more quarters. **Lam Research plunged 9%** on soft wafer-fab tool guidance. Meta edged lower. Separately, the State Department ordered non-essential diplomats out of Saudi Arabia and the UK sent RAF jets to guard Saudi airspace against Houthi threats. That is how you get **WTI near $100 and Brent near $112**. Gold's drop was a margin-call flush: leveraged tech and crypto longs selling their winners to cover Nasdaq losses. The thesis did not break. The accounts did.

Homebuilders ([XHB](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XHB)) and leveraged REITs stay off the table.

The open will gap lower. S&P futures are off 1.01% to 7,721 and Nasdaq futures are down 1.76% to 29,479. Asia saw it coming: **KOSPI dropped 3.52%, Hang Seng fell 3.03%, and Nikkei lost 2.65%**, the same earnings hangover on a different time zone. Today's swing factors are the Lockheed Martin and Procter & Gamble prints, plus any Pentagon or UK MoD readout on Saudi air defense. Watch **7,700 on the S&P 500** as first support. A break opens the door to 7,650.
