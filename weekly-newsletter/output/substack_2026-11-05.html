Peace Broke Oil. The KOSPI Fell 5.3%. Gold Wasn't Impressed.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Democrats took Congress. Wall Street threw a parade. The Dow gained 1,570 points to 53,356, the S&P 500 rose 1.6% to 7,689, and even the Russell 2000 showed up at +1.6%. Investors looked at gridlock and saw a feature.

WTI crude cratered 8.3% to $88.12. Trump admitted the Hormuz energy shock cost Republicans the House. The White House now has a political reason to keep oil cheap. The war premium just got an eviction notice. The 10-year yield fell 8 bps to 4.89%, because nothing calms bond traders like a Congress that can't pass a spending bill.

Everyone is celebrating. Everyone except gold. A relief rally is supposed to kill the safe haven. Nobody told the metal.

Here's my read:

Gold settled at $4,073, up 3.4%, on a day stocks rose 3%. Then it jumped another 1.6% overnight to $4,137 in futures. Bonds say calm. Gold says I'll believe it when I see it. One of them is wrong, and gold has been right more often this year.

Overnight was a split screen. The Hang Seng jumped 3.7% and Nifty added 2.4%, the oil-importer winners. The KOSPI fell 5.3% and the Nikkei slid 1.8% as chipmakers took the hit. Nasdaq futures are down 0.5% while S&P futures are up 0.15% at 7,732. Qualcomm fell 7% on a memory shortage. Snap missed on revenue. The market is not rewarding anyone who says "supply chain" this week.

Today's swing factor is the Bank of England. The expectation is a 25 bps hike to 4.00%. Friday's NFP is the week's anchor, with CME FedWatch odds of a December Fed hike at 82%. A hot print would make this relief rally look premature.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

Gold rose 3.4% on a day equities rose 3%, then added another 1.6% overnight. A relief rally that can't kill the safe haven is the signal, not the noise.

Confirms: GLD holds above $390 at 11:00 AM ET after the BoE volatility clears. If Bailey's press conference compresses global yields further, gold accelerates through $4,150 spot.

Kill switch: spot gold breaks back below $4,000 on a hotter-than-expected NFP print that pushes 10-year yields back above 5.00%. If that happens, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Trim upstream energy (XLE, XOP). WTI at $88.12 and a White House that wants cheap oil kills the war-premium story. If the Qatari MoU holds, there's room toward $85.
  • Add consumer discretionary and transports (XLY, XTN). $88 crude is a direct cost cut for airlines, truckers and retailers. Below $90 through Friday, these names have room to run.
  • Hold core gold (GLD, IAU). $4,000 is acting as support. Don't chase with fresh size, and don't you dare sell into this trend.
  • Stay selective in tech (QQQ). Nasdaq futures lag S&P futures by about 65 bps. Keep AI infrastructure. Stay away from the memory and social names with earnings problems.
  • Watch the BoE (EWU, FEZ). A clean hike with a hawkish Bailey strengthens sterling. A pause gives it back, and European duration gets choppy.
