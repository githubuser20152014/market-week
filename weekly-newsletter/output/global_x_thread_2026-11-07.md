1/ Gridlock was supposed to be bullish.

Democrats swept Congress. The Dow cheered for one session. Then the Nasdaq fell 5.5% in a day, and the S&P 500 finished the week down 3.1%.

The rally lasted roughly one news cycle.

2/ The markets that refused to celebrate:

Bonds: 10-year at 4.86% after a 165,000 jobs print vs ~60,000 expected.
Gold: cleared $4,000, closed near $4,066, up 4.5% on the week.
Crude: WTI fell 11.3% to $90.83. Nothing lowers oil like losing an election.

3/ The proof point: SK hynix fell more than 10% on Friday and Samsung dropped over 6%.

Korea's Kospi lost about 6% in a single session as the AI chip unwind hit Asia.

Wall Street bought the dip. Seoul did not get the memo.

4/ The story nobody is pricing: the regional split.

Nikkei: -10.7% on the week, bear market.
Hang Seng: +5.1%, because Beijing leaned on banks and brokerages.
CAC 40: +2.3% while eurozone inflation hits 4.0%.

Pick a continent. They all disagree.

5/ So which market got it right?

Equities say buy the AI dip. Gold, oil and bonds say the political cycle just made it harder to underwrite.

One of them is wrong.
