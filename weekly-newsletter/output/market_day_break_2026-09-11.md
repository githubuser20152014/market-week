## The Brief

The dominant signal today is stagflation repricing, and the bond market got there first: the 10-year at 4.94% after a 10.7 bps spike, oil at $99 with diesel hitting a record $6/gallon, and a scorching [PPI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PPI) print yesterday, all pointed at this morning's 8:30 CPI release like it owes them money. Equities sold off across the board, small-caps got it worst, and gold is holding firm near $4,380 while the dollar shrugged and stayed flat, a safe-haven divergence that only makes sense if you assume the smart money already knows how today ends. Futures are green by half a point pre-open, but that's a bet CPI stays under 0.3% core, and the bond market has made no such bet.

Yesterday's session closed ugly across the board. The **S&P 500 fell 0.58% to 7,591**, the Nasdaq shed 0.65% to 26,081, and the **Russell 2000 took the hardest hit at -1.04%, closing at 2,890**, because small-caps have no oil hedge, no pricing power, and a balance sheet that reads every basis point like a threat. The **10-year Treasury yield surged 10.7 basis points to 4.944%**, its highest level of the year and knocking on 5% like it has an appointment. Gold held at **$4,380**, the dollar was flat at 99.17, and **WTI crude cratered 3.16% to $99.24** after running hot earlier in the week, apparently deciding one record was enough for one week.

The selloff traces directly to Thursday's scorching [PPI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PPI) print, 5.4% annual wholesale inflation, which markets read as a warning shot ahead of today's CPI. Energy is the transmission mechanism: diesel above $6 a gallon is a freight surcharge stapled to every product in America, whether the sticker admits it or not. The **Strait of Hormuz** remains the structural driver, Iranian forces enforcing maritime exclusion zones, Houthi forces seizing a key Yemeni port, and Treasury Secretary Bessent lining up sanctions on a major bank Monday as part of the Iran strategy. This is not a demand story the Fed can cool with a rate hike. It's a supply shock with a geopolitical fuse, and Jerome Powell does not have a tool that fixes a shipping lane.

Small-caps ([IWM](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/IWM)) are the most exposed name on the board into a hot CPI print, leveraged balance sheets meeting a 30-year mortgage rate north of 7.45% is not a combination that ends well.

Futures are pointing to a green open, S&P futures up 0.52% to 7,637, Nasdaq futures up 0.57%, but that's pre-CPI optimism, not conviction, and optimism has a track record of folding by 8:31. **CPI is the entire session.** A core monthly print at 0.3% or above locks in an 85%+ probability of a September FOMC hike and flips this morning's green tape into a risk-off flush before lunch. APAC closed broadly lower, Nikkei down 1.93%, KOSPI off 1.76%, both pricing the same rate-hike-and-stagflation fear that's keeping the 10-year bid. Europe is flat in early trade, sensibly waiting to see what Washington does before it commits to an opinion. The 5.00% yield level on the 10-year is the number that matters more than any equity print today.
