Futures are green this morning. S&P futures up half a point, Nasdaq futures up more. Read that as good news and you're reading it wrong.

Here's why you shouldn't trust the pre-market tape today:

BONDS: The 10-year Treasury yield surged 10.7 basis points yesterday to 4.944%, its highest level of the year and six basis points from the 5% line everyone's pretending not to watch. Yields don't spike like that on a growth story. They spike when the bond market decides inflation is stickier than the Fed admits, and this morning's CPI print is the test of that thesis, not the cause of it.

GOLD: Gold held $4,380 while the dollar sat flat at 99.17 and real yields climbed against it. That combination shouldn't happen. Gold is supposed to lose to a firm dollar and rising real rates. When it doesn't, that's not a fear trade, that's institutional money treating gold as a hedge against a policy mistake, not a headline.

CRUDE: WTI actually fell 3.16% to $99.24 yesterday. Diesel hit $6 a gallon anyway, a record high. Crude and diesel moving in opposite directions is the tell: this isn't a demand story where oil and everything downstream move together. It's a refining and logistics squeeze layered on top of a Strait of Hormuz chokepoint, and it shows up in freight costs before it shows up in a barrel price.

The corporate data cuts against the growth-scare read too. Oracle jumped 6% yesterday after reporting 30% revenue growth on AI cloud demand. That's not what a demand-destruction economy looks like. Capex appetite is intact. The problem sitting under this market is input costs and energy logistics, not a buyer strike.

The angle nobody's pricing: a record diesel print isn't just a trucker's problem. Freight is the input cost baked into literally everything on a shelf, and when diesel sets a record while crude falls, that's a margin squeeze moving through supply chains right now, weeks before it shows up in a CPI basket. Watch freight and logistics costs over the next month, not just the headline oil number.

So which market has this one right, equities or bonds?

Bonds. Futures this morning are a bet that CPI comes in soft. Yields at 4.944%, gold at $4,380, and a diesel price divorced from crude are all pricing the opposite: that inflation is stickier than the pre-market tape wants to admit. Until equities catch up to what the bond market has already decided, expect more mornings where the green open doesn't survive 8:30.
