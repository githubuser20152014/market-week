A 5% Yield and a 92% Hike Bet. Wall Street Bought Futures Anyway.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Wall Street sold off yesterday. Twelve hours later, futures are up more than a percent like nobody remembers.

The Fed drops its verdict today at 2:00 PM ET. Swap markets have a 25-bps hike priced at 92%. The 10-year is parked above 5%, a level this market hasn't seen since 2007. And gold just closed up 1.25% at $4,387, climbing right next to that yield and a dollar that barely moved.

Gold rallying alongside a 5% yield and a flat dollar is not supposed to happen. Somebody in this market knows something the futures rally doesn't.

Here's my read:

The whole session bends around one appointment: FOMC's Wednesday verdict, with markets pricing a 92% chance of a hike after August's hotter than expected CPI. Think of a 5% 10-year as a margin call on every overleveraged long in the building: it mechanically raises the discount rate on future earnings and makes cash the most interesting asset on the desk again. Yesterday the S&P fell 0.45% to 7,585, the Nasdaq dropped 0.78% to 25,981, and the Dow lost 0.63% to 52,093, because nothing says confidence like selling into a Fed meeting. Oil got a one-day reprieve, falling 2.11% to $103.60 on a surprise inventory build and progress on Saudi Aramco's pipeline bypass testing. That eased crude. It did not ease yields, and it did not stop gold from climbing. Bonds never bought the equity rally of the past month, and yesterday was them collecting the receipts.

The One Trade: Long $GLD

Gold rising 1.25% with the 10-year at 5% and a flat dollar signals a genuine safe-haven bid, not an inflation trade. That bid gets stronger, not weaker, if Warsh delivers a hawkish dot plot that breaks equities this afternoon.

Confirms: GLD holds above $408 through the 2:00 PM Fed announcement and extends higher or holds flat into the 2:30 PM press conference while equities fade.

Kill switch: GLD drops below $405 on a risk-on surge after Warsh signals no further hikes, sending capital back into equities. Get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Short-duration Treasuries (SHV, BIL) are the boring, correct answer today: 5% yield, next to no duration risk. Don't overthink it.
  • QQQ and growth ETFs: stay away from chasing the pre-market pop. It smells like a short squeeze, not conviction, and a hawkish dot plot ends the party fast.
  • XLE: lighten into the pre-market strength. The Saudi pipeline story is doing the heavy lifting, and Thursday's Ministry of Energy bulletin could flip the trade back on with zero warning.
  • XLF is the trade nobody is talking about as a real-time Fed sentiment gauge: if financials rally through the press conference, the market is reading Warsh as dovish. If XLF fades, the dot plot is the problem.
  • Whatever happens, don't you dare sell your short-duration Treasuries before 2:30 PM. That's the one position immune to today's chaos either way.
