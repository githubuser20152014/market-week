The Dow Hit a Record 54,349. It Celebrated by Torching the Nasdaq 0.8%.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Wall Street set a record and immediately picked a fight with itself. The Dow closed at 54,349, a fresh all-time high. Twenty minutes of press coverage later, Nasdaq investors were nursing a 0.8% loss, courtesy of SpaceX and its casual $18.4 billion annual habit.

Gold didn't care about any of it. Up 1.8% to $4,321 on a day when the dollar didn't move and yields barely blinked, gold wasn't hedging inflation. It was hedging Washington.

The market believes two things at once: that the Hormuz deal is basically done, and that it isn't done at all. Gold is the one betting on the second answer.

Here's my read:

SpaceX told investors it's spending $18.4 billion a year on orbital AI compute, and growth investors reacted like discovering scale costs money was news. The Nasdaq fell 0.8% while every other high-multiple name got repriced in sympathy, because that's how contagion works when the marquee grower shows its bill. Meanwhile Palantir's 30% surge and Caterpillar's 5.6% pop from the prior session kept feeding an industrial-and-value rotation that doesn't need a satellite constellation to turn a profit. The "US ready to return to commitments" Hormuz headline explains why crude sat still at $75.74 instead of spiking: the risk premium is already half unwound. Gold's 1.8% gain on a flat dollar and flat yields is the real tell. It's not trading like an inflation hedge, it's trading like insurance on a deal that hasn't actually been signed.

The One Trade: Long $GLD

Gold up 1.8% on a flat-dollar, flat-yield day means the Hormuz deal is not priced as done, and this morning's Iran headline confirms it's still "looming," not closed: asymmetric upside if talks stall, limited downside if confirmed, since gold already held $4,280 through the prior risk-on surge.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,290 through the first 30 minutes of trading and crude stays below $78, confirming gold is trading as a standalone geopolitical hedge rather than an oil-correlated move.

Kill switch: A formal joint US-Iran statement on Strait of Hormuz shipping protocols. If that lands, get out fast, gold gives back 1.5-2% as the residual risk premium exits.

What else I'm watching

  • GLD: hold or add on dips toward $4,280, and don't you dare sell it into a headline that hasn't actually been confirmed yet.
  • XLI: lean long into the open. Caterpillar and the reshoring trade are a multi-day tailwind, not a one-session pop.
  • QQQ: stay away from the morning session. Nasdaq futures are down another 0.5% and KOSPI's 4.6% overnight crash says the tech unwind bought a plane ticket to Seoul.
  • XLE: neutral. Crude at $75.74 already has diplomacy priced in, this only wakes up if the Hormuz talks collapse.
  • TLT: the trade nobody is talking about, because there isn't one. A 1 basis point yield move is noise dressed up as signal. Hold cash instead.
