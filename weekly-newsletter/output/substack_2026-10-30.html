AWS Grew 39%. Amazon Fell 7%. Intel Fell 25%. Somebody Wants Cash.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Amazon's cloud business grew 39%. The stock is down more than 7% pre-market anyway. Intel is down 25% on a bleak Q4 outlook, a sluggish PC cycle, and foundry cash burn. Nasdaq futures are off 2.4% to say thanks.

Meanwhile the Dow futures are up 0.19%, because apparently nobody told the boring stocks. Seoul did not get the memo either: Kospi dropped 5.46% and the Nikkei 2.33% after the Bank of Japan declared its 2% inflation target sustainably reached and kept selling ETFs.

Wall Street used to fund construction on the promise of future rent. Now it wants cash at the door.

Here's my read:

Thursday's close was mixed and nervous. The Nasdaq dropped 1.38% to 26,184. The S&P 500 lost 0.30% to 7,720. The Dow gave up 0.25% to 52,342, which in this tape counts as courage. The 10-year yield slid 5.6 basis points to 4.989%. Gold fell 1.06% to $3,988 and lost the $4,000 handle, a level it held for roughly one coffee break. WTI jumped 2.07% to $104.11 on Middle East supply fears, because oil never misses an invitation.

Then the earnings bombs. Amazon cratered more than 7% even though AWS grew 39%. Wall Street wanted free cash flow and got a data-center construction bill. Alphabet ate a 10% wipeout the session before, so this is a pattern, not a fluke. AMD, Broadcom, and Nvidia fell in sympathy with Intel.

The Bank of Japan held at 1.25% and sped up its ETF sales. The Strait of Hormuz is still the neighborhood fire: Iran claims four tankers struck mines, and WTI sits above $104. Tech is getting audited. Energy is getting paid.

The One Trade: LONG $XLE

Chevron and Exxon just beat on surging refining margins. WTI is holding above $104 on Iran's tanker-mine claims. Energy is the only S&P sector with positive earnings momentum on a day when Nasdaq futures are off 2.4%.

Confirms: XLE opens above yesterday's close and holds that level through 10:30 AM ET, with WTI staying above $103.00 a barrel.

Kill switch: WTI drops back below $102 on fresh Qatari-mediated Iran diplomacy headlines, removing the risk premium driving today's energy bid. If that happens, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Energy: XLE and XOP are the day's cleanest long if WTI holds above $103 through mid-morning.
  • Nasdaq: Stay away from QQQ at the open. If Amazon fails to stabilize above its pre-market low in the first 30 minutes, that is your signal.
  • Gold: Don't you dare ignore GLD and IAU on the sub-$4,000 flush. The dip is levered tech funds raising cash, not a change in the macro thesis.
  • Defensives: The trade nobody is talking about is XLP, XLU, and DIA. Dow futures up 0.19% against Nasdaq futures down 2.4%: the spread is the signal.
  • North Asian chips: Stay away from SOXX and SMH today. Seoul's circuit breakers are program selling, and nobody should step in front of that.
  • Month-end: Rebalancing flows could defend the Nasdaq's 50-day moving average, or they could just close the books.
