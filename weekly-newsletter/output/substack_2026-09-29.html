Iran Offered a 7-Day Ceasefire. The White House Passed. Tanker Traffic Fell 85% Anyway.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Iran put a 7-day Hormuz ceasefire on the table. The White House said no. Commercial tanker traffic through the strait is down 85% from baseline, and WTI is still holding above $92 like nothing happened.

Equities didn't love it either. The S&P fell 0.77% to 7,683.69, the Nasdaq dropped 0.92%, and the Dow shed 245 points. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.24%, its highest sustained level since 2007, because bonds apparently wanted their old neighborhood back.

A rejected ceasefire and a seven-year yield high landed on the same Tuesday, and the market picked exactly one thing to panic about.

Here's my read:

Two things did the damage today. Regional Fed presidents reaffirmed higher-for-longer, and swap markets now price a 68% probability of a 25-basis-point hike before year-end. That's the bond market politely informing a 40-CAPE equity portfolio that its rent is due. Then the White House rejected Iran's ceasefire offer outright, tanker traffic collapsed to under 15% of normal, and crude's energy-inflation premium came roaring back.

Gold noticed too. Gold edged up 0.27% to $4,179.60 on a day the dollar also firmed to 101.42, which is not supposed to happen. When gold rises with the dollar, somebody is hedging a risk that has nothing to do with rates.

Futures are barely breathing into the open: S&P futures at 7,743 are down 0.04%, Nasdaq futures are fractionally green. The whole session comes down to one number: the BEA's revised PCE price index, due this morning. Hot above 2.8% core year-over-year and yields go higher. Soft below 2.5% and rate-sensitive names get a bounce, capped by the oil premium.

The One Trade: LONG $XLE

The Hormuz blockade is not a news cycle. It's a physical supply disruption, tanker traffic down 85%, no resolution likely before Thursday's OPEC+ meeting at the earliest. That's a direct windfall margin for North American upstream operators.

Confirms: XLE holds above its prior-day close and WTI sustains above $91.50 through the first hour, with no headlines suggesting a Geneva breakthrough.

Kill switch: Iranian state media or the White House confirms a formal interim maritime safety corridor. That collapses the geopolitical oil premium and likely drops WTI below $88. If that hits the tape, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Long-duration Treasuries (TLT, EDV): Stay underweight. A 10-year at 5.24% with a 68% hike probability priced in is a structural headwind, not a dip to buy.
  • Energy (XLE, AMLP): Hold or add. The Hormuz deadlock is the real driver, not a headline. Backchannel Geneva talks could produce a surprise resolution, so size accordingly.
  • High-multiple software and growth (IGV, ARKK): Trim exposure. A Shiller CAPE above 40 competing against 5%+ risk-free yields is a valuation tax on every speculative multiple, not a momentum call.
  • Gold (GLD): Maintain or add. Gold rising while the dollar also firms is a real divergence tied to Hormuz and broader UNGA-week uncertainty, not noise.
  • China tech (FXI, KWEB): Small tactical entry only. State Council stimulus and a factory PMI back to 50.1 are modest but real, with Thursday's NDRC briefing as the next confirmation gate.
