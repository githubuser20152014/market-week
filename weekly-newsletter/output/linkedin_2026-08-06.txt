The Dow closed at a record 54,349 yesterday. Nobody who owns Nasdaq stocks is celebrating.

SpaceX told the market it's spending $18.4 billion a year on orbital AI infrastructure, and growth investors responded by dumping every high-multiple name in sympathy. Here's why you shouldn't trust the "risk-on" headline just because one index hit a new high.

Bonds didn't buy it. The 10-year Treasury yield ticked down just 1 basis point to 4.617%. That's not a bond market voting for a soft landing, that's a bond market that didn't bother showing up to vote at all.

Gold didn't buy it either. Up 1.8% to $4,321 on a day the dollar didn't move and yields barely blinked, that combination only happens when buyers are pricing a specific, unresolved risk rather than a broad macro theme, in this case the "Strait of Hormuz deal" that US and Iranian officials are reportedly close to but haven't actually signed.

Crude didn't buy it. Oil sat almost still at $75.74 despite a Ukrainian drone strike on one of Russia's largest refineries overnight. Under normal geopolitical math that headline should have moved the tape. It didn't, because the market has already decided the Hormuz diplomacy story matters more than the Russia supply story, at least until one of them proves it wrong.

The corporate proof point: Warner Bros. Discovery's streaming revenue jumped 10% ahead of its proposed Paramount combination, and the market barely blinked. That's the macro thesis in miniature: capital is rewarding cash-generating, already-scaled businesses over anything still burning money to get there, whether that's a legacy media merger or a mega-cap AI buildout.

The angle nobody's covering: a new WTO/IMF report on record trade interventions is quietly fueling a reshoring capital wave, and it showed up yesterday in the same place the AI selloff money went, industrials. Caterpillar's 5.6% gain wasn't just an earnings pop, it was capital finding a home in companies that build things domestically while the AI capex story gets repriced for risk. Supply chain reshoring is becoming a multi-quarter tailwind hiding inside a single-day tech rotation headline.

So which market got it right yesterday: the Dow celebrating a record high, or gold and bonds pricing in unresolved risk? One of them is going to be wrong by Friday. My money's on the assets that don't need a press release to move.
