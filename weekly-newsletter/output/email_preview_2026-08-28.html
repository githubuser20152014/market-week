The Morning Brief - 2026-08-28

Daily Edition · Market intelligence at the open

The Brief

Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole speech as Fed Chair today, and the bond market already voted on what it expects to hear: ten-year yields climbed to 4.67% yesterday, pricing in a chair comfortable with elevated term premiums before he's said a single word. Equities didn't get the memo, closing broadly higher on Nvidia's earnings afterglow. Gold at $4,656 is the tell nobody's talking about: up on a day yields rose and the dollar shrugged, which is not how gold is supposed to behave unless something structural just changed. Nasdaq futures are off 0.31% this morning, tech quietly sobering up from the Nvidia high and staring down Marvell's hangover, with Warsh's actual remarks still ahead of us.

Yesterday's scoreboard: S&P 500 at 7,730.99 (+0.72%), Nasdaq +1.57%, Dow +0.20%, Russell 2000 +0.28%. Gold at $4,656 (+1.00%), the 10-year yield up to 4.672%, WTI slipping to $83.47, dollar index dead flat at 99.18. Treasury futures are down 0.30% pre-market, positioning ahead of a speech that hasn't happened yet.

Two catalysts are doing the work. Nvidia's $96.2B Q2 print, Data Center revenue up 117% year-over-year, Q3 guidance of $108B, acted like jet fuel under the Nasdaq and dragged the whole AI trade higher on fumes of relief. Then there's Warsh: preview coverage of his Jackson Hole debut frames him as comfortable with elevated term premiums and allergic to rigid forward guidance, and the bond market leaned that way before he's even at the podium. Ten-year yields at 4.67% are a bet on what he says, not a reaction to it. Add Strait of Hormuz corridor talks easing crude's war premium, and WTI just snapped a two-week win streak on the mere suggestion of diplomacy.

What it means for you

The market is already trading Warsh's playbook before he's said a word. High free-cash-flow, low-leverage names (QQQ mega-caps ex-speculative junk, COWZ, QUAL) keep winning; anything that needs cheap long-term money (TLT, and it is not your friend right now) keeps losing. GLD is the name worth staring at: gold climbing while yields rise and the dollar sits still is not the script, and scripts don't get rewritten for no reason. XLE takes a breather as Hormuz corridor talks deflate the war premium, though the $80-86/bbl floor holds on inventory draws alone. And Marvell just proved that beating estimates by 37% and still dropping 8% is this market's version of a participation trophy: second-tier semis are priced for perfection, and perfection is a tall order on a Friday.

Nasdaq futures are off 0.31% this morning, still nursing the Marvell hangover and pre-positioning for Warsh. S&P futures are nearly flat at 7,737.50, unbothered as usual. KOSPI was the lone APAC casualty, down 1.79%, while Nikkei (+0.41%) and ASX (+0.60%) shrugged the whole thing off. New Home Sales at 10:00 AM ET kicks off the morning, but Warsh's actual remarks later today are the real event: a hawkish, higher-for-longer tone confirms what yields already priced and leans harder on homebuilders (ITB) and utilities (XLU), any dovish surprise unwinds the whole move fast.

The One Trade

GLD - Long

Gold is up 1% on a day yields rose and the dollar flatlined, the usual headwinds are simply not working, which signals institutional structural allocation, not a tactical flight-to-safety bid.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,630 through the New Home Sales print at 10:00 AM ET and through Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks later today, without giving back the overnight gain. Kill switch: Gold breaks $4,600 on a closing basis, particularly if Warsh's actual remarks spike the 10-year yield above 4.75% and the dollar index reclaims 99.80, restoring the inverse correlation and invalidating the decoupling thesis.

Positioning Notes

  • GLD: Hold or add on any intraday dip toward $4,620-4,630. Gold rising 1% on a day when yields ticked up and the dollar barely moved signals structural demand, not a tactical hedge. This decoupling from the rate/dollar correlation is the kind of setup that precedes sustained runs. Exit the thesis if gold fails $4,600 on a closing basis.
  • QQQ: Cautious hold, not a chase. Nvidia's earnings afterglow is largely priced into the tape; Marvell's 8% drop on strong revenue growth is a concrete signal that the market will punish any sub-perfection print across second-tier AI names. If futures recover through the 29,700 level by 10:30 AM, the dip is buyable; if they stall there, trim tech exposure into strength.
  • TLT: Avoid or maintain short bias. Preview coverage of Warsh's Jackson Hole debut points to a framework that endorses elevated term premiums and rejects the kind of central bank pivot communication that would bail out long-duration bondholders. T-Note futures are already down 0.30% pre-market, positioning ahead of confirmation. A TLT short (or underweight) is confirmed by any 10-year yield print above 4.70% once Warsh actually speaks today.
  • XLE: Neutral to slight underweight near-term. Hormuz corridor talks removing the acute supply-disruption premium, combined with WTI stalling at $83.47, reduces the near-term asymmetry for energy. The $80/bbl floor and steady inventory draws keep the medium-term thesis intact, but today is not the day to add. Re-engage if WTI pulls back toward $81 and corridor talks stall.
  • ITB (homebuilders): Watch the 10:00 AM New Home Sales print, then watch Warsh. A beat sends yields higher and pressures the sector further; Warsh's actual remarks later today are the bigger risk if he confirms the hawkish preview. A miss, or a softer-than-expected Warsh tone, is a potential tactical long entry given how much rate-sensitive sectors have already repriced. Set your entry condition off the data and the speech, not the pre-market noise.
