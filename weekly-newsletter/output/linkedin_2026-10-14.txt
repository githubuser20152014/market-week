Stocks closed red in every direction yesterday. Nobody panicked. Nobody bought either.

Here is why you shouldn't read that as calm. The whole tape is parked in front of an 8:30 AM inflation print, and the other markets are not being quiet about it.

Bonds. The 10-year yield is sitting at 5.217%. It dipped 2.3 basis points yesterday and that counted as relief, because we are at multi-decade highs. When a rounding error feels like good news, the market is not relaxed. It is bracing.

Gold. It slipped 0.43% to $4,159 while Saudi officials confirmed attacks on Aramco facilities. The classic safe haven looked at a war and shrugged. That tells you real rates are running the show, not headlines.

Crude. WTI jumped 1.58% to $95.63 after the IEA warned the market is tight. Oil is the only asset in the room that took the geopolitics at face value.

Now the proof point. Goldman posted $17.01 EPS on $6.44 billion in equities revenue. Bank of America absorbed a $1.2 billion Treasury fine. Same sector, same quarter, opposite outcomes. Volatility pays the trading desks and invoices the balance sheets.

The story nobody is covering: mortgages. More borrowers are choosing riskier loan structures because higher rates have crushed affordability. Households are not borrowing more. They are borrowing worse. That is what a 5%+ 10-year does when it leaves the Treasury market and walks into a kitchen.

Add the pre-market tape and the split gets louder. S&P futures are up 0.14%, Dow futures up 0.29%, Nasdaq futures down 0.20%. Europe is up 0.5% to 0.6%. Tech is the one sweating, because long-duration growth is what a hot print hits first.

So which market got it right? Equities say wait. Gold says rates win. Oil says supply is the story. Bonds say brace. Only one of those is reading the data correctly, and consensus on CPI is 3.6%, up from 3.4%. I trust the markets that moved on fundamentals over the one that sat still.
