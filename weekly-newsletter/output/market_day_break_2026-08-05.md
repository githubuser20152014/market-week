## The Brief

Gold ground higher 1.5% on the day the geopolitical risk premium got vaporized. That's the tell. Stocks hit fresh all-time highs on Hormuz progress and a Palantir earnings explosion, but the gold bid did not fade. Dollar hedgers do not need a headline to keep buying. Today's real test: SpaceX's 8% capex-shock collapse after hours is the sword hanging over AI infrastructure names at the open, and Nasdaq futures are already flat while the S&P adds 0.28%.

The S&P 500 closed at a fresh all-time high of **7,736.52**, up 1.79%, while the Dow Jones surged 907 points to **54,085.88** and the Nasdaq jumped 2.59% to 26,585. The Russell 2000 added 1.85%, confirming broad participation. **Gold settled at $4,095.40, up 1.53%**, while WTI crude got demolished, settling down 5.7% at $75.77 on the Hormuz relief trade itself. The 10-year Treasury yield dropped 5.9 basis points to **4.627%**, and the USD index barely moved at 99.82.

Two catalysts drove yesterday's session. First, Palantir surged nearly 30% and Caterpillar gained 5.6% on earnings beats in enterprise software and heavy industrials, pulling the broader market higher. Second, Oman-brokered talks between US and Iranian officials produced preliminary transit protocols for the **Strait of Hormuz**, collapsing the geopolitical risk premium that had been embedded in crude for months, and WTI paid for it with a 5.7% haircut in a single session. Lower oil directly relieves CPI pressure, which is why bonds rallied alongside stocks, an unusual confirmation. The quieter tell: gold still added 1.5% on a day when tail-risk was unwinding and everything else screamed risk-on. That is not a rounding error. It suggests gold buyers are reacting to something else, likely dollar credibility concerns and the structural fiscal backdrop, not the Iran headline.
