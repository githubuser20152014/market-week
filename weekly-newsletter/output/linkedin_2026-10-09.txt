Drones hit Riyadh airport. Oil jumped 3.6%. Equity futures are up this morning anyway.

Here's why I wouldn't trust the bounce.

The Nasdaq lost 1.25% yesterday while the Dow gained 0.10% and the Russell 2000 moved 0.03%. That is not a market with a view. That is a market where the index that stayed home got a medal.

Now look at who disagreed with the bounce.

Bonds: The 10-year yield sits at 5.23%, one of the highest levels in two decades. A 30-year auction cleared at a solid 2.54x bid-to-cover, and yields still barely flinched. Long-duration growth stocks are discounting future earnings at a rate that keeps eating them. The September Fed minutes put roughly a 38% probability on another October hike. The Fed is still holding the leash.

Gold: Up 0.4% yesterday at $4,157, then up another 1.2% in futures overnight while stocks rally. When equities and gold rise together, someone is buying insurance while the party is on. That is worth noticing.

Crude: WTI closed at $91.49 after Houthi strikes on Riyadh and continued harassment of tankers near the Strait of Hormuz. It is already down 1.1% pre-market on hopes that escalation is being deferred. Deferred is not cancelled.

The corporate proof point: Delta Air Lines cut its 2026 forecast this morning on fuel costs, even as its CEO insisted demand is still strong. When an airline blames fuel, the oil move is no longer a headline. It is a line item.

The second-order story nobody is covering: France. Nobel laureate Paul Krugman warned that France may be too big to save, and French spreads sit near 80 basis points over German Bunds. Europe is rallying 1.0 to 1.3% on a German industrial rebound while its second largest economy has a budget fight and a possible no-confidence vote this weekend. One of those stories is going to win.

So which market got it right? Stocks say the shock is priced. Bonds, gold and oil say it isn't. I side with the three that are charging a premium for risk. Futures bounce on hope. Gold, yields and crude bounce on arithmetic.
