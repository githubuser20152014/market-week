Nvidia just delivered one of the biggest beats in tech history. $96.2 billion in Q2 revenue, up 106% year-over-year. A $108 billion Q3 guide. Nasdaq futures are up 1.1%.

Here's why you shouldn't just chase the number: the market that's supposed to celebrate a beat like this isn't celebrating uniformly. The Dow closed dead flat. That's not confirmation. That's the market telling you this is a one-sector story.

Bonds didn't buy it. The 10-year yield ticked up to 4.664% even as risk assets wobbled yesterday, and it's been refusing to rally alongside equities for several sessions now. If this were a genuine broad-based risk-on move, yields would be easing, not creeping toward 4.70%.

Gold didn't buy it either. Gold held $4,635, up 0.81%, on a day when equities were essentially flat. Gold gaining while stocks tread water is a hedge, not a momentum trade. Someone is still pricing in Strait of Hormuz tanker risk and the fallout from the "Operation Economic Outcast" sanctions package, quietly, while headlines focus on chips.

Crude shrugged too. WTI slipped 0.23% to $82.04 even with unresolved shipping-lane tension, which tells you the market is betting on demand-side softening more than supply risk.

The corporate proof point that actually confirms the macro thesis: CEO Jensen Huang confirmed Vera Rubin silicon has entered full-scale production. That's not a one-quarter story. That's a multi-year AI capex commitment getting validated in real time.

The angle nobody's covering: this print isn't just an Nvidia story, it's a memory chip story. Samsung and SK Hynix are riding the HBM demand wave straight into Nvidia's supply chain, and that showed up overnight in KOSPI, up 1.53% while most of APAC was flat to lower. The real read-through of a Blackwell/Vera Rubin ramp isn't just GPU demand, it's a structural bid under high-bandwidth memory that most investors are still pricing as an afterthought.

So which market got it right yesterday? Equities said euphoria. Bonds and gold said caution. With Fed Chair Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote landing Friday, the bond market's read is the one that matters more. A single hawkish sentence from Warsh does more damage to this rally than Nvidia's entire quarter can offset.
