Bessent Is Capping Yields At 4.80%. The Strait Of Hormuz Isn't Playing Along.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Oil pierced $100 a barrel on Tuesday because Houthi drones hit Saudi energy hubs, CENTCOM disabled three Iranian tankers, and Tehran declared the Strait of Hormuz a maritime exclusion zone. That is not a headline risk. That is 20% of the world's daily oil supply running through a valve someone just froze shut.

The S&P dropped 0.58% to 7,673, the Dow shed 628 points (-1.18%) to 52,786, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.32% to 26,421. Meanwhile gold ripped to $4,446 (+1.19%), the 10-year climbed to 4.806%, and the dollar softened. Rising yields, a soft dollar, and surging gold all moving together is not a rates story. It's a fear story.

Futures are down again this morning. Tuesday's selloff didn't just lack a floor, it left the seatbelt at home.

Here's my read:

Two catalysts did the damage. First, the Houthi strikes on Saudi installations at Abha, Jazan, and Najran, plus Tehran's exclusion zone over Hormuz, sent Brent above $100. Think of the Strait as a pipeline valve for a fifth of global oil demand. Freeze it and every refinery, airline, and chemical plant on the planet feels the input shock by lunch. Second, Friday's nonfarm payrolls print of 162,000 had already killed the rate-cut narrative before the energy shock even showed up to make things worse.

Now Treasury Secretary Bessent is running a debt buyback operation specifically to hold the 10-year near 4.80%. That is the only thing standing between an orderly market and a full duration unwind, and it is held together with government-issued duct tape. Gold at $4,446 rallying into a soft dollar and rising yields at the same time is the tell: this is a geopolitical fear bid, not a rate trade, and gold does not care whether Bessent's buyback holds or not.

The One Trade: Long $GLD

Gold is rallying into a soft dollar and rising yields simultaneously. That divergence only happens when the market is pricing pure geopolitical fear, not a rates trade, and it does not unwind until the Strait of Hormuz reopens or Iran stands down.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,420 through the first hour and doesn't give back gains if equities attempt a bounce. Gold ignoring an equity recovery is the confirmation that the bid is structural, not reactive.

Kill switch: A confirmed UN Security Council ceasefire resolution, or a Pentagon announcement of full armed tanker escorts restoring Hormuz traffic. Either headline collapses the premium and sends spot gold into an $80-100 reversal, fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Long XLE: WTI at $94.89 with Brent already above $100 and Hormuz effectively closed to unescorted traffic is a structural supply deficit, not a headline blip. Don't you dare sell it on a dip below the prior breakout.
  • Stay away from TLT: the 10-year at 4.806% with oil re-accelerating into CPI week means duration is fighting inflation risk and payrolls at the same time. Bessent's buyback caps the spike, it does not create a floor.
