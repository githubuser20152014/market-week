Friday's jobs report: 29,000. Wall Street wanted 85,000 or more. The Nasdaq's response: a record close at 27,477.

Here's why I wouldn't trust that move.

The market didn't read a weak labor market as a warning. It read it as permission. Fed-pause odds for October 27-28 jumped above 78%, so bad news became good news again. That works right up until something else in the data refuses to cooperate.

Three markets didn't join the celebration.

Bonds. The 10-year yield rose 3.4 basis points to 5.31%. If the economy were truly stalling, the bond market would be buying the story. It isn't. A yield that high is a valuation ceiling for tech, and equities are ignoring it.

The dollar. The dollar index rose 0.28% to 102.18. A stronger dollar on a weak jobs print is not what a "the Fed is about to ease" narrative looks like.

Oil. The G7 released reserves. Middle East shipments are rising. Brent is still sitting at $100, with Houthi attacks keeping traders on edge. Supply headlines improved and the price didn't move. That is a market telling you it doesn't believe the relief.

The corporate story today is Paramount's takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery closing. Big deals close when financing is available and confidence is high. Keep that in mind next to a 5.31% yield.

The second-order story nobody is covering: ISM Services Prices spiked to 74.0, the highest since July 2022. Labor is cooling, services inflation is not, and $100 oil feeds directly into jet fuel and diesel costs for airlines and trucking. Margins get squeezed from the cost side while equities price the demand side.

Why this matters beyond one session: when stocks, bonds, the dollar and oil all tell different stories, the stories get reconciled eventually. Usually the price of the loudest one moves. Right now the loudest one is the Nasdaq.

So which market got it right? Stocks say the Fed will blink. Bonds, the dollar and oil say inflation isn't finished. One of them is wrong, and the bond market has a better record of not being sentimental.
