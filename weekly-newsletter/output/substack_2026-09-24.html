The 10-Year Hit 5.11%. Small Caps Lost 1.77%. Oil Just Smiled.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The economy came in hot. Stocks sold off. That is not a typo, that is the new rulebook.

The 10-year Treasury hit 5.11% yesterday, the highest since 2007. The S&P 500 gave up 0.75% to 7,706. The Nasdaq lost 1.13%. The Russell 2000 fell 1.77% because small caps borrow at floating rates, and floating rates just went to the gym.

Gold fell too. The dollar rallied. Oil rose. Stocks down, gold down, dollar up, crude up. Nobody was safe, and nobody was even hiding well. Good news is now the bad news, and the bond market is holding the pen.

Here's my read:

Two things did the damage. First, flash composite PMI printed 58.4, the fastest private-sector growth since July 2021. Second, New York Fed President Williams called another rate hike by year-end "reasonable." October hike odds jumped to roughly 77% from about 53% the day before.

The 10-year surged 14.7 basis points in one session. Gold dropped 1.33% to $4,318. The dollar index climbed to 101.10. WTI gained 1.81% to $92.16. At a 5% risk-free yield, paying 22x forward earnings needs a story, and the story is now on probation.

Bonds never bought the rally. Equities are just catching up to the news. Futures point lower again this morning: S&P down about 0.5%, Nasdaq down about 0.9%. Asia was a split decision. Nikkei +1.30%, Hang Seng -0.29%, ASX -0.72%, Nifty 50 -1.64%. KOSPI was closed for Chuseok, so it dodged the whole mess. Europe is slightly lower.

The One Trade: LONG $XLE

Oil is up 1.5% to about $93.5 while the S&P sits down 0.75% and the 10-year prints a 19-year high. Energy is the only sector ignoring the bond market. The Strait of Hormuz risk premium and a fight over a diesel export ban explain why. That divergence is the trade.

Confirms: XLE holds above its prior session close within the first 30 minutes of trading and WTI stays above $92.50 through 10:30 AM ET.

Kill switch: WTI reverses below $91.50. That means the geopolitical premium is collapsing, not that equities are catching up to crude. If it breaks, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Trim or avoid TLT and long-duration bond ETFs. The Fed is not coming to help, and the 10-year at 5.1% has room to run. Duration risk is not paid here.
  • SGOV and BIL over utilities and staples. Boring finally has a coupon. At 5.1% risk-free, T-bill ETFs outyield the defensive sleeves without the duration hit.
  • Stay away from SOXX and SMH for now. The Trump-Xi summit is a mild tailwind, but a 5% 10-year compresses growth multiples mechanically. Don't buy diplomacy before the inflation data.
  • Hedge your small-cap longs into the next inflation print. The Russell 2000 is the most rate-sensitive index in the complex. A hot number makes RWM the protection. A cool one makes IWM interesting, if you can stomach the volatility.
  • The trade nobody is talking about: cash. A 5% Treasury is real competition for speculative tech, unprofitable growth and REITs. The next inflation print is the live grenade, and a hot number pushes the 10-year further past 5.1%.
