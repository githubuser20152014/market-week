Amazon's cloud business grew 39%. The stock is down more than 7% before the open. If that makes sense to you, the market would like a word.

Here is why I would not trust Thursday's close. The Nasdaq fell 1.38% to 26,184, while the Dow lost just 0.25%. This morning the gap is wider: Nasdaq futures are off 2.4%, Dow futures are up 0.19%. Same planet, allegedly.

Three markets refused to play along.

Bonds. The 10-year yield slid 5.6 basis points to 4.989%. Money ran toward safety while stocks sold. That is not a shrug, that is a flinch.

Gold. It fell 1.06% to $3,988 and lost the $4,000 handle. A safe haven selling off on a nervous day usually means leveraged funds are raising cash from whatever they can sell. Gold got drafted.

Crude. WTI jumped 2.07% to $104.11 after Iran claimed four tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Oil did not get the risk-off memo.

The proof point is Intel. It collapsed 25% on a bleak Q4 outlook, a sluggish PC cycle, and foundry cash burn. AMD, Broadcom, and Nvidia fell in sympathy. Alphabet lost 10% the session before. That is a pattern, not a fluke.

Now the story nobody is covering: Tokyo. The Bank of Japan held at 1.25%, declared its 2% inflation target sustainably reached, and sped up its ETF sales. Kospi dropped 5.46% and the Nikkei 2.33%. For years the BOJ was a quiet, price-insensitive buyer of equities. When the buyer turns seller, every crowded momentum trade from Seoul to Silicon Valley feels it.

Add the calendar. It is month-end, which means fund managers are closing October books. Some will defend the Nasdaq's 50-day moving average. Some will just close the books. Either way, the first 30 minutes of trading in Intel and Amazon will tell you who is buying the dip and who is hiding from it.

So which market got it right? Stocks say panic. Bonds say worry. Oil says war. Gold says nothing, loudly.

My read: equities are finally pricing what the cash flow statements have been saying. Investors used to fund construction on the promise of future rent. Now they want cash at the door.
