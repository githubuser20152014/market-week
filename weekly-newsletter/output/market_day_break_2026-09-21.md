## The Brief

Diplomacy did what six OPEC meetings couldn't. WTI cratered nearly 7% to $93.51 overnight, because nothing kills a risk premium like Trump and Xi agreeing to be civil for a week. Equity futures are throwing a party, S&P futures up 1.4% and Nasdaq futures up 2.1%, with APAC already dancing: KOSPI +1.6%, Nikkei +1.4%, Hang Seng +1.2%. But gold is flat and yields eased 4 basis points to 4.957%, which is the market's polite way of saying this is a relief rally, not a growth story.

Yesterday's tape was a split decision. The **Nasdaq eked out 0.39% to 26,522** and the S&P added 0.17% to **7,650.50**, while the Dow slipped 0.18% and the Russell 2000 went nowhere at all, proof the buying was narrow and mega-cap tech is still carrying the whole team by itself. Commodities did the real damage: **WTI collapsed 6.77% to $93.51**, its fourth straight losing session, and **gold dropped 0.78% to $4,390.30**. The 10-year eased to **4.957%**, backing off this week's 5.01% scare. The dollar, characteristically, shrugged: USD Index up 0.04% to 100.26.

Blame diplomacy for the crude massacre. The **Trump-Xi summit, locked in for September 23-25 in Washington**, drained the war premium that had oil trading above $105 last week. Bessent called his sit-down with Vice Premier He Lifeng 'successful,' the two sides agreed to a $30 billion tariff mitigation framework, and suddenly nobody needs to hedge against a trade war that isn't happening. Saudi Aramco's expanded Red Sea bypass capacity and a 2.1 million barrel distillate inventory build finished the job: there is no supply-shock story left to tell, just diplomacy making the traders who bet on chaos look foolish. Meanwhile the **Bank of Japan's 1.25% policy rate** keeps unwinding the yen carry trade, USD/JPY up to 157.32 overnight, because someone always has to ruin a good mood.
