The S&P 500 closed up 0.72% and the Nasdaq up 1.57% yesterday, riding Nvidia's earnings afterglow into the weekend. Here's why you shouldn't trust the rally: the bond market spent the same session pricing in a Fed chair who hasn't given his signature speech yet.

Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote today. Yesterday, before he said a single word, the 10-year yield climbed to 4.672%. That's not a market reacting to news. That's a market front-running a speech, and front-running is exactly the kind of positioning that reverses hard when the actual event doesn't match the priced-in version.

Gold is the market that should worry you most. It rose 1% to $4,656 on a day when yields went up and the dollar barely moved, the two forces that are supposed to be gold's biggest headwinds. When gold rises anyway, that's not a tactical hedge, that's capital making a structural bet that something bigger than one Fed speech is in motion.

Crude told its own story: WTI slipped as Strait of Hormuz corridor talks eased the war premium, snapping a two-week win streak. The geopolitical bid that's propped up energy for weeks just took its first real hit from actual diplomacy.

The corporate proof point that should give you pause: Marvell beat revenue estimates by 37% and its stock still dropped 8% because the outlook wasn't perfect enough. That's not a company-specific problem, that's a market that has stopped rewarding good numbers and started demanding flawless ones. If that's the bar for semis today, it's the bar for everything once Warsh actually speaks.

The angle nobody's covering: this isn't really a Fed story, it's a credibility test. Warsh is new to the podium, and markets that pre-price a speech this aggressively are betting they already know his framework. If he delivers anything softer than what's priced in, the unwind in yields, and everything that's been trading off them, could be fast and ugly.

So which market has it right: equities partying on Nvidia, or bonds and gold bracing for a hawkish debut that hasn't happened yet? History says don't bet against the market that moves first and asks questions later. Bonds and gold moved first.
