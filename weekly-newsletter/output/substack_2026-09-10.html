Bessent Spent $6 Billion to Cap Yields. The 10-Year Hit 4.837% Anyway.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The Treasury tried to buy its way out of a bond selloff yesterday. It failed in real time, on camera, with the whole market watching.

Scott Bessent expanded the debt buyback facility to $6 billion specifically to hold down the 10-year. Primary dealers took the liquidity, said thanks, and kept selling anyway. The 10-year Treasury yield settled at 4.837%, up 3 basis points, closing in on levels not seen since late 2023. Meanwhile the S&P 500 fell 0.48% to 7,636, the Russell 2000 got run over at -1.32%, and WTI crude closed at $97.22, up 1.22%, because the Strait of Hormuz is still the only macro variable that matters this week.

You can't buy back a bond market's confidence. You can only rent it, briefly, at a very bad price.

Here's my read:

The driver is the Strait of Hormuz, again. U.S. CENTCOM strikes disabled three Iranian tankers this week, Iran answered with naval exclusion zone threats and its own interdictions, war-risk insurers pulled Gulf coverage like it was a bad Yelp review, and OPEC+ confirmed it will hold production quotas through October just to make sure nobody feels better about any of this. Net effect: 20% of global oil shipments now trade on geopolitical mood, PPI and CPI expectations are re-accelerating, and central banks are back in hiking posture whether they wanted to be or not. Rate futures now show an 82% probability of a 25 basis point hike at the September 15-16 FOMC. Gold held firm at $4,435, up 0.43%, quietly doing its one job while everything else scrambled. The smart money voted with its wallet, and the wallet voted for gold.

The One Trade: LONG $XLE

WTI at $97 with a structurally blocked Hormuz and OPEC+ holding quotas through October is a direct, sustained margin windfall for domestic energy producers, and the market has not yet fully repriced E&P earnings to this oil level.

Confirms: XLE holds above its prior session close in the first 30 minutes of trading and WTI does not break below $95 on any IEA SPR headline before noon.

Kill switch: a confirmed IEA emergency SPR coordination announcement or a credible U.S.-Iran de-escalation statement out of the UN Security Council session this afternoon sends WTI below $93. If that prints, get out fast, the whole thesis is dead.

What else I'm watching

  • Hold GLD as a core position, not a trade, don't you dare sell it here. Gold rising alongside a firm dollar and higher real yields is the anomaly that says this demand is structural, not speculative.
  • Stay away from TLT and anything long-duration. Bessent's buyback failed to hold yields below 4.85% yesterday, and duration is a losing position until the Fed gives a credible pivot. SGOV and BIL pay you to wait instead.
  • Trim IWM. The Russell 2000 underperformed large caps by 83 basis points yesterday, and it's the most exposed index to higher refinancing costs and energy input inflation. If Durable Goods disappoints this morning, small caps lead the selloff.
  • The trade nobody is talking about: ITA. NATO's critical infrastructure reinforcement after Germany pinned the Leipzig airport drone strikes on Russian intelligence, stacked on active CENTCOM operations in the Gulf, is a sustained defense spending tailwind that hasn't been priced in yet.
