Producer prices ran at 6.3% year over year. The 10-year yield fell 10 basis points to 5.09%.

Both of those happened on the same day. Here's why you shouldn't trust the rally that came with it.

The Russell 2000 gained 1.38%. The Nasdaq gained 0.08%. That's not a broad risk-on tape. That's a rotation out of growth wearing a risk-on costume.

The dissenting markets are the real story.

Bonds: a 6.3% producer price print should push yields up. Instead, buyers showed up again near 5.25% and pulled the 10-year down. Either duration buyers see a ceiling, or the bond market is ignoring the data. Both can't be right.

Gold: it held $4,196 while equities celebrated. Gold doesn't hold through a risk-on session unless real-rate skepticism and a softer dollar are doing the work. The dollar index slipped to 101.67.

Crude: WTI dropped 2% to $95.98 after Trump said he won't use nuclear weapons or send ground troops to Iran. The market took a list of things he won't do as de-escalation. That's a very generous reading.

Small caps are the canaries of domestic credit. This week the canary is singing, and it's doing it with 5%+ mortgage rates in the room. New Home Sales lands at 10 AM ET today, and that print will say a lot about whether the Russell's 2,767 is conviction or momentum.

The corporate proof point: BNY Mellon, Charles Schwab, U.S. Bancorp and Morgan Stanley's $9 billion wealth quarter all beat. That gave allocators a fundamental reason to leave long-duration growth for banks and industrials, even with inflation sticky at 3.7%.

The second-order story nobody is covering: EU gas storage sits at 74% against a historical 85 to 90% seasonal buffer. If oil is pricing peace while Europe heads into winter short on gas, one of those prices is wrong.

So which market got it right? Equities say growth is fine and cyclicals are the new leaders. Bonds, gold and gas say the inflation problem isn't solved. I side with the dissenters. Rotation is real, but the 10-year holding below 5.10% is the only thing keeping this party polite.
