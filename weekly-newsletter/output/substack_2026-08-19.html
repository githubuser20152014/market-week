$4,415 Gold. A 19-Year High on the 30-Year. Stagflation Just RSVP'd.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Wall Street had a bond problem and a Hormuz problem on the same Tuesday, and it picked the wrong one to worry about first.

Gold ripped to $4,415 while stocks fell. The Nasdaq dropped 1.3%, the S&P 500 fell 0.7%, and the Russell 2000 lost 1.3%. Nobody was spared except the one asset that doesn't need permission to rally.

Futures are pretending everything is fine this morning. KOSPI just fell 5.8% to tell you otherwise.

Here's my read:

The US-Iran truce expired without so much as a phone call. The UAE cut Iran off financially after missiles landed near its shipping lanes, and a commercial tanker took a direct hit in the Strait of Hormuz, a corridor that moves roughly 20% of the world's oil. Energy desks are now pricing actual war risk, not diplomatic theater. Meanwhile the 30-year yield punched to 5.335%, a 19-year high, and someone had to pay for that: Nvidia, Broadcom, and Micron got the bill. When the discount rate climbs, the stocks pricing in the most distant profits fall hardest. Gold surged 1.1% to $4,415 anyway, the 10-year slipped to 4.706%, the dollar softened to 99.40, and WTI held above $85, a combination that smells less like a growth scare and more like stagflation trying on a new outfit.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

Gold is ripping to fresh highs while equities fall, the dollar softens, and the Gulf builds toward an actual supply shock. Three tailwinds, one asset, and none of them are close to played out.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,380 through the first hour of trading and adds to gains after FOMC minutes release this afternoon, confirming the bid is structural, not just overnight positioning.

Kill switch: FOMC minutes signal an unexpected hawkish tilt that spikes the 10-year above 4.80%, triggering a broad dollar reversal and unwinding the safe-haven premium in gold fast.

What else I'm watching

  • XLE / XOP: the Hormuz trade is back on the table as real money instead of a headline. If Brent holds above $91 in early European trading, don't you dare sell energy here.
  • TLT: stay away from duration. The 30-year at multi-decade highs and no Fed rescue in sight makes this a losing bet before it starts.
  • IWM: small caps are stuck between tight credit and rising input costs, the worst possible combination for the most fragile leg of the market. Reduce or avoid.
  • QQQ: the trade nobody is talking about is a second compression day in megacap tech if Target misses and the FOMC minutes hold hawkish. Wait for the 10-year to pick a direction before touching it.
