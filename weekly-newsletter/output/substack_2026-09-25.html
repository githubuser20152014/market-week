The 10-Year Sits at 5.17%. Stocks Called It a Rally Anyway.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The S&P added 0.14% yesterday and the bond market declined to clap. The Dow gained 0.17%. The Nasdaq gained 0.11%. Nobody opened champagne.

The Russell 2000 slipped 0.11%, because small caps are the canaries of domestic credit, and right now they are canaries with a cough. Mega-caps got the trophy. Everyone else got a haircut.

This rally has a guest list, and the 10-year did not make it.

Here's my read:

Two peace headlines landed at once. The Trump-Xi summit extended the Busan Agreement through January 10, 2027. Iran offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within 7 days and restart nuclear talks. Crude noticed. WTI dropped 1.86% to $92.85 as the risk premium bled out.

The bond market watched all of it and did nothing. The 10-year edged up 0.7 bps to 5.169%. A yield that holds 5.17% through two pieces of good news is telling you exactly what it thinks of the good news.

Then there is gold. Gold hit $4,323.80, up 0.60%, on a day equities rose and a diplomatic risk-off catalyst unwound. Somebody is hedging something they do not trust yet. Likely the 5%+ yield regime. Possibly Warsh's brewing Fed restructuring. Gold refusing to sell off into a tailwind is the signal that matters.

Futures have the S&P adding another 0.20% to 7,782.25 and Nasdaq futures up 0.33% to 30,867.75. The level to hold is S&P 7,700. USTR Greer's Monday trade detail announcement is the swing factor. New Home Sales at 10:00 AM ET is the secondary yield check.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

Gold gains 0.60% on a day when crude sells off 1.86% and equities close fractionally higher. Buyers are defending $4,300 against two separate risk-off unwinds. That means the bid is structural, not reactive.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,300 intraday by 11:00 AM ET with the 10-year yield not breaking below 5.10%. That combination is institutional accumulation, not a flight-to-safety pop that reverses.

Kill switch: The 10-year drops decisively below 5.10% on a surprise dovish catalyst, sending capital out of gold and into TLT. Or Iran formally confirms Hormuz reopening before Monday's open, triggering a broader risk-on rotation that pulls GLD below $4,280. If either happens, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Gold (GLD): Hold or add on any morning dip toward $4,290. If yields keep climbing, gold could catch a second bid.
  • Semiconductors and China tech (SOXX, KWEB, XLK): The Busan extension gives supply chains a defined tariff-free window into the holiday cycle. Buy SOXX on any early-session dip below yesterday's close. If Greer's Monday details add carve-outs, add more. If talks stall, trim quickly.
  • Energy (XLE, USO): The Hormuz offer is not a done deal, but the risk premium is unwinding fast. WTI is down from $94.61 the session before. Stay underweight XLE until next week's OPEC+ JMMC review provides a floor. If Iran talks collapse over the weekend, re-enter on the pop.
  • Small caps (IWM): Stay away from it. The Russell 2000 was the only major index to fall yesterday, and debt-heavy small caps have no relief catalyst with the 10-year at 5.17%.
  • Long-duration Treasuries (TLT): Do not chase. The 10-year edged up 0.7 bps, so there is no reversal to buy yet. TLT becomes a trade when yields break lower with conviction, not when a headline says they might.
