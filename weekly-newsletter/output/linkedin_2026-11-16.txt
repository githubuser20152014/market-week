Nvidia lost its CEO last week. This week the Treasury Secretary wants to help pick the next one.

Here is why you should not trust Friday's calm.

The Dow gained 0.1%. The Nasdaq lost 0.9%. The S&P 500 slipped 0.4% to 7,561. That looks like a shrug. It is not. It is the sound of money quietly leaving the one corner of the market that carried the last two years.

Three markets did not buy the story.

Bonds: the 10-year yield climbed 5 basis points to 5.15%. CME FedWatch now prices 100% odds of a December rate hike after October PPI posted its biggest annual jump since late 2022. When cash pays 5.15% and the Fed is still hiking, long-duration tech multiples have a math problem. Math does not take meetings.

Gold: it sat at $3,886 through all of it. Not a rally, not a collapse. Gold is holding while yields climb, which is not supposed to happen. Somebody with a very large balance sheet is buying the dip and not telling anyone.

Crude: WTI slipped to $88.56 as the IEA projected a Q4 surplus and shrugged at Iran's strikes on Bahrain. The geopolitical premium is getting harder to defend.

The corporate proof point: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent demanded a board shakeup at Nvidia, an outside CEO search, and a 10-day export audit. A governance vacuum just became a national-security compliance event. By Monday morning Tokyo was down 2.4% on a surprise GDP contraction, and Seoul's KOSPI was down 3.6% with SK Hynix off 5-8%. Asia did not wait for the audit.

The second-order story nobody is covering: the IMF's chief just warned the global economy is "standing on one leg" as AI circular financing hits $1 trillion. Chipmakers fund their customers, customers buy the chips, and everyone books the revenue. If the anchor of that loop is in governance limbo, the problem is not one stock. It is the plumbing.

So which market got it right? Bonds say rates stay high. Gold says someone knows something. Equities say it is fine. One of them is wrong, and the bond market has a better track record than the Nasdaq.
