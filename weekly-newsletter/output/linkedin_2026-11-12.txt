Wednesday's rally was real. It also lasted about as long as a dinner reservation.

The S&P 500 jumped 1.31% to 7,619.38. Semis ripped 5.7%. Nvidia closed up 2.87%. Then the Wall Street Journal reported Nvidia's board is preparing to oust its CEO, and the stock lost 7% to 9% after hours. Roughly $300 billion of market cap, gone between the entree and the check.

Here is why you shouldn't trust the green screen. Three markets weren't celebrating.

Bonds. The 10-year closed Tuesday at 4.99% after a hot October CPI, and the bond market was shut Wednesday for Veterans Day. So the whole equity rally happened with Treasuries on mute. Waller is already talking about a 50 basis point hike in December. Stocks cheered. The rate market never got to vote.

Gold. It fell 1.1% to $3,988 on a stronger dollar and Fed hike bets, and futures are lower again this morning. It is now 24% below the January record. The safe haven is not having a safe week.

Crude. WTI settled at $86.53, up 0.57%. Oil used to drag yields down when it moved. That link is broken. Cheaper-ish oil did nothing for rates.

The corporate proof point is Nvidia itself. A company that rose on Wednesday afternoon and fell on Wednesday night is not a demand story. Nobody cancelled a single chip order. This is a key-person problem, and key-person risk does not show up in a P/E ratio. Nvidia has confirmed nothing, filed no 8-K, and the board reportedly met into the night.

The second-order story is Europe. The DAX is up 0.86% and the CAC is up 0.71% this morning, helped by a UK Q3 GDP beat of 0.7%. Neither index has Nvidia in it. Futures say the damage is concentrated: Nasdaq down 0.77%, Dow up 0.06%. The sell-off is one stock deep.

So which market got it right? Stocks said everything is fine. Bonds, gold and a hot CPI print said the cost of money is going up. I side with the dissenters. A rally that needs the rate market to stay asleep is a rally on borrowed time.
