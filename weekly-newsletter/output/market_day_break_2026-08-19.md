## The Brief

Wall Street had a bond problem and a Hormuz problem on the same Tuesday, and it picked the wrong one to worry about first. Gold ripped to $4,415 while stocks fell, which is the market quietly admitting the safe-haven trade is not a drill. Futures are pretending everything is fine this morning. KOSPI just fell 5.8% to tell you otherwise.

The Nasdaq dropped **1.3% to 26,289**, the S&P 500 fell **0.7% to 7,691**, and the Russell 2000 lost **-1.3%**, so nobody was spared. Gold surged **1.1% to $4,415** anyway, because when equities and yields both look ugly, gold does not ask permission. The 10-year slipped to **4.706%**, the dollar softened to **99.40**, and WTI held above **$85**, a combination that smells less like a growth scare and more like stagflation trying on a new outfit.

The US-Iran truce expired without so much as a phone call, the [UAE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UAE) cut Iran off financially after missiles landed near its shipping lanes, and a commercial tanker took a direct hit in the Strait of Hormuz, a corridor that moves roughly 20% of the world's oil. Energy desks are now pricing actual war risk, not diplomatic theater. Meanwhile the 30-year yield punched to **5.335%, a 19-year high**, and someone had to pay for that: Nvidia, Broadcom, and Micron got the bill, because when the discount rate climbs, the stocks pricing in the most distant profits fall hardest. **Semiconductor and [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) hardware names bore the brunt** of that arithmetic.

Target earnings and the **release of July FOMC minutes** both land today, and either one can end the truce. A hawkish tilt plus a Target miss sends the S&P testing 7,600. Watch the 10-year: back above 4.75% and this morning's bounce is over before lunch.
