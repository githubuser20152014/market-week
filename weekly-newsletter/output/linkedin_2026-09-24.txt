The economy just posted its fastest growth in over five years. The stock market sold off anyway.

Read that again before you trust any headline about yesterday. Good news is now bad news, and the bond market wrote the rule.

The S&P 500 fell 0.75%. The Nasdaq lost 1.13%. Small caps dropped 1.77%. Equities want you to believe this was a wobble. The other markets disagree.

BONDS: The 10-year Treasury yield jumped nearly 15 basis points in a single session to 5.11%, the highest since 2007. Bonds never bought the rally. A flash PMI of 58.4 and a New York Fed president calling another hike "reasonable" gave them all the proof they needed. October hike odds went from about 53% to roughly 77% in a day.

GOLD: Down 1.33% while the dollar climbed. The classic safe haven got hit by the same math as everything else. When cash pays 5%, a shiny rock that pays nothing has to work a lot harder for your attention.

CRUDE: Up 1.8% on Strait of Hormuz risk. It was the only major market that ignored the rate scare entirely.

The proof point: Newmont fell 4.1% yesterday, more than the S&P, as metals slid and the dollar strengthened. Even the miners couldn't dodge the yield.

The second-order story nobody is covering: oil is feeding the Fed. Energy prices push inflation, inflation pushes hike odds, and a possible US diesel export ban has big business writing joint letters to the White House. Last week's Fed hike already cited the energy-price shock. Crude is the input the equity market forgot to price, and the Fed has noticed.

Asia felt it overnight too. Nifty 50 dropped 1.64% and Australia's ASX lost 0.72%, while Japan's Nikkei shrugged and gained 1.30%. Global markets are not agreeing on much, except that 5% is a number that changes behavior.

The verdict: stocks say growth is fine. Bonds say the Fed is not done. Which market got it right?

Bonds. They have the better track record, and they were the only ones who read the PMI as a warning instead of a trophy. Equities are catching down to the news.
