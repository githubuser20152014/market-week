## The Brief

The Dow just printed a record high and celebrated by torching tech. SpaceX's disclosure of **$18.4 billion** in annual orbital-AI capex knocked the Nasdaq down 0.8%, because nothing spooks a growth investor like a growth company that actually spends money on growth. Gold's 1.8% pop to **$4,321** on a flat dollar and flat yields says the smart money trusts the Hormuz ceasefire a lot less than the headlines do.

The Dow hit a new all-time high of **54,349**, up 0.5%, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.2% to 7,723.55 and the Nasdaq fell 0.8% to 26,363, because apparently record highs and selloffs now share a ticker tape. The Russell 2000 dropped 0.6% to 3,019, confirming this was a calculated flight to value, not a broad loss of nerve. Gold surged 1.8% to **$4,321**, WTI crude idled at $75.74 like it had somewhere better to be, the 10-year yield ticked down 1 basis point to 4.617%, and the USD index barely twitched at 99.74.

The catalyst is a two-headed story, and both heads are expensive. SpaceX's **$18.4 billion in annual capex** for orbital [AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AI) infrastructure rattled every high-multiple tech name by association: when the marquee grower reveals what scaling actually costs, the market reprices the whole genre and the Nasdaq picks up the tab. Palantir's 30% surge and Caterpillar's 5.6% gain from the prior session kept compounding into industrials and value, because tractors don't need $18 billion satellite farms to turn a profit. The Hormuz diplomatic progress, with US officials signaling readiness to return to "commitments" per Iran's own statement, explains why crude did not spike: the risk premium is already half-priced out. Gold's 1.8% gain on a do-nothing dollar and do-nothing yields is the tell: **gold is not trading as an inflation hedge right now, it's trading as a hedge against Washington's optimism being premature**.
