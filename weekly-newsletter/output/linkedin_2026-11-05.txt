The Dow added 1,570 points yesterday. Democrats took Congress and Wall Street decided gridlock is a feature.

Here's why I wouldn't trust the celebration. Three markets heard the same news and reached a different conclusion.

Bonds: the 10-year yield fell 8 bps to 4.89%. The bond market read a divided Congress as less deficit spending and priced it in within hours. That is a real signal. It is also the only market that fully bought the story.

Gold: it settled up 3.4% at $4,073 on a day the S&P 500 rose 1.6%, then added another 1.6% overnight. A relief rally is supposed to drain the safe haven. Gold did the opposite. Someone is buying protection while everyone else buys the dip.

Crude: WTI fell 8.3% to $88.12 after Trump acknowledged the Hormuz energy shock cost Republicans at the ballot box. The White House now has a political reason to keep oil cheap. The war premium didn't fade. It got evicted.

The corporate proof point: Qualcomm fell 7% on a weak forecast, blaming a memory shortage hitting smartphone sales. Broad indexes were celebrating while a company with a front-row seat to the supply chain was warning about it.

The second-order story is Korea. The KOSPI fell more than 5% overnight and SK Hynix dropped about 9.5% as chipmakers sold off. Cheaper oil helps the economies that import it. The memory shortage is hitting the economies that make the chips. Hong Kong rose 3.7% and India rose 2.4% in the same session. Same peace dividend, very different winners.

One more thing worth sitting with. Cramer's warning on CNBC was that investors may be reading too much into the post-election rally. When the loudest voice on the floor tells you to slow down, the rally has probably already priced in the good news. Today's Bank of England decision and tomorrow's jobs report are the next tests, and neither one cares who controls the House.

So which market got it right? Equities are pricing a soft landing with cheaper energy. Gold is pricing something nobody wants to say out loud. I'd side with the market that isn't throwing a party. Equities have the better mood. Gold has the better record this year.
