## The Brief

Trump paused the airstrikes on Iran and oil did what oil does when a war-risk premium gets a partial refund: it fell off a cliff. WTI is down 6% to $83.89, Nasdaq futures are up 1.3%, and gold refused to get the memo, holding firm near $4,088 anyway. Relief rally, sure, but gold sticking around says the market isn't fully buying that the Hormuz story is over, and the FOMC meeting starting today is about to test how far this relief can actually run.

The S&P closed at **7,411.98, up just 0.05%**, which is a rounding error dressed up as a session: the Dow gained 0.46% while the Nasdaq dropped 0.64% and small caps got left holding the bag, down 0.35%. The real headline is **WTI crude collapsing 6.1% to $83.89**. Gold didn't care, edging up 0.51% to **$4,088.20** anyway, the 10Y yield slipped 3.2 basis points to 4.647%, and the dollar drifted to 101.40 like nothing happened.

Blame Trump's suspension of the two-week bombing campaign against Iranian military targets, three days after Brent cleared $100 on Red Sea tanker strikes. The **Strait of Hormuz** premium evaporated the second the strikes stopped, gutting energy names and handing airlines and consumer discretionary stocks a fuel-cost gift they didn't ask for. The Fed holding at 3.5%-3.75% was already priced in, but with crude back under $85, Chair Kevin Warsh just got a lot less political cover to sound hawkish.

## What it means for you

Energy exposure ([XLE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLE)) just lost its two-week tailwind and upstream margins compress accordingly, but **the transportation and consumer trade is back on the table**: airlines ([JETS](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/JETS)) and consumer discretionary ([XLY](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLY)) just got a fuel bill nobody had to ask for. The real tell is gold ([GLD](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GLD)) refusing to sell off into a genuine risk-on catalyst, which is the kind of anomaly that should make you pay attention, not shrug. **Bonds never fully bought the geopolitical panic** either, and with oil down, [TLT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TLT) picks up a quiet tailwind from softer inflation expectations and the FOMC hold. The Nasdaq's tech capex jitters from Alphabet and Tesla earnings haven't gone anywhere, so a gap-up open in [QQQ](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/QQQ) is running into resistance it hasn't earned yet.

S&P futures are up 0.85% to **7,510.50**, Nasdaq futures up 1.3%: a strong open, on paper. APAC didn't need convincing, with the [ASX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ASX) 200 leading at +1.4% and the Hang Seng and KOSPI each up roughly 1%. Europe is sitting on its hands, flat and clearly waiting to see what New York does first. The **FOMC meeting begins today**, and Chair Warsh's Wednesday press conference is the one event that actually matters this week, everything else is noise dressed up as a data print. The 9:15 AM Industrial Production number could still jostle the first hour if it surprises. Watch whether the Nasdaq gap holds above 25,200 through midday: that's the difference between a durable rotation and a one-day sugar high.
