Gridlock Was Supposed to Be Bullish. The S&P 500 Lost 3.1% by Friday.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Democrats swept Congress. The Dow celebrated on Wednesday, because apparently gridlock is a bullish catalyst. Then Thursday showed up.

A Democratic House and Senate means oversight of AI data-center power use, tighter export controls, and War Powers limits on Iran policy. The Nasdaq fell 5.5% in a single day. The S&P 500 finished the week down 3.1%. The VIX closed at 25.6, up 34.6% on the week. The rally lasted roughly one news cycle.

Meanwhile oil lost 11.3% and gold cleared $4,000. Two assets, opposite directions, one election. Somebody in this market is wrong, and it is not the commodity that costs $4,066 an ounce.

Here's my read:

The macro driver is simple: the political cycle just made the AI trade harder to underwrite. Chip names took the worst of the liquidation, the Russell 2000 fell 2.7%, and the Dow lost 1.4% on the week even after its election-day party. This was not a narrow tech wobble. It was a repricing of every assumption that depended on Washington staying out of the way. Washington has now entered the chat.

International equities: the rest of the world did not get the memo. In Europe, the CAC 40 gained 2.3% and the Euro Stoxx 50 added 0.6%, the DAX was flat at +0.1%, and the FTSE 100 slipped 0.5%. That is not strength. That is Europe being the cleanest shirt in a dirty laundry basket, with eurozone inflation at 4.0% and the Bank of England hiking to 4.0% on an 8-1 vote. In Asia-Pacific, the Hang Seng surged 5.1% to 24,481.86 on Beijing leaning on banks and brokerages for market support. The Nikkei collapsed 10.7% to 57,434.77, entering a bear market on the Thursday tech unwind. MSCI EM dropped 4.4%, and the ASX 200 added 1.3%. Pick a continent. Any continent. They all disagree.

Currencies, commodities and metals: the dollar index closed up 0.3% at 102.3, with the 10-year yield at 4.86%. The Swiss franc gained 1.2% and sterling added 0.5%. The yen edged up 0.1%, the euro slipped 0.1% to 1.1143, and the Australian dollar fell 1.4%, the worst of the majors. In commodities, WTI started the week above $100 and fell 11.3% to $90.83 as Qatari mediators floated a Hormuz transit deal and Trump admitted that energy inflation cost Republicans Congress. Nothing lowers oil prices like losing an election. Gold closed near $4,066, up 4.5%, silver gained 3.4% to $51.80, and natural gas added 3.8%. Bullion was the only adult in the building.

And Friday's jobs report did not help the mood. October payrolls came in at 165,000 against a consensus near 60,000, with unemployment at 4.1%. Yields rose, the dollar firmed, and December Fed hike odds went up. Good news for the economy. Terrible news for anyone hoping the Fed would blink.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

The signal: gold broke $4,000 this week on three catalysts at once, and then held it after the hottest jobs report in months handed the dollar a tailwind. A US Congress now built for debt-ceiling brinkmanship. ECB President Lagarde announcing an early exit, right as eurozone inflation hit 4.0%. A Bank of England telling markets UK CPI hits 5.0% by mid-2027.

This is not momentum chasing. The $3,900 floor held through Monday's margin-call liquidations, through crude's disinflationary plunge, and through Friday's yield spike. When an asset absorbs that many downside catalysts without breaking, the signal is directional. A divided Washington virtually guarantees a 2027 debt-ceiling standoff, and central banks have been net buyers of gold for twelve straight quarters. Dips toward $4,020 to $4,050 are the buy-the-pullback level, not a regime change.

Confirms: spot gold holds $4,000 on a weekly closing basis through the aftermath of Friday's jobs print, and the 10-year real yield stays below 2.0%.

Kill switch: the 10-year yield back above 5.0% and the dollar index above 104, which sends gold toward $3,920 and means you get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • GLD: don't you dare sell it on a jobs-print wobble. Add on dips toward $4,020 to $4,050.
  • TLT: stay away from long duration. The 10-year is at 4.86% and December hike odds just moved higher. Keep duration underweight until the post-payrolls repricing settles.
  • EWH: the Hang Seng's 5.1% week had actual Beijing support behind it. Keep a measured position and watch the PBOC liquidity details before adding size.
  • QQQ: the VIX is 25.6 and the AI semiconductor de-grossing is still digesting. Wait for the VIX to close below 20 before rebuilding tech exposure.
  • USO: the trade nobody is talking about is that $90.83 WTI is a repriced risk premium, not a demand collapse. Trim leveraged upstream names and lean toward integrated majors with low breakevens.
