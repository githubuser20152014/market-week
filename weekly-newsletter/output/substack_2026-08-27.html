Nvidia's $108B Guide Had a Great Night. The Dow Didn't Care.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Nvidia printed $96.2 billion in Q2 revenue, up 106% year-over-year, and guided to $108 billion for Q3. Futures know exactly what to do with a beat like that: Nasdaq futures up 1.1%, S&P up 0.48%.

The Dow, dead flat, wants nothing to do with the party.

That's the tell. This is a chip-specific mania, not a market-wide risk-on move. Gold sat at $4,635 through yesterday's chop and the 10Y ticked up to 4.664% anyway, because nobody told the hedges the AI trade solved anything. Chair Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote lands Friday, and the bond market has already made up its mind not to care about Nvidia's beat.

Here's my read:

Yesterday's drag was pre-earnings jitters stacked on Jackson Hole dread. The 10Y creeping toward 4.70% did the actual damage: higher long rates hate duration, and tech is nothing but duration, so allocators sold first and asked questions never. The S&P 500 closed at 7,675, down a rounding error of 0.02%. The Dow shed 0.21% to 53,464, the Russell 2000 dropped 0.14% to 3,006, small caps doing what small caps do whenever rates so much as clear their throat. Then Nvidia showed up and answered anyway: CEO Jensen Huang confirmed Vera Rubin silicon is in full production. That is the entire gap between flat futures and Nasdaq futures up 1.1% overnight.

The One Trade: Long $SOXX

Nvidia's $96.2B print and $108B Q3 guide confirm the Blackwell ramp is real. Every HBM memory supplier, advanced packaging house, and optical interconnect name in SOXX just got a forward-revenue upgrade without reporting a single number themselves.

Confirms: SOXX holds above its pre-market implied open level through 10:30 AM ET and the 10Y yield stays below 4.70%, both conditions together mean the AI hardware bid is sustaining, not fading into the gap.

Kill switch: 10Y Treasury yield breaks above 4.70% intraday or Warsh delivers hawkish remarks in any pre-keynote symposium session today. That flips this into a multiple-compression reversal, and you want to be out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Gold at $4,635, up 0.81% into a risk-on futures tape. Don't you dare sell it: someone is still quietly hedging Hormuz tanker risk and the Operation Economic Outcast sanctions mess while the tape parties.
  • KOSPI surged 1.53% on Samsung and SK Hynix HBM demand riding Nvidia's coattails, while the ASX 200 dropped 0.99% because rate-sensitive sectors don't care whose earnings beat.
  • Stay away from QQQ into morning strength: chasing long-duration software here is a bigger bet on Warsh's Friday keynote than on Nvidia's quarter.
  • The trade nobody is talking about: XLE. WTI is holding near $82 while Hormuz corridor talks stay unresolved, and a breakdown in Qatari-Iranian mediation flips energy from laggard to leader fast.
  • Avoid IWM: Russell 2000 underperformed again, and with the 10Y near 4.664% and Warsh's framework favoring higher term premiums, the rate headwind on small-cap borrowing costs is structural, not temporary.
