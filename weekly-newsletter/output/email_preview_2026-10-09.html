The Brief

Nasdaq lost 1.25%. Gold gained. The 10-year dropped 4.6 basis points. Bonds and bullion are whispering caution while equity futures bounce like nothing happened. Houthi drones hit Riyadh airport and lit WTI up 3.6%, and that fear premium did not go home overnight. Europe is rallying on a German industrial rebound, which makes this open borrowed confidence with a 5.23% yield as collateral.

The Dow gained 0.10%. The Nasdaq lost 1.25% to 27,193, the S&P 500 slid 0.47% to 7,765, and the Russell 2000 did absolutely nothing. Congratulations to the index that stayed home. WTI surged 3.6% to $91.49 on drone strikes at Riyadh airport and tankers getting harassed near Hormuz. Gold crept up to $4,157. The 10-year eased 4.6 basis points to 5.231% after a $22 billion 30-year auction cleared at 2.54x bid-to-cover, and the dollar index slipped to 102.14.

The tech selloff was not random. A 5.23% 10-year is a gravitational anchor on long-duration growth, a slow leak in the tire of every future earnings stream. The Houthi strike on Riyadh drove the oil spike, stacked on Gulf of Mexico hurricane shut-ins that were already tightening supply. The 30-year auction went fine, which kept long yields off their worst and the equity unwind from getting uglier. The September FOMC minutes put a 38% implied probability on another October hike. The Fed is still holding the leash.

What it means for you

Crude at $90+ keeps energy names (XLE) in focus as free cash flow machines, though Delta's fuel-cost warning this morning proves the trade has a flip side. Airlines are the canary and the canary is coughing. TLT is a tactical minefield with the 10-year at 5.23% and another hike possible on October 27. Do not add duration. Gold at $4,157 cash and $4,207 in futures is the hedge for everything at once: geopolitics, Fed risk, and Krugman's warning that France may be too big to save, with OAT spreads near 80 basis points over Bunds. Short-duration Treasuries (SGOV, BIL) yielding near 4.95% are the boring carry trade, and boring is currently winning.

S&P futures are up 0.40% to 7,847, Nasdaq futures are up 0.83%, and European bourses are rallying 1.0 to 1.3% on German industrial production and a cleaner bond picture. KOSPI fell 2.6% and nobody seems to care, which is exactly when you should. Gold futures are already at $4,207, up 1.2%. If equities rally and gold holds those gains, somebody is still buying insurance. WTI has pulled back 1.1% to $90.45 pre-market on signs that military escalation near Hormuz is being deferred. Deferred, not cancelled. The swing factor is weekend tanker passage data through Hormuz and any Saudi-led coalition response to the Riyadh attack.

The One Trade

GLD - Long

Gold futures are up 1.2% to $4,207 overnight while equity futures also rally, a divergence that exposes real institutional hedging demand: the Houthi strike on Riyadh, French fiscal crisis risks, and a Fed that may still hike in October are all live simultaneously, and gold is not giving back a single dollar of its geopolitical bid.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,175 cash equivalent through 11am ET while SPY is also green. Simultaneous strength in both confirms institutional safe-haven buying, not just a risk-off rotation. Kill switch: WTI drops below $88 on confirmed Hormuz de-escalation and GLD retreats below $4,140, signaling the geopolitical premium is unwinding rather than structural safe-haven demand holding.
