Stocks rose 0.14% yesterday. Here's why you shouldn't trust it.

Two peace headlines landed in the same session. A Trump-Xi trade truce, extended through January 10, 2027. Iran offering to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within 7 days and restart nuclear talks. Textbook risk-on. Equities responded by adding a few hundredths of a percent and calling it a day.

Three markets did not go along with the story.

Bonds. The 10-year yield edged up 0.7 bps to 5.169%. Two pieces of good news and it went nowhere useful. It still wants 5.17% to hold duration. That is not a market that thinks the coast is clear.

Gold. Up 0.60% on a day diplomacy was supposedly working. Gold is what people buy when they are hedging something they do not trust yet. Someone is still hedging.

Crude. Down 1.86% to $92.85 as the Hormuz risk premium bled out. This was the one market that took the peace headline at face value.

The proof is in the tape itself. The Russell 2000 was the only major index to fall, down 0.11%, while the S&P 500, the Dow and the Nasdaq all rose. Small caps carry the debt load that a 5%+ 10-year makes expensive. When a rally skips the companies most sensitive to rates, it is not a broad rally. It is a selective one.

Now the angle nobody is covering: supply chains. The truce buys runway for semiconductor and critical minerals negotiations and gives trans-Pacific shippers a breather heading into Q4. Details land Monday from USTR Greer. Hormuz talks could change what it costs to move all of it. Everything that travels by ship cares about both headlines, and almost nobody is pricing them together.

So which market got it right?

Equities say the peace is priced. Bonds and gold say the peace is not the point. I'll side with the two that are not cheering. The bond market has a long memory and a short list of things it believes.
