Oil Cratered 5.7% on Peace Talks. Gold Added 1.5% Anyway.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

The market got exactly what it wanted yesterday, a preview of peace, and priced it in like it was already signed. Oil got demolished, down 5.7% to $75.77, the reward for diplomatic progress that has not technically happened yet.

The S&P closed at a record 7,736.52, Palantir ripped nearly 30% on an earnings beat, and Caterpillar tacked on 5.6%, dragging the Dow up 907 points. Everyone got the memo and traded accordingly.

Everyone except gold. It added 1.5% anyway, on a day custom-built to make it irrelevant. That is the entire story this morning.

Here's my read:

Oman-brokered talks between the US and Iran produced preliminary transit protocols for the Strait of Hormuz, and crude paid for it immediately: WTI's 5.7% single-session haircut is the market betting the deal holds. Lower oil relieves CPI pressure, which is why the 10-year yield dropped 5.9 basis points to 4.627% at the same time equities hit all-time highs, a bond-equity confirmation that does not happen often. Gold not joining the celebration is the tell that matters. It suggests the bid is coming from dollar credibility concerns and the structural fiscal backdrop, not the Iran headline, and that kind of buyer does not sell the moment a ceasefire gets announced. Watch SpaceX this morning too: SPCX dropped 8% after hours on a capex shock, $18.4 billion in one quarter, mostly AI infrastructure, and that is the live risk hanging over Nasdaq futures, which are sitting flat at -0.03% while S&P and Dow futures are both green. The Nikkei and KOSPI both ripped more than 3.6% overnight on the same Hormuz relief trade. The swing factor today: Disney's pre-market earnings and whether Muscat actually confirms the deal instead of just teasing it.

The One Trade: LONG $GLD

Gold adding 1.53% on a day the Hormuz premium evaporated and equities hit record highs exposes a bid that is structural, not fear-driven, and that asymmetry does not reverse overnight.

Confirms: GLD holds above $370 through the first hour of trading with the USD index staying below 100.50.
Kill switch: USD index breaks above 101.00 on a hawkish Fed speaker or surprise strong data print, signaling dollar demand is absorbing the gold bid, get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Trim or avoid adding to $XLE and $XOP into the open. The supply-risk premium is gone and WTI already ate the haircut for it, any confirmation from Muscat pushes it lower still.
  • $JETS is the cleanest Hormuz beneficiary in the market and don't you dare sell it on a confirmed Muscat statement, the jet fuel math is too direct.
  • $QQQ has a real morning headwind: the AI infrastructure spending fatigue narrative that just hit SpaceX could bleed into every high-multiple software name at the open. Watch whether it holds 510 in the first 30 minutes.
  • $TLT is a buy, and the trade nobody is talking about: yields fell while stocks hit records, a rare confirmation, and falling oil is the CPI catalyst that keeps it going.
