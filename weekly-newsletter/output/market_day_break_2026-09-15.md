## The Brief

The theme today is stagflation, plain and ugly. The FOMC is in session, the 10-year yield punched through 5% overnight, and WTI crude is holding above $103 because Saudi Arabia's main pipeline got drone-struck into silence. Equities sold off across the board, gold slipped as real yields rose, and the yen is weakening while the BOJ sits in a blackout that keeps the carry trade breathing for one more day. Pre-market futures are bouncing 0.5-0.7%, but T-note futures are down another half-point, so that bounce is rented, not owned.

Yesterday's close was a broad retreat, no survivors. The **S&P 500 fell 0.48% to 7,619.98**, the Nasdaq dropped 0.56% to 26,186, and the Dow shed 0.29% to 52,421: everybody lost, nobody's shocked. Small caps underperformed again, the **Russell 2000 down 0.40%** to 2,892, because small-caps are the canaries of domestic credit and this canary has a persistent cough. The 10-year Treasury yield actually slipped 1.4 basis points to **4.961%** at the cash close, but that was a head-fake: overnight T-note futures are already pricing a fresh leg higher, with yields breaching 5.05% in pre-market. Gold dropped 0.82% to **$4,316**, the dollar index ticked up to 99.60, and **WTI crude surged 1.73% to $103.14**, because a pipeline on fire beats a Fed press conference for setting the price of oil.

Two catalysts are running this show. First, the FOMC, with rate swaps pricing a **92% probability of a 25-basis-point hike** on Wednesday, fed by August CPI at 3.4% year-over-year and energy inflation that refuses to cooperate. Second, and louder: **Saudi Petroline**, a 1,200-kilometer artery that normally moves 5 million barrels a day, got taken out by drone strikes on pumping stations in Riyadh and Medina. Yanbu's storage tanks have 48 to 72 hours before they run dry. Call it a pressure-relief valve for the entire global oil market, and someone just welded it shut. With the **Strait of Hormuz** already carrying a war-risk premium, every refiner from Rotterdam to Seoul is now bidding for the same barrels. Oil and the 10-year moving in lockstep hasn't happened in seven years, and it is not a coincidence you want to see: that's a stagflation signal, not a growth signal, and stagflation is lethal for equity multiples.

## What it means for you

For ETF investors, none of this is ambiguous. **Bonds never bought the equity rally**, and the 10-year at five-year highs just confirmed the skepticism was correct. Duration is the enemy here: long-bond funds ([TLT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TLT)) are exposed because Chair Warsh's Wednesday Dot Plot is shaping up higher-for-longer, and no amount of hoping changes a dot plot. Energy is the one sector with a fundamental bid instead of a speculative one: the Petroline is down, and no FOMC statement refills a pipeline. Energy ETFs ([XLE](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLE), [XOP](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XOP)) and broad commodity funds ([DJP](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/DJP), [PDBC](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PDBC)) are where the real trade lives right now. Gold's pullback to $4,316 is just a real-yield story: rates up, opportunity cost up, gold down, simple math. But [BRICS](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BRICS) de-dollarization chatter and gold-backed settlement rails ([GLD](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GLD), [SGOL](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SGOL)) put a floor under it, so don't confuse a pullback with a breakdown. Utilities ([XLU](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XLU)) and REITs ([VNQ](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/VNQ)) are still priced like rate cuts are coming: they are not, and Wednesday is going to be a rude reminder.

Going into today, S&P futures are up 0.61% to 7,671 and Nasdaq futures are up 0.73% to 29,364, a modestly positive open on paper. But T-note futures are down 0.51% at the same time, so the bond market got the invite and didn't show. APAC was uniformly red overnight: **Hang Seng fell 1.00%, Nifty 50 dropped 1.19%, KOSPI slid 0.85%**, and [ASX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ASX) 200 lost 0.88%, all pricing in the same yield and energy shock New York already ate yesterday. Watch the **10-year yield**: below 5.10% and the futures bounce has legs into the afternoon. Above 5.10% on heavy volume, equity futures reverse and Wednesday becomes a sell-the-news event no matter what the Fed actually says. Treasury Secretary Bessent testifies today too, a live wildcard for the dollar.
