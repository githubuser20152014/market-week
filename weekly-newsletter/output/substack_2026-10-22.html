Nasdaq Gained 1.05% Wednesday. Futures Took It Back by Breakfast. Oil Is $99.46.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Tesla cut capex. Lam Research guided soft. Meta asked Wall Street to trust its AI spending, and Wall Street asked for a receipt.

Wednesday's close was a victory lap taken one hour too early. The S&P 500 gained 0.58% to 7,757. The Nasdaq added 1.05% to 26,703. Then the earnings dropped, and Nasdaq futures are now down 1.76% before you have finished the coffee.

Wednesday's gains lasted about as long as a Tesla capex target.

Here's my read:

The gains were built on sand. Lam Research plunged 9% on soft wafer-fab tool guidance. Tesla slid 5% and pushed the Cybercab and the humanoid robots back a few more quarters. Meta edged lower. Three misses at record valuations, and the market has stopped paying for stories.

Meanwhile, the Gulf did not get the memo about calm. The State Department ordered non-essential diplomats out of Saudi Arabia. The UK sent RAF jets to guard Saudi airspace against Houthi threats. WTI jumped 2.38% to $99.46 and Brent is pressing $112.

Gold fell 1.09% to $4,105. That is not a verdict on safe havens. That is leveraged tech and crypto longs selling their winners to cover Nasdaq losses. The thesis did not break. The accounts did.

The 10-year yield sits at 5.146%, which makes every homebuilder flinch. The yen woke up and chose violence: USD/JPY fell to 156.93. Asia saw it coming. KOSPI lost 3.52%, Hang Seng lost 3.03%, Nikkei lost 2.65%.

The One Trade: LONG $XLE

The State Department evacuation of Saudi Arabia and the RAF deployment are direct threats to Gulf infrastructure. WTI at $99.46 with Brent near $112 prices in a supply premium, and that premium does not reverse on a single diplomatic headline.

Confirms: WTI holds above $98.00 through the 11 AM ET check and XLE opens within 1% of Wednesday's close. That means energy is absorbing the tech selloff instead of joining it.

Kill switch: A Pentagon or UK MoD statement confirming a credible de-escalation framework before noon ET, or WTI breaking back below $96 on a surprise inventory build. Either one kills the geopolitical premium, so get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Stay away from the open gap in tech. Reduce QQQ and XLK. If Nasdaq futures hold above 29,200 by 10:30 AM ET, trim. If they break 29,000, cut harder: the next support is thin.
  • Don't you dare sell gold on a margin-call flush. The $4,075 to $4,100 pocket is where central-bank buyers have stepped in twice this month. GLD is an accumulation zone unless WTI drops below $95 and the dollar index reclaims 102.5.
  • Add to XOP on any dip below the open. The energy trade has legs into Friday if WTI holds above $98.
  • The trade nobody is talking about: defense. Wait for Lockheed's print. If LMT confirms backlog and raises guidance, ITA becomes a conviction long next to energy. If PG shows pricing-power erosion, XLP is no safe haven today.
  • Stay away from rate-sensitive real estate. XHB, VNQ and XLRE face a 5.146% 10-year, homebuilder confidence at 14-year lows, and housing starts down 5.2% MoM. A move above 5.20% makes it worse.
  • Level to watch: 7,700 on the S&P 500 is first support. Lose it and 7,650 is next.
