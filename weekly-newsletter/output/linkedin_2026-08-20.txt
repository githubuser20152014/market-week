The S&P added 0.21% yesterday and gold added 1.2% on the same tape. That's not two markets agreeing, that's one of them lying.

Here's what the dissenting markets were saying while equities congratulated themselves on a green close:

Bonds: The 30-year Treasury yield touched 5.335%, its highest print since 2007, and the U.S. Treasury responded by announcing it will at least double its long-bond buybacks starting September 9. The 10-year fell 5.3 basis points on the news, but T-Note futures still dipped even as yields fell, the bond market took the rescue and shrugged.

Gold: Gold surged 1.2% to $4,543 on the same day stocks rose. That combination, gold up and equities up together, isn't a risk-on cheer. It's institutional money hedging something the equity desk hasn't priced in yet.

Crude: WTI held above $86 as the Strait of Hormuz standoff hardened, tanker traffic at three-month lows and the UAE severing all trade ties with Iran. Crude didn't need to spike to make the point. It just needed to hold, and it held.

The proof point nobody's arguing with: a government doesn't double its own bond buyback program because everything is fine. It does it because the 30-year just hit its highest yield in nearly two decades and someone had to intervene first.

The story nobody's connecting: Bitcoin and ether ripped overnight on crypto regulatory clarity out of Congress, a rally built on certainty. Gold ripped on the opposite, a rally built on doubt. Two flights of capital, same 24 hours, completely different reasons, and most portfolios are only watching one of them.

So which market got it right yesterday, equities pricing in a rescue or gold pricing in doubt? Gold. A government doesn't rescue a bond market that's actually fine, and gold has been paying attention to that fact longer than the S&P has.
