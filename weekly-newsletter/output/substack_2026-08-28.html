Yields Hit 4.67% Before Warsh Said a Word. Gold Didn't Wait Either.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Kevin Warsh hasn't said a word yet. The bond market already voted anyway.

Ten-year yields climbed to 4.67% yesterday, pricing in a Fed chair comfortable with elevated term premiums before he's even at the Jackson Hole podium. Equities didn't get the memo, closing broadly higher on Nvidia's earnings afterglow.

Gold went up 1% on the same day. That's the part nobody's talking about. Warsh speaks today. The market already decided what he's going to say.

Here's my read:

Yesterday's scoreboard: S&P 500 at 7,730.99 (+0.72%), Nasdaq +1.57%, Dow +0.20%, Russell 2000 +0.28%. Gold at $4,656 (+1.00%), the 10-year yield up to 4.672%, WTI slipping to $83.47, dollar dead flat at 99.18. Two catalysts did the work: Nvidia's $96.2B Q2 print, Data Center revenue up 117% year-over-year, Q3 guidance of $108B, acted like jet fuel under the Nasdaq. And Warsh, whose Jackson Hole debut lands later today: preview coverage frames him as comfortable with elevated term premiums and allergic to rigid forward guidance, and the bond market leaned that way before he's even opened his mouth. Ten-year yields at 4.67% are a bet on what he says, not a reaction to it. Strait of Hormuz corridor talks eased crude's war premium, snapping WTI's two-week win streak on the mere suggestion of diplomacy.

The One Trade: Long $GLD

Gold decoupling from the usual rate/dollar correlation, up on a day yields rose and the dollar sat still, signals structural allocation, not a tactical flight-to-safety bid.

Confirms: GLD holds above $4,630 through the New Home Sales print at 10:00 AM ET and through Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks later today, without giving back the overnight gain.

Kill switch: Gold breaks $4,600 on a closing basis, particularly if Warsh's actual remarks spike the 10-year yield above 4.75% and the dollar index reclaims 99.80, restoring the inverse correlation and invalidating the decoupling thesis. Get out fast.

What else I'm watching

  • Marvell beat estimates by 37% and still dropped 8%. That's this market's version of a participation trophy: second-tier semis are priced for perfection, and perfection is a tall order on a Friday.
  • QQQ: cautious hold, not a chase. Nvidia's afterglow is largely priced into the tape, and Marvell just proved the market will punish anything short of perfect.
  • Stay away from TLT. Preview coverage of Warsh's framework already has T-Note futures down 0.30% pre-market, and that's before he's confirmed a single word of it.
  • XLE: neutral to slight underweight near-term. Hormuz corridor talks deflate the war premium, though the $80/bbl floor holds on steady inventory draws.
  • The trade nobody is talking about: ITB. Watch the 10:00 AM New Home Sales print, then watch Warsh, a dovish surprise later today is the tactical long entry rate-sensitive names have been waiting for.
