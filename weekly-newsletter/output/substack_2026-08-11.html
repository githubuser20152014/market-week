3.4% Inflation Forecast. $84 Oil. Take Your Pick of Disaster.

The Morning Brief · Market intelligence at the open

Wall Street woke up with two fires to put out and only one fire truck. WTI ripped through $84.23, gold is sitting near record highs at $4,430.80, and the S&P still closed red at 7,753.11. That's not a market shrugging off risk. That's a market that can't decide which disaster to hedge first.

The Strait of Hormuz talks collapsed, the Strategic Petroleum Reserve just fell below 300 million barrels for the first time since 1983, and July's inflation print lands this morning to settle the argument. Futures are barely green, which means nobody actually believes it's going to print clean.

WTI at a multi-week high and gold near record highs on the same day the S&P closed red isn't confidence, it's a hedge against two different disasters at once.

Here's my read:

Blame Hormuz first. The Strait of Hormuz talks between Iran and Oman collapsed into impasse, U.S. resistance killed the transit fee framework, and traders priced in prolonged rerouting risk and fatter war-risk premiums on every barrel moving through the Gulf. WTI crude surged 2.56% to $84.23, ripping clean through the $78-$82 range that had held for weeks. Then blame the data: nonfarm payrolls contracted 23,000 in July, yet the 10-year yield climbed to 4.699% anyway, a split that means the bond market doesn't believe inflation is beaten. The July FOMC vote split 9-3, with three hawks pushing for a hike, so don't expect the Fed to calm anyone down before the print. Gold refusing to sell off into a flat equity session while yields rise at the same time is the market hedging a stagflation surprise, not a coincidence.

The One Trade: Long $XLE

WTI broke above a multi-week $82 ceiling on a concrete supply catalyst, Hormuz impasse confirmed and the SPR at a 43-year low, and the disruption has no near-term diplomatic resolution. That makes this a structural move, not a spike.

Confirms: XLE holds above yesterday's close through the 10:30 AM ET check, with WTI futures staying above $83.50 after the inflation print absorbs the initial reaction.

Kill switch: WTI reverses back below $82 on a surprise Hormuz transit agreement or an emergency SPR release from the White House. If that happens, get out fast, the supply story is dead.

What else I'm watching

  • TLT: hold or trim ahead of the print. The 10-year at 4.699% with a hawkish 9-3 FOMC split means bonds have almost no cushion if inflation surprises above 3.4%, and a hot number pushes the 10-year toward 4.80% fast.
  • XLE / USO: add on the Hormuz breakdown. The SPR is at its lowest since 1983 and the Iran-Oman impasse shows no near-term resolution, this is a supply-risk catalyst, not momentum chasing.
  • GLD: stay long through the print and don't you dare sell it into the headline. Gold rising alongside yields on a flat dollar day is a stagflation hedge, and the asymmetry favors holding either way it prints.
  • IWM: stay away today. Small caps already led yesterday's losses and carry the most floating-rate debt in the index, wait for a confirmed soft print before touching this one.
  • QQQ / XLK: the trade nobody is positioned for yet. A print at or below 3.3% flips the September cut odds back on and sends the relief rally straight into tech, and Nvidia's $500 billion financing news has already primed the sector for it.
