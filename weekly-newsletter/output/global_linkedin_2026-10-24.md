Brent crude blew past $114 this week. Gold, the asset everyone buys when the world is on fire, fell 4.23%.

If that sounds like a market that makes sense to you, I have a bridge to sell you. Here is why you should not trust the headline story.

The headline says "AI wobble." The tape says something messier. Three markets refused to follow the script.

Bonds. The 10-year Treasury yield slid about 10 basis points to 4.99% while oil screamed higher. An inflation shock should push yields up. Instead, bond buyers showed up. Either they see a growth scare coming, or they know something the oil market has not priced yet.

Gold. A Houthi strike on Saudi oil infrastructure is a textbook safe-haven catalyst. Gold dropped to about $4,008 anyway. The likely culprit is forced selling: when the Nasdaq has a rough session, leveraged funds sell what they can, and winners get sold first. That is liquidation, not a verdict on gold.

Crude. WTI closed the week at $103.27, up 1.49%. A 60-day Russian diesel waiver was supposed to cool oil. It lasted about 24 hours. Sanctions relief does not secure a shipping lane or a production field.

The corporate proof point: Meta fell nearly 7% after floating new share structures to fund data centers. Lockheed Martin jumped about 5% on a record $292 billion order backlog. Procter & Gamble gained about 5% for selling toothpaste at higher prices. Investors stopped paying for promises and started paying for backlogs.

The second-order story nobody is covering: Europe. France's 10-year yield crossed 5% for the first time since 2002, cost-of-living protests spread from Paris to Tirana, and the IMF is hinting the ECB may need to hike again. A central bank considering a hike while citizens riot over prices is a policy bet with very little margin for error. The Euro Stoxx 50 fell 1.97% on the week. The Hang Seng fell 5.05%.

So which market got it right? Bonds say growth is cracking. Oil says supply is the problem. Gold says nothing, because it was busy being sold. My read: bonds, because they are the one market that is not being forced to sell anything.
