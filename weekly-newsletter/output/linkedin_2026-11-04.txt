Democrats took Congress overnight. Futures threw a fit, then crawled back to roughly +0.2% by morning.

Yesterday's rally looked tidy. Here is why I would not trust it.

The S&P 500 added 0.36% to 7,568. The Nasdaq gained 0.83%. The Dow lost 0.38%. Everyone agrees gridlock is good news. Three other markets disagree.

Bonds. The 10-year yield climbed 5.6 basis points to 4.97% while stocks celebrated. If gridlock really meant calm, yields would be falling. Instead the bond market watched the rally and politely declined. Five percent is a round number, and round numbers have a way of getting tested.

Gold. It slipped 0.7% to $3,940 even with the dollar index down 0.43% to 101.43. A weaker dollar usually gives gold a lift. It didn't. Someone is taking profits, or someone is nervous about rates.

Crude. WTI fell 0.9% to $95.21 on Qatar's draft US-Iran memorandum on Strait of Hormuz transit. A week ago crude was above $104. The geopolitical risk premium is leaving the building, and peace turns out to be bearish for oil.

The corporate proof point is AMD. Data-center revenue nearly doubled in a quarter, and chip stocks led the Nasdaq. That is the one part of the rally with an earnings print behind it.

The second-order story is memory. Apple is carrying a margin hangover from memory-chip costs, while the Kospi reversed a 4% intraday crash to close up 0.6%. The same input that hurts one balance sheet is the thing Korean buyers keep defending on every dip.

Meanwhile Europe is ripping. DAX and CAC are up 1.5%, Euro Stoxx 1.8%, with an ECB that has no leader after Lagarde's February exit and 3% inflation against 1% growth. Celebrating a US election they cannot vote in.

So which market got it right? Stocks say gridlock is a gift. Bonds, gold and crude say the story is more complicated. I side with the ones that are pricing risk, not vibes.
