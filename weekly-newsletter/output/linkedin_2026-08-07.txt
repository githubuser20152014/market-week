Jobless claims hit a two-year low yesterday. The Dow dropped 464 points anyway. Here's why you shouldn't trust the "strong economy" headline today.

Bonds didn't buy it. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed 5.3 basis points to 4.67%, the market pricing out whatever cover the Fed had left to cut rates in September. Good news for jobs, bad news for anyone still holding a rate-cut thesis.

Gold really didn't buy it. Up 3.1% to $4,373 on a day the dollar barely moved, that's not an inflation hedge, that's a bet that the Fed just got boxed in. Gold doesn't rally 3% on strong labor data unless the market thinks strong labor data is the problem, not the solution.

Crude got its own reason to move. Iran's draft plan to ban U.S. and Israeli vessels from the Strait of Hormuz added a fresh geopolitical premium, even with weeks of diplomatic progress in Muscat and Geneva that was supposed to be capping the upside.

The corporate proof point: Airbnb said it's seeing strong demand "across all regions," backing a better-than-expected forecast. The consumer isn't slowing down, which is exactly why yesterday's jobs data landed as a threat instead of a relief. A resilient consumer plus a red-hot labor market is not a recipe for September rate cuts.

The angle nobody's covering: the yen. Coordinated Treasury and Bank of Japan intervention pulled it from above 163 to as low as 155 against the dollar. That effect is already fading, the yen has drifted back to 158.38, and Nikkei exporters are about to find out that currency intervention has a shelf life.

So which market had it right yesterday: the labor data celebrating a two-year low in claims, or gold pricing in a Fed that just lost its excuse to ease? One of them gets proven wrong by this morning's payrolls print. Watch the 10-year at 4.70%, that's the line that decides it.
