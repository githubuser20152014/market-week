Framework Foundry / Markets / Global — Oct 24, 2026
France Crossed 5%. The Hang Seng Lost 5.05%. Europe Brought Riots, Asia Brought Hope.

Houthi drones hit Saudi Arabia's Shaybah oil field and the Dammam airport. Brent blew past $114 and WTI cleared $100. Gold, the thing you buy when the world is on fire, fell 4.23%. Sure. That tracks.

The market spent the week firing megacap tech. Meta fell nearly 7% for asking investors to fund data centers with new share classes. Lam Research dropped 9%+ on soft guidance. Tesla lost 5% on capex cuts. The AI spending party now has a cover charge, and nobody brought a wallet. Meanwhile Lockheed Martin jumped 5% on a $292 billion backlog, and Procter & Gamble jumped 5% for selling toothpaste at higher prices. Boring is the new alpha. At a 4.99% risk-free rate, it always was.

Europe brought the riots. France's 10-year yield crossed 5% for the first time since 2002, and cost-of-living protests ran from Paris to Tirana. The PBOC held loan prime rates for the 17th straight month, because stimulus is apparently a rumor. And a 60-day Russian diesel waiver bought Washington roughly 24 hours of lower oil. Money well spent.

What This Means For You

The week's defining moves were a Brent crude surge past $114/bbl on Houthi strikes targeting Saudi oil infrastructure and a sharp rotation out of AI-heavy megacap tech into defense and value names. Gold fell 4.23% to just above $4,000/oz as margin calls from the Nasdaq selloff forced liquidation of profitable bullion positions. The Hang Seng dropped 5.05% and the Nikkei lost 3.28% after the PBOC held rates unchanged for the 17th straight month and AI hardware earnings from Meta, Lam Research, and Tesla disappointed markets. For globally diversified investors, the key risk this week is concentrated in European equities via FEZ and EWG: France's 10-year yield broke 5% for the first time since 2002, the ECB decision on October 29 could widen sovereign spreads further, and the euro's structural weakness creates a currency drag on unhedged positions. The rotation signal in IWM outperforming QQQ by nearly 340 basis points this week deserves attention as a potential regime shift away from the AI concentration trade.

The One Trade

US small caps posted a 1.72% gain this week while the S&P 500 lost 1.03% and the Nasdaq fell 1.67%. That divergence is not noise. It is the first clean week of Russell 2000 outperformance during a broad large-cap selloff since the AI-driven concentration trade began unwinding. The catalyst mix is favorable for domestic small caps specifically. The Fed is on hold at 93% probability, removing the rate-hike tail risk that crushed small-cap earnings in 2022-2023. The rotation out of high-capex megacap tech and into value, defense, and domestically oriented businesses benefits smaller US names that have no exposure to AI data-center capex cycles or European fiscal contagion. The 10-year Treasury yield fell 10 basis points on the week to 4.99%, easing the discount-rate headwind on smaller balance sheets without signaling a growth collapse.

The macro backdrop supports a continued bid. US homebuilder confidence is at 14-year lows, but that reflects rate sensitivity in construction, not in the broader small-cap universe which tilts toward industrials, financials, and regional services. Defense and aerospace aftermarket spending is accelerating: Lockheed's $292 billion backlog and GE Aerospace's fourth consecutive beat-and-raise filter into the supply chains of smaller domestic contractors. The VIX at 19.45 and rising modestly is consistent with continued choppiness, but the Russell held its weekly high of 2,791 before closing at 2,775, a constructive pattern relative to large-cap indices that closed near their weekly lows.

Confirms: IWM holds above 2,750 and Russell 2000 continues to outperform QQQ on any subsequent week of tech earnings disappointment or Fed-hold confirmation. Risk: A sharp reversal in the 10-year yield back above 5.15% on hot inflation data, or a broad risk-off event from Middle East escalation that triggers indiscriminate selling across all equity size factors.

Equity Markets

The Nasdaq fell 1.67% and the S&P 500 dropped 1.03%. The Russell 2000 gained 1.72% and the Dow added 0.46%. Same week, same country, opposite verdicts. Tech got Meta, Lam and Tesla. Value got Lockheed's $292 billion backlog and P&G's 5% pop on 4% organic growth. Institutions did not stumble into that rotation. They had been quietly packing for weeks.

Europe lost everywhere, evenly. The Euro Stoxx 50 fell 1.97%, the FTSE 100 dropped 1.76%, the DAX lost 1.41%, and the CAC 40 slid 1.30%. The causes were stacked: riots across France, Italy, Spain and Albania, a French 10-year above 5% for the first time since 2002, and an IMF hint that the ECB may hike again before year-end. Hiking into riots. Bold.

Asia had it worse. The Hang Seng fell 5.05% after the PBOC held rates again and stimulus hopefuls went home empty. The Nikkei dropped 3.28% as AI hardware valuations deflated. Emerging markets fell 2.84% via EEM, with Korea's KOSPI eating semiconductor selling mid-week. The ASX 200 slipped just 0.13%. Commodities and geography, doing their job.

Currency Markets

The dollar index slipped 0.20% to 101.81, a modest move masking significant cross-rate divergence. The Japanese yen gained 0.62% against the dollar as the safe-haven bid intensified alongside Brent's surge past $114 and the Nasdaq's Thursday rout. The Australian dollar gained 0.69%, supported by commodity exposure and relative distance from the European fiscal crisis. The Swiss franc added 0.45%, consistent with its role as a geopolitical hedge during weeks featuring active conflict escalation and sovereign stress.

Sterling gained 0.28% against the dollar and hit a 17-month high against the euro, driven by two catalysts: UK September CPI printing at 3.5% YoY, pushing money markets to 95% probability of a 25 basis point Bank of England hike on November 5, and UK unemployment unexpectedly dropping to 4.7%. The euro was the week's laggard at -0.22% versus the dollar, weighed by French sovereign stress, continent-wide civil unrest, and Germany's refusal to support joint EU debt issuance. For global ETF investors, EUR weakness creates a quiet headwind for unhedged European equity positions in FEZ and EWG. GBP strength and BoE hawkishness make the pound the cleanest G10 carry trade heading into next week's UK Autumn Budget.

Commodities & Metals

WTI crude closed the week at $103.27, up 1.49%, but the weekly close understates the intraday drama. Brent surged past $114/bbl on Thursday after Houthi strikes targeted Dammam airport and the Shaybah oil field. Monday's White House Russian diesel waiver briefly pulled WTI below $100 before Persian Gulf supply risk reasserted itself within 24 hours. The message from energy markets is direct: regulatory sanctions relief cannot substitute for physical security along Gulf choke points. Natural gas collapsed 14.56%, the week's sharpest commodity move.

Gold fell 4.23% to $4,007.90/oz, its worst weekly performance in months, after touching a three-month low near $4,000 on Thursday. The selloff had two engines: first, $114 Brent reignited higher-for-longer Fed rate fears, mechanically pressuring non-yielding bullion; second, the Nasdaq's 2.6% single-session drop forced leveraged multi-asset funds to liquidate profitable gold positions to cover equity margin calls. Silver dropped 5.79%, amplifying gold's move. The $4,000/oz level is now the key structural test for sovereign central-bank accumulation bids. The 30-year Treasury yield fell 11 basis points to 5.32%, confirming that Thursday's Houthi-driven oil spike triggered genuine safe-haven demand in duration, not just a mechanical risk-off flush.

This Week’s Economic Events

Economic data this week reinforced the two-speed economy thesis. US homebuilder confidence fell to its lowest level since 2012 and September housing starts dropped 5.2% month-over-month, the clearest evidence yet that 5%+ Treasury yields are freezing rate-sensitive sectors while corporate balance sheets with strong cash generation shrug off the cost of capital. The Fed hold probability locked in at 93% via CME FedWatch, removing near-term rate catalyst risk from the US equity picture. In the UK, the data flow was unambiguously hawkish: unemployment fell to 4.7% against a consensus forecast of 5.0%, CPI accelerated to 3.5% with motor fuel up 29.8% year-over-year, and August GDP grew 0.1% against a forecast contraction. China's Q3 GDP at 4.5% YoY and September industrial output at 5.5% were technically solid but structurally misleading: the expansion is factory-led and export-driven, with domestic consumption and property still contracting. The PBOC's 17th consecutive LPR hold confirmed Beijing is not deploying monetary stimulus, leaving fiscal tools as the only lever.

Next Week: What to Watch

Two events define next week's macro landscape. The UK Autumn Budget on October 28 is the first major fiscal test of the Healey chancellorship, landing against a 3.5% CPI print, a 4.7% unemployment rate, and market odds firmly pricing a Bank of England hike on November 5. Any bank tax surcharge or spending overrun will move gilts and sterling immediately. The ECB Governing Council decision on October 29 matters just as much: France's 10-year yield has broken 5% for the first time since 2002, civil unrest is spreading across continental Europe, and the IMF has warned of at least one more ECB hike before year-end despite 0.9% Eurozone GDP growth. A hold with hawkish language will widen OAT-Bund spreads further. Beyond Europe, Sunday night's Brazilian presidential runoff results will set the opening tone for emerging-market FX and equities on Monday morning, with binary gap risk in BRL and EWZ depending on whether Bolsonaro or Lula takes the presidency.
