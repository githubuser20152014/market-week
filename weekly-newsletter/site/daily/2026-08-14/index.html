The Brief

The S&P cleared 7,800 for the first time ever, and gold still gained more. Cooling inflation gave stocks the green light, but gold crashed the party anyway, up 1.13% while the dollar slid to 99.66. That's not what a soft landing sounds like, that's what a market hedging its own rally sounds like. Oil held $81.70 despite a massive inventory build, because the Strait of Hormuz blockade doesn't do glut discounts. Futures are flat to marginally higher, tech leading, but the cross-asset setup says stay alert, not complacent.

The S&P 500 closed at 7,799, up 0.65%, punching through 7,800 for the first time in history. The Nasdaq ran 0.81% to 26,803, while the Dow limped to just +0.13%, proof this rally has a type and industrials aren't it. Gold surged to $4,413, up 1.13%, out-running every major equity index, because apparently nobody trusts the party they're at. The 10-year Treasury yield dropped 4.1 basis points to 4.641%, the dollar index slid to 99.66, and WTI crude held $81.70, firm considering last week's 17.4-million-barrel inventory build tried to sink it.

Twin inflation prints did the heavy lifting. July CPI came in soft at 3.4% annual, 0.1% monthly, and July PPI landed flat at 0.0%, both below consensus. Translation: the Fed holds at the September FOMC meeting, rates stay at 3.50-3.75%, and every high-multiple growth name from Nvidia to CoreWeave re-rated on cue like clockwork. But the 30-year touched its highest yield since 2001 on soft auction demand, so somebody on the long end is nervous about who's paying for all this. And gold rallying into a dollar selloff isn't just a disinflation trade anymore, it's a geopolitical floor: the Strait of Hormuz blockade is doing that work now.

What it means for you

The playbook splits cleanly. Tech and AI infrastructure (QQQ, SMH) stay the momentum trade as long as rate-hold confidence holds, and the Nasdaq beating the Dow by 67 basis points yesterday says it's holding. But gold's refusal to pull back while equities rallied is the tell: GLD isn't just an inflation hedge anymore, it's absorbing geopolitical premium from the Iran naval standoff. Energy (XLE) is the asymmetric call. Oil at $81.70 should be lower given a 17.4-million-barrel build, but the Hormuz blockade, 59 vessels diverted and tankers rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope, is holding a floor under crude anyway. Long-duration Treasuries (TLT) remain the trap: short-end yields fell, but 30-year auction weakness and fiscal deficit fears mean TLT buyers are swimming against the current.

Futures this morning: S&P +0.06%, Nasdaq +0.19%, Dow slightly negative, cautious and not much more. KOSPI was the standout APAC mover at +2.4%, while Hang Seng fell 1.1% on China demand doubts ahead of tomorrow's July activity dump from Beijing. Europe is flat in early session. The one calendar event today, New Home Sales at 10:00 AM ET, is a tier-one flag: housing is the most rate-sensitive sector in the economy, and a surprise here either validates or cracks the soft-landing story. Watch S&P 500 futures holding above 7,800 on any morning dip, because losing that reclaimed level intraday is the first sign this breakout is running on fumes.

The One Trade
GLD — Long
Gold outran every US equity index by 50+ basis points yesterday while the dollar fell and bonds rallied, a three-way divergence that, combined with an indefinite US naval blockade of Iranian ports, gives buyers two independent reasons to own it and no obvious catalyst to sell.
Confirms: GLD holds above $4,390 spot equivalent through the 10 AM New Home Sales print. Any equity softness that doesn't drag gold lower confirms the geopolitical bid is independent of the risk-on/risk-off toggle.
Kill switch: A de-escalation headline from the Strait of Hormuz, specifically a US-Iran diplomatic agreement to resume commercial transit, collapses the geopolitical floor and invalidates the trade immediately.

Positioning Notes
Signal Suggested Action
GLD: Maintain or add exposure. Gold outperformed every US equity index yesterday and futures are holding gains overnight. The signal is dual-source: disinflation keeps real rates in check, while the Hormuz naval standoff sustains a geopolitical bid. If S&P futures fade and gold holds $4,400, that confirms safe-haven demand is structural, not incidental.
QQQ/SMH: Hold tech and semiconductor exposure into the open. The Nasdaq's 0.81% lead over the Dow yesterday reflects real money rotating into AI infrastructure names off the CPI/PPI double print. The trade stays alive as long as 10-year yields stay below 4.70%, watch that level intraday, because a move back through it would pressure high-multiple names fast.
XLE: Consider a tactical long in energy. WTI at $81.70 is elevated relative to last week's 17.4 million barrel inventory build, the only explanation is Hormuz risk premium. The US naval blockade of Iranian ports is indefinite per Pentagon, and Cape of Good Hope rerouting is raising insurance costs structurally. If oil holds above $80 on any morning weakness, the geopolitical floor is real and XLE has room.
TLT: Avoid new long-duration bond exposure. The 10-year dipped 4 bps yesterday on disinflation, but the 30-year yield hit a 25-year high on soft auction demand. The fiscal deficit story is steepening the curve from the long end. TLT buyers are fighting both supply and skepticism about long-term US fiscal discipline, wrong side of the trade until refunding demand improves.
IYR/XLRE: Watch New Home Sales at 10 AM as a real-time check on rate-sensitive sectors. If the print surprises to the upside, real estate ETFs get a near-term bid as rate-hold confidence deepens. If it misses badly, it confirms housing is still impaired at 3.50-3.75% rates and these sectors underperform into the afternoon. Trade the data, not the pre-market drift.
