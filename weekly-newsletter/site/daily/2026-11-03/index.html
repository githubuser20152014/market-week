The Brief

Election day, and the market is already pricing a winner nobody has announced. Nasdaq dropped 1.07% to 25,164 and slid into correction, while the Dow rose 0.41% to 51,984. Tech is a fire, the Dow is a bystander eating popcorn.

Gold gained 1.83% to settle at $3,956.90. Crude fell to $96.46 after a two-session plunge of roughly 7%, and the 10-year slipped 3 bps to 4.91%. Oil drops, yields drop, gold rises. Someone is buying fear and someone else is selling inflation.

Apple printed $114.5B and guided gross margin down to 45-46% on memory costs. Palantir grew revenue 96% to $2.313B and still got sold after hours. Beating by a mile and getting punished anyway is this market's love language. Then US Central Command hit Iranian SAM sites near Qeshm Island, and oil responded by climbing 2.1% to $98.52 pre-market. Diplomacy in Doha is still on, so call it a bounded escalation with a price tag.

What it means for you

Money is leaving crowded mega-cap tech (QQQ, ARKK) and sneaking into equal-weight and value (RSP, DIA). Gold held above $3,950 while oil collapsed. That means the bid is not about inflation. It is about deficits, and no ballot fixes those. Energy (XLE, USO) is the live wire: WTI is +2.1% to $98.52, and one Doha headline moves it 3-5% either way. TLT has a ceiling at 4.91%, and a priced-in BoE hike is not helping.

Futures are in a holding pattern. S&P futures sit at 7,561 (-0.18%), Nasdaq futures are +0.20%, and Dow futures are -0.84% at 51,694. KOSPI dropped 3.59% overnight, the loudest Asian echo of the Nasdaq correction. Hang Seng rose 0.57% and Nifty rose 1.58%, so not everyone is panicking. The swing factor is tonight's midterm tallies from 7-8 PM ET. A Democratic sweep could rattle equities on tax worries. A divided Congress has historically been the equity-friendly outcome, and it could spark a relief rally in SPY and QQQ on Wednesday. Watch $3,900 gold futures as the floor and $99 WTI as the trigger for the next energy scare.

The One Trade
GLD — Long
Gold settled at $3,956.90 while crude fell 7% in two sessions. A bid that ignores falling oil is not an inflation trade, it is a fiscal-deficit trade, and the overnight dip to $3,914 in futures is the entry, not the warning.
Confirms: GLD holds the equivalent of $3,900/oz through the first 90 minutes of trading and recovers toward $3,940-$3,950 by 11 AM ET. That is institutional buyers defending the floor.
Kill switch: A signed Doha communique on Hormuz transit, or a midterm result that implies aggressive fiscal tightening, pushes gold futures below $3,880/oz. That breaks the floor and kills the thesis.
