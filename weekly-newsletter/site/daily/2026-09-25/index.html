The Brief

The S&P added 0.14% and the bond market declined to clap. Gold gained 0.60% while the 10-year sat at 5.17%, which is what buyers do when they say they're relaxed but keep mapping the exits. Mega-caps got the trophy, the Russell 2000 got a 0.11% haircut, and this rally has a guest list.

Yesterday's close, in order of how much you should trust it. The S&P 500 settled at 7,714.74 (+0.14%), the Dow at 51,435.61 (+0.17%), and the Nasdaq at 26,969.88 (+0.11%). Fractional gains. Nobody opened champagne. The Russell 2000 slipped 0.11%, because small caps are the canaries of domestic credit, and right now they are canaries with a cough. Gold hit $4,323.80 (+0.60%). The 10-year yield edged up 0.7 bps to 5.169%, so much for the relief rally in bonds. WTI dropped 1.86% to $92.85 on Hormuz diplomacy, and the USD Index fell 0.32% to 100.96.

Three catalysts did the talking. First, the Trump-Xi summit extended the Busan Agreement through January 10, 2027. Semiconductors and critical minerals get runway, and trans-Pacific supply chains get a breather heading into Q4. Second, Iran offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within 7 days and restart nuclear talks. Crude noticed. The risk premium bled out and WTI slid from $94.61 to $92.85. Third, the bond market watched all of it and did nothing. A 10-year that holds 5.17% through two pieces of good news is telling you exactly what it thinks of the good news.

What it means for you

Two peace headlines, one stubborn yield. The lanes are clear. The trade truce helps semiconductor and China-exposed plays (SOXX, KWEB, XLK). The Iran headlines lean on energy ETFs (XLE, USO). Gold refusing to sell off into a diplomatic tailwind is the signal that matters: somebody is hedging something they do not trust yet, likely the 5%+ yield regime and Warsh's brewing Fed restructuring. TLT stays a live risk because yields have not broken lower with conviction. The Russell divergence warns you off rate-sensitive small caps (IWM) until yields stop flirting with new highs.

Futures this morning have the S&P adding another 0.20% to 7,782.25 and Nasdaq futures up 0.33% to 30,867.75. The mega-cap quality trade lives to see another session. USTR Greer's Monday trade detail announcement is the swing factor through the session. New Home Sales at 10:00 AM ET is the secondary yield check. The level to hold is S&P 7,700, flagged by institutional desks as the floor through the weekly close.

Positioning Notes
Signal Suggested Action
Gold (GLD): Hold or add on any morning dip toward $4,290. Gold gaining 0.60% on a day equities rose and a diplomatic risk-off catalyst (Hormuz) unwound is an anomaly that signals structural demand from institutional hedgers, not speculative momentum. If yields keep climbing, gold could catch a second bid.
Semiconductors and China tech (SOXX, KWEB): The Busan Agreement extension through January 2027 gives semiconductor supply chains a defined tariff-free window into the holiday cycle. Buy SOXX on any early-session dip below yesterday's close. If USTR Greer's Monday detail announcement introduces new carve-outs, these add further. If talks stall, trim quickly.
Energy (XLE, USO): The Iran Hormuz offer is not a done deal but the risk premium is unwinding fast. WTI at $92.85 is already down from $94.61 the session before. Short-term pressure favors staying underweight XLE until the OPEC+ JMMC review next week provides a floor. If Iran talks collapse over the weekend, re-enter on the pop.
Small caps (IWM): Stay sidelined. The Russell 2000 was the only major index to fall yesterday, and with the 10-year at 5.17% and no confirmed reversal, debt-heavy small caps have no relief catalyst. The Warsh Fed restructuring headlines add governance uncertainty that further delays any dovish pivot.
Long-duration Treasuries (TLT): Do not chase. The 10-year edged up 0.7 bps to 5.17%, so there is no reversal to buy yet. TLT becomes a trade when yields break lower with conviction, not when a headline says they might.
