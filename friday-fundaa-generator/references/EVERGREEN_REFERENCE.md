# Evergreen Mode Reference

Used when the weekly topic is NOT a market story: a philosopher, an economic idea, a book, a concept. First piece in this mode: "What Camus Taught Us About Laundry" (2026-10-30).

## The Rules

1. **Any topic, one per week.** The user picks it. Do not default to a market angle.
2. **Lay audience first.** Assume no background. Define every term in plain English the first time it appears. If a term is fancy, say so ("Fancy word, simple feeling"). No academic jargon, no quoting scholars for authority.
3. **Open on a relatable everyday moment**, not the concept name and not a lecture. (Camus piece opens on folding laundry on a Tuesday.)
4. **Title format:** a playful "What [Person/Idea] Taught Us About [Everyday Thing]" style, or similar curiosity gap. WhatsApp title line is `*Friday Fundaa: <Title>*`. Substack title drops the "Friday Fundaa:" prefix.
5. **No emojis** (global rule, also applies to Markets mode). Use punctuation for emphasis and structure: colons to lead into a section, exclamation marks for light energy, plain numbered lists (`1.` `2.` `3.`), ellipses for a beat. The cheeky voice comes from word choice, not icons.
6. **Integrate into life by showing, not instructing.** Land the idea on an ordinary moment (laundry, commute, inbox, coffee). No advice, no "you should", no question at the end.
7. **Keep it light.** Drop dark or heavy details (e.g. the cause of a thinker's death). Keep fun, human ones (e.g. Camus the goalkeeper).
8. **Disclaimer:** `For educational purposes only. Not life advice.` Never "Not financial advice" or "Peak 2026" on a non-market piece, and none of the market tropes (surcharge, geopolitical premium, "Representative Math").
9. **Do not reuse the surcharge template.** Recent market pieces all ran the same shape. Evergreen pieces must not.
10. **No em dashes.**

## Structure (WhatsApp and Substack share the same wording)

1. Relatable hook (everyday moment, then the question it raises).
2. "His/Her/Its idea, in plain English" (the core concept, 2-3 short paragraphs, one numbered list max).
3. The story or example that makes it stick (e.g. Sisyphus).
4. "Here's the 'huh' part" (the twist, then the everyday tie-back).
5. One fun fact.
6. "Worth knowing:" one-line close.
7. WhatsApp: "Full breakdown on Substack:" plus link, then disclaimer. Substack: disclaimer only, no link line.

## Platform Notes

- **WhatsApp:** bold title line, italic tagline (`_A short weekly moment of "huh, didn't know that" from the world of ideas._`), short paragraphs, blank line between every beat.
- **Substack:** same wording as WhatsApp. Add up to three short subheads (e.g. "His idea, in plain English", "The guy with the boulder", "Here's the 'huh' part"). No bullet-heavy formatting.
- **Drafts live in** `ContentRepo/drafts/friday-fundaa-<topic>.md` (Substack) and `friday-fundaa-<topic>-whatsapp.md`.
