Use the March 21, 2026 Global Investor Edition as the reference style for all future Global editions.

**Key characteristics:**
- **Plain English throughout** — no jargon without an immediate plain-English explanation
- **Analogies to explain economic concepts** — e.g. "fire sale at a shop", "canary in a coal mine", "drought pushing up food prices", "car breaking down AND it starting to rain"
- **Interpret every data point for long-term investors** — don't just report numbers; explain what they mean for a diversified ETF holder
- **Bold key ideas** using `**bold**` — guide the reader's eye to the most important takeaways; do this throughout, not just in the positioning section
- **Name specific ETF tickers** (e.g. EFA, EWJ, XLU, GLD, SHV) when giving actionable positioning — not just sector descriptions
- **Section headings without page numbers** — "## What Happened & Why" and "## Data Appendix", never "## Page 1 — ..." or "## Page 2 — ..."
- **Big theme title should show both risk AND opportunity** — e.g. "Stagflation Warning Flashes — But Volatility Is Creating the Entry Points Patient Investors Wait For"
- **Long-term investor framing at the end of the main narrative** — 2 paragraphs: first on rebalancing/repositioning, second on real-asset hedging. Both concrete with tickers.

**Why:** User explicitly approved this approach after the March 21 edition and asked to replicate it going forward.

**How to apply:** When drafting the Global Investor Edition narrative sections (big_theme_body, equity_narrative, fx_narrative, commodities_narrative), apply all of the above from the start — do not wait for the user to request edits.
