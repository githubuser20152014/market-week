The Brief

CPI lands at 8:30 AM and the whole tape is holding its breath. Futures are flat, the 10-year sits at 5.217%, and nobody wants to be the first to blink.

Equities closed red in every direction. S&P 500 at 7,805, Dow down 0.35%, Nasdaq down 0.15%, Russell 2000 down 0.40%. Small caps took the worst of it, because small caps are the canaries of domestic credit, and right now they are canaries with a cough.

WTI jumped 1.58% to $95.63. The IEA warned the market is tight and Saudi officials confirmed attacks on Aramco facilities. Oil heard "supply gone" and said thank you. Meanwhile gold slipped 0.43% to $4,159 with a war on. The safe haven read the room and chose rates instead.

The 10-year dipped 2.3 bps to 5.217%. That counts as relief at multi-decade highs. The dollar did nothing at 102.26, which at this point is a personality.

Earnings split the tape. Goldman posted $17.01 EPS on $6.44 billion in equities revenue. Bank of America ate a $1.2 billion Treasury fine. One bank printed money, the other paid a cover charge. Today's September CPI is the most consequential print of the week, with consensus at 3.6% headline (up from 3.4%) and core at 0.2% month over month.

What it means for you

The energy trade has a hard catalyst, which is rare and unfair to everyone who waited. Supply disruption, an IEA warning and thin OECD inventories all point the same way. (XLE, XOP) give direct upstream exposure. (IEZ) gets the oilfield services bill. Gold refusing to rally into geopolitical risk is the anomaly. If CPI runs hot and yields head toward 5.35%, (GLD) is a hold, not a buy. Cash-like bills (BIL, SGOV) pay near 5% with no duration risk, which is the most boring good idea on the board. (TLT) is dead money until the Fed says something dovish. Today will not be that day.

S&P futures are up 0.14% to 7,866. Dow futures are up 0.29%. Nasdaq futures are down 0.20%. Tech is quietly sweating before the print. Europe is up 0.5% to 0.6% on the DAX and CAC, with energy tailwinds and a Brussels summit doing the lifting. CPI is the swing factor. A headline at 3.7% or core above 0.3% sends the 10-year toward 5.35% and growth names take the beating. A 3.4% or lower is the green light for a relief rally in (QQQ) and (TLT). Watch supercore services. The Fed does, even if the headline doesn't.

Signal Suggested Action
Hold (XLE) and add to (XOP) on any morning pullback: the Aramco facility attack confirmation and IEA supply warnings give energy a multi-day bid with a hard fundamental catalyst. If CPI prints hot, energy becomes the natural inflation hedge rotation and these names outperform regardless of the broader tape direction.
Stay short-duration in fixed income via (BIL) or (SGOV): with the 10-year at 5.217% and CPI expected to tick higher, there is no risk-adjusted reason to extend duration ahead of the print. If CPI surprises to the upside, (TLT) will get hit. If it surprises to the downside, you can rotate into (TLT) after the 8:30 number clears.
Avoid (QQQ) exposure into the open: Nasdaq futures are the only major index in the red pre-market, and large-cap tech carries the most duration sensitivity in equities. A hot CPI print compresses multiples on high-growth names fastest. Wait for the data before adding.
In financials, favor (KCE) or individual capital markets names over broad (XLF): Goldman's blowout versus BofA's fine illustrates that the sector is not monolithic. Asset-light trading and advisory franchises are outperforming deposit-heavy commercial lenders in a high-rate environment. (XLF) blends both and dilutes the thesis.
Watch (GLD) for a directional tell post-CPI: gold fading 0.43% despite an Aramco attack and ongoing Middle East conflict is a warning sign that real rate pressure is dominating the safe-haven narrative. If CPI is hot and yields spike, gold likely sells off further despite geopolitics. A cold CPI print that pulls yields back is the only near-term bullish catalyst for (GLD).
