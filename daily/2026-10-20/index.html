The Brief

The Nasdaq jumped 1.11% to 26,430 on Monday. Futures are down 0.67% this morning. That rally lasted one session. The 10-year sat at 5.136% the whole time, like a landlord watching a party.

The S&P added 0.41% to 7,690. The Dow lost 0.07% to 51,966. Gold slipped to $4,141 while the dollar index sagged to 101.88. Everyone sold something and nobody bought safety. Pick a narrative, markets.

Crude is the plot twist. WTI closed at $100.73 after Washington waived its Russian diesel sanctions for 60 days. Then Persian Gulf tanker strikes hit overnight and the waiver aged like milk. Brent is back above $110. A sanctions waiver is a sandbag in front of a flood. The Strait of Hormuz does not read paperwork.

Asia took the news personally. The KOSPI fell 3.7% after a South Korean AI data-center IPO collapsed. The Nikkei and Hang Seng each lost 1.3%. CoreWeave's CDS widened earlier this week, so the AI buildout is now getting an audit instead of applause.

What it means for you

Energy is the only sector with a straight face today. WTI above $100 is a gift to upstream producers (XLE, XOP). It is also a tax on every trucker and shopper. That is stagflation with a ticker symbol.

Bonds never bought the equity rally. At 5.136%, TLT is a value trap with a duration problem. CME FedWatch gives a pivot a 7% chance in October. Hope is not a catalyst.

Gold at $4,141 is sitting above the $4,120 floor. That is digestion, not a breakdown. GLD stays a core hold.

Semis are the canary, and the canary has a cough. If you hold SOXX or any chip-heavy fund, the KOSPI's 3.7% drop is your problem too.

Futures say the pain continues. S&P futures are down 0.25% at 7,715. Nasdaq futures are down 0.67% at 29,523. Dow futures are flat at 52,185, courtesy of a GM Q3 beat. Europe is red too: Euro Stoxx 50 is off 0.99%. No major US data today. The swing factors are oil, tech's mood, and the Rubio-Jaishankar energy talks in New Delhi.
