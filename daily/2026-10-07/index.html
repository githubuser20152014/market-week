The Brief

Wall Street printed records. Asia and Europe read the memo and sold anyway. KOSPI lost 1.98%. The Nikkei gave back 0.92%. Euro Stoxx 50 is down 1.84% before most of us have had coffee. Wall Street is the only party still dancing, and the music is coming from a different time zone.

US equities closed at records Tuesday. The S&P 500 finished at 7,818.93 (+0.58%), the Dow at 51,521.28 (+0.49%), the Nasdaq at 27,599.79 (+0.45%). The Russell 2000 slid 0.59% to 2,830.30. Small-caps are the canaries of domestic credit, and right now they are canaries with a cough. The 10-year eased about 4 basis points to 5.27%. Still five and a quarter percent. Relax. Meanwhile gold fell 1.09% to $4,141.60 with Iran talks stalled and the Red Sea still on fire, because the USD Index gained 0.53% to 102.37 and the dollar does not do fear. WTI added 0.83% to $90.18.

The rally has two pillars. Chips and AI hardware led the buying. And Friday's +29,000 payrolls print, against a 85,000-90,000 consensus, pushed odds of a Fed hold at the October 27-28 FOMC to 78%. The economy missed by roughly 60,000 jobs and stocks hit a record. Bad news is good news until it isn't. The bond market is not convinced. The 10-year sits at 5.27%, down from 5.31-5.34% earlier this week, and ISM Services Prices printed 74.0 on Monday, the highest since July 2022. The Fed can pause. It cannot cut with that number on the table. The September FOMC Minutes land today, and they are the first real test.

What it means for you

The playbook splits in two. Large-cap tech (QQQ, SMH) is working, and it is also crowded. One hawkish line in the Minutes and the crowd finds the exit at the same time. The Russell lagging on a record day is a red flag for IWM. Small-caps are the most rate-sensitive corner of the market and they did not show up. Energy stays in the conversation: WTI above $90 with Red Sea disruptions and Gulf storm risk keeps XLE and XOP bid, though the Chevron CEO's warning against a diesel export ban adds a political wildcard. And gold is failing to hold its safe-haven premium with Iran stalled and strike risk live. The dollar is outbidding the fear. GLD longs need a new thesis.

S&P futures sit at 7,837, about 18 points above yesterday's close, pointing at a quiet open. They are pointing straight into a wall of red from Asia and Europe. The FOMC Minutes at 2:00 PM ET are the swing factor. Any language that worries more about sticky services inflation than the labor slowdown, or any hint of faster balance-sheet runoff, reprices the October 27-28 pause odds holding this tape together. The level to watch is 7,773, Monday's S&P close. Lose it on a hawkish read and consolidation becomes distribution.
