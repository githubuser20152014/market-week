The Brief

Happy Wednesday: the Strait of Hormuz is a war zone, Fed Chair Warsh is sharpening his hiking pen, and gold just shrugged off a stronger dollar to sit near record highs anyway. That is not a coincidence, that is the market pricing stagflation and refusing to pretend otherwise. APAC took the overnight beating, KOSPI down nearly 4% and the Nikkei off 2.85%, because export economies do not enjoy paying more for oil that might not show up.

The S&P 500 fell 0.71% to 7,631, its third straight losing session, because apparently three in a row is the new normal. The Russell 2000 led losses at -1.23%, small caps always first in line when the 10-year Treasury yield rises to 4.796%, the highest since late 2023 and climbing like it has somewhere to be. Gold added 0.20% to $4,356.50 anyway, and when the dollar firms and yields spike and gold still will not sell off, that is not fear, that is a vote of no confidence in the people setting rates.

Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole routine pushed September hike odds above 65%, and then the Strait of Hormuz decided to make his job easier: U.S. strikes on IRGC coastal assets drew Iranian mines into two tankers, transit through the strait is down 40%, and roughly 20% of the planet's oil now runs through a chokepoint nobody controls. Higher oil means higher inflation means Warsh gets to hike anyway and call it prudence. Two birds, one geopolitical crisis.

What it means for you

This is not a dip to buy, it is a regime to trade around, so retire the buy-the-dip reflex for a session. Gold refusing to sell off despite a stronger dollar and rising real yields is the loudest signal on the tape: GLD and SGOL are behaving like insurance, not a commodity. Energy stays structurally bid while a war-risk premium gets priced into every barrel, XLE, XOP, and OIH deserve exposure with stops wide enough to survive a headline. Small caps and anything long-duration are the first casualties of a 4.796% 10-year, so IWM can sit this one out short or underweight. TLT remains a value trap until Warsh actually pivots, and he shows zero interest in doing that. Defensives with pricing power, utilities on rate pass-throughs, midstream energy infrastructure, offer the clearest shelter (XLU selectively, EPD).

S&P futures are off 0.23% at 7,625 and Nasdaq futures are down 0.54%, so tech gets to lead the retreat again. KOSPI's near-4% overnight collapse is Asia pricing a genuine escalation scenario, not a one-day reaction. Today's swing factor is entirely Hormuz headline flow: a naval escort deal spikes crude lower and gives equities a bounce, another tanker or mine incident sends WTI back toward $95 and drags sentiment with it. The Bank of Canada also has a rate decision today, tariff-adjacent noise unless it moves USD/CAD. Watch 4.81% on the 10-year: break it cleanly and the equity de-rating accelerates.
