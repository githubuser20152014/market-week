The Brief

Stocks closed near records into the biggest jobs report of the month. The 10-year sits at 5.24% and ISM Prices Paid just printed 77.9. Nobody has noticed, or nobody cares.

The S&P 500 closed at 7,666.45 (+0.19%). The Nasdaq added 0.04% to 26,871.60. The Dow added 0.04% to 50,926.56. That is the sound of a market holding its breath while pretending to nap. Small caps did the heavy lifting: the Russell 2000 rose 0.35%, because apparently the canaries feel fine today.

The 10-year eased 5.6 basis points to 5.237% after touching 5.34% intraday. Bonds blinked. WTI crashed 3.7% to $89.47 on reports that the EU and the IEA are drafting a 100-million-barrel emergency reserve release. Gold rose 0.22% to $4,211.70 and the dollar index slipped to 102.07. The economy is running hot, inflation is not cooling, and oil just got a government-sized discount. Pick a narrative.

Jobless claims came in at 197,000, sub-200k for the third straight week. ISM Manufacturing hit 54.5. Nike fell 10% on weak revenue guidance and layoffs, because the consumer is fine right up until someone checks the guidance. The yield dip is a crude hangover, not rate-cut hope. Pressure valve, not regime change.

What it means for you

Oil fell 3.7% and energy is now pricing demand doom it did not believe 48 hours ago. XLE has free cash flow yields above 11%, which is a floor with a pulse. If the release proves as temporary as past SPR drops, XLE at current levels is the asymmetric side of this fork.

ISM Prices Paid at 77.9 is a loaded gun pointed at long bonds. TLT is wearing a target with $739 billion of quarterly Treasury supply still arriving. SGOV and BIL pay 4.9-5.1% to do nothing, which is the most honest trade on the board. Gold above $4,200 while stocks coast is the quiet tell: someone is buying insurance and paying cash.

Futures are green. S&P is up 0.43% to 7,757, Nasdaq up 0.63%. The Hang Seng fell 2.6% on K-shaped Golden Week spending data, so China's recovery is for some people. The DAX rose 0.86% on Eurozone manufacturing. Everything hangs on the 8:30 AM ET NFP: consensus is 165,000 payrolls and 4.1% unemployment.

Above 200,000 and the 10-year heads back to 5.34%, multiples compress, and the futures bid evaporates. Below 130,000 and rate-cut pricing returns with a bond rally. Watch S&P 7,700 up and 7,620 down. A hot print hits XLY and XLE first, because Nike and crude already loaded the gun.

The One Trade
GLD — Long
Gold is holding above $4,211 while futures run 0.5% higher, the Swiss franc surged 1.5%, and 5.24% yields should be crushing it. It is not selling. Someone is defending this floor against whatever NFP delivers.
Confirms: GLD holds above $4,190 by 10:00 AM ET regardless of NFP direction. If a hot print pushes yields toward 5.34% and gold still holds, the safe-haven bid is structural, not momentum.
Kill switch: GLD breaks below $4,170 on a hot NFP print above 200,000 combined with a dollar index spike above 103. That combination means the inflation-driven yield surge is overwhelming the safe-haven bid and the thesis is wrong today.
